Here's the unit map. It runs 15 days — three weeks of 50-minute periods — and ends with a Thesis-Driven Essay on whether George was right to kill Lennie. The class starter arc seeds the big themes before students ever open the book, so by essay day they have their own thinking to mine.

The recurring class starters are:
- Day 1 — "I am the captain of my destiny. Agree or disagree and explain your rationale."
- Day 3 — "We need other people more than we like to admit."
- Day 6 — "Loneliness and being alone are different things."
- Day 8 — "A society should be judged by how it treats its weakest members."
- Day 11 — "Laws and morality are not the same thing. When should you choose one over the other?"
- Day 13 — "It's worse to do nothing than to do the wrong thing."

A few things worth checking before you build any day:

- Does this arc feel right for your pacing? Some classes need a full day on Chapter 3 alone; you can split Day 4 and compress the writing-skills days if so.
- The essay prompt — "Was George right to kill Lennie?" — is deliberately open: mercy, betrayal, and necessity are all defensible, and that is what makes it worth grading. If you want to narrow or widen it, say so and I'll adjust.
- Day 15 ends with a reflection that loops back to the Day 1 class starter on destiny. That closing move is worth keeping — students who can say "my thinking changed because..." are doing real intellectual work — but it is easy to cut if the period gets tight.
