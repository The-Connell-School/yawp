# Kevin's rules R14 to R25, and how each is tested

*Nov 23. These come from Kevin's notes on the Antigone lesson and unit, the timelines, the maps and the visuals work. They extend R1 to R13 (see `2026-11-22-lesson-quality-review-and-plan.md`). Each rule says what a lesson must do, the note it came from, and how a test checks it. **Auto** means a script checks it from the planner's reply. **Review** means a reviewer judges it from the slides and screenshots.*

## How the reply reads

| Rule | What it means | From Kevin's note | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R14 Lesson first, caveats last** | The reply opens on the lesson, or on the unit map. Notes for the teacher (quotations not checked, things to check) come at the end, in "Check before class". | "This first part shouldn't be on top... quotations feels like a caveat that should be somewhere else" | **Auto:** the first paragraph is not a caveat; the quotation line, if any, is in the last quarter of the reply. |
| **R15 No narration** | The planner never narrates its own process: what it has, what it searched, what it will write itself, what it is about to do. | "Good — I have everything I need. I'll write all the prompts myself..." | **Auto:** none of the narration patterns appear in the reply's prose. |
| **R16 No wall of text** | A unit reply has at most two sentences before the map. Any prose is short, with its questions as a short list after the map. | "This long preamble is poorly formatted"; "it's just a wall of text" | **Auto:** at most two sentences before a unit map. **Review:** the reply is easy to scan. |
| **R17 Notes that are real** | Nothing under "Check before class" says it is not an error. Every note is something to act on. | "This part doesn't feel very well done" ("this is not a real error, so ignore") | **Auto:** no "not an error / ignore" in the notes. **Review:** each note is real. |

## How a unit looks

| Rule | What it means | From Kevin's note | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R18 A unit at a glance** | Each day on the unit map shows its title, its minutes, its main activities as icons (book, pencil, gavel, projector...), and its reading on one line. The long text is one click away. | "Less words... a picture or emoji that highlights the major activities in each lesson" | **Review:** screenshot of the unit card; every day has its activity icons; the full text is under Details. |
| **R19 Not slow** | A lesson arrives in under about 2½ minutes, a unit map in under about 2. The wait is measured, not guessed. | "It's a little slow... especially when drafting units" | **Auto:** seconds per request, from the timed runs. |

## What the slides show

| Rule | What it means | From Kevin's note | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R20 The right places on the map** | A map pins the places the text is set in, including ancient and literary places (Thebes, Troy, Elsinore), never an American town of the same name. Nothing the lesson names as its setting is "not on the map". | "Why isn't Thebes on the map here?" | **Auto:** every place on a map slide was found, and none is in the wrong country. **Review:** the place the text is set in is pinned. |
| **R21 Timelines with pictures** | A timeline's events carry pictures. They are visible on the whole line, not only one at a time at the end. Famous historical events get pictures of the event itself. | "These timelines appear a bit lifeless without cool pictures on them... you do it here but only for one and only at the end"; "pictures of famous historical events too" | **Auto:** share of timeline events with a picture. **Review:** the whole timeline shows its pictures. |
| **R22 Show the world** | When a text's world is far from students (ancient Greece, a war, a period), one slide shows it in several pictures side by side: the ruins, what it looked like, the people, an object. | "You could [have] multiple pictures on one slide that show kids what Greece is like... the ruins, what it would have looked like" | **Auto:** a context day or first lesson on a distant world has a world slide with 2 to 4 pictures. |
| **R23 Memorable, not only clear** | A content slide carries a picture of the thing itself, a diagram of how the text is built, or a marked passage. A deck where most content slides are words only is clear but not memorable. Decoration does not count. | "It's clear... but it's not memorable"; "devoid of pictures or flavor" | **Auto:** share of content slides with a picture, diagram or marked passage. **Review:** the deck would be remembered; nothing is decoration. |

## The words of the text

| Rule | What it means | From Kevin's note | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R24 Quote only a real text** | A work in copyright, including a modern translation of an old work (Fitts and Fitzgerald's *Antigone*, Fagles' Homer), is never quoted from memory. Yawp! prints a public-domain translation and says which, or the teacher's own pages. Otherwise it leaves a labeled slot and describes the moment with its page. | Antigone printed in Fitts and Fitzgerald; "we gotta be clear to teachers about when they need to give us a copy of the text" | **Auto:** the quotation check's result (found, corrected, not found) and the slots. **Review:** no long passage of a copyrighted text from memory. |
| **R25 Tell the teacher what was checked** | Every lesson that quotes says, in one line at the end, what its quotations were checked against, or that they were not, and how to fix it. | "We gotta be clear to teachers about when they need to give us a copy of the text" | **Auto:** the line is present on lessons that quote and absent from unit maps. |

## The test

Eight requests on the PR #997 preview, chosen to exercise these rules:
- three lessons on distant worlds (*Antigone*, *The Odyssey*, *Macbeth*);
- a poem ("Dulce et Decorum Est");
- a copyrighted novel (*Their Eyes Were Watching God*);
- two context days with timelines (*The Great Gatsby*, King's letter);
- one unit map (*Of Mice and Men*).

Each is scored pass, partial or fail on every rule that applies to it, with the evidence.
