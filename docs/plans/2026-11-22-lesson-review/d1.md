# d1: 1984 unit plan (12th grade, four weeks, language and power)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Build a unit plan for 1984 for 12th grade: four weeks, 50-minute periods, with a focus on language and power, ending in a research-backed essay."

**What came back:** One turn took 111 s. It produced a unit map titled "Language, Power, and Control in 1984": 20 days of 50 minutes each, 1,000 minutes in all. Each day has a title, objective, reading, student activity, "builds to" and check, plus a "Build this day" button. Days 1 to 10 cover the novel. Day 11 covers secondary sources. Days 12 to 18 cover pre-writing, thesis, evidence, drafting, revision and submission, using Yawp! Quick Writing Lessons and Lounge decks. Day 19 is a gallery share and Day 20 is "1984 and now". Above the map the planner lists a "class starter arc" of six prompts by week and day. Below it are three things to check (the calendar fit, an open or fixed essay prompt, the Lounge decks) and the closing question "Want to refine or edit any part of this?". The only follow-up chip is "Look at my classes and tell me what they need work on".

There is no deck and no handout, which is expected for a unit plan. The stack page (`/packet`) for this conversation is an empty "Untitled lesson" showing "0 of 50 minutes planned", 0% writing and "Nothing in this lesson yet". There was no "Add everything" button. The printable plan PDF is one page: the raw request is its title, the overview reads "A 50-minute lesson ... 0 parts", and Goals and Standards are placeholders. `handouts.pdf` returned 409 and `slides.pdf` returned 404.

## 2. Scores (1 to 4)

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** Day 1 opens on the theme in students' own lives ("where do you feel watched or managed by language?") and briefly finds out what they know about "totalitarianism, propaganda, or surveillance". That is a textbook R12 (1) and (2). Day 12 has students reread their class-starter entries to find an essay thread, and Day 20 returns to "the students' own world". Within days the direct teaching (step 3) is mostly missing: only Day 3 has a mini-lecture (the Panopticon, 5 min).
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** The reading schedule leaves holes in the novel. Part 1 Ch. 4–5 (Winston rewriting the past, and Syme on Newspeak, the core language-and-power passage), Part 1 Ch. 8, Part 2 Ch. 4–8 and Ch. 10 are never assigned. All of Part 3 is one night's homework (Day 9). Some passages are misplaced or misattributed (section 5). Page numbers are given without naming an edition.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 2.** There is no context day (Orwell, the Spanish Civil War, Stalinism, wartime BBC propaganda, publication in 1949). There is no Newspeak vocabulary (Ingsoc, thoughtcrime, unperson, Minitrue, prole, telescreen) before Day 4. "Politics and the English Language" (1946), the natural paired essay for a language-and-power unit, is never mentioned. The "research-backed" essay gets one day with two unnamed "teacher-selected" critical excerpts (Day 11) and no teaching on finding, judging or citing sources beyond 10 minutes of MLA on Day 18.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding: null.** The unit produces no deck. No map, timeline or faces are suggested even for Day 1 (R1 to R4 would apply if a context day existed).
- **E. Writing: 3.** The arc leads to a real essay with thesis, evidence and analysis, hooks, conclusions and transitions mini-lessons, a peer conference, and a check that at least two secondary sources are used (Day 17). Short Responses on Days 3, 7 and 9 are good questions. However, class starters appear on only 5 of the 11 text days. The class-starter arc in the prose does not match the days (see Problem 1), and two of the six listed prompts ("History is just whichever version of the past won", "The crowd believes the crowd") appear on no day.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** Every day has a check, and most match the objective (Day 4: "define Newspeak in your own words and give one real-world example"; Day 11: summarize a critic's claim, then take a stand). Some are weak. Day 2's objective is "identify Orwell's first moves with setting and diction", but its check is "one word or phrase that surprised you". Day 20 has "No formal check".
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** Day 2 reads Part 1 Ch. 1 aloud in class after a starter. The chapter is roughly 20+ pages and will not fit, and "pp. 1–10" does not cover it. Day 4 reads the whole Newspeak appendix in class alongside a starter, discussion and pair work. Day 9 assigns all six chapters of Part 3 for one night. Revision gets one day, while two days follow submission. The stack and the printable plan are empty for a unit conversation, so nothing is ready to print until each day is built separately.
- **H. Teacher usability: 2.** The day cards are clear, and the closing offers real choices (compress the calendar; an open or fixed prompt). But the prose summary contradicts the map, and deck references are inconsistent. The prose says "Days 13–17 use Yawp!'s Lounge decks for pre-writing", but the Pre-Writing deck is on Day 12. The 24-slide Thesis Statement deck (slides 12–35) is paired with a 12-minute mini-lesson. A teacher would have to audit every day against the summary.
- **I. Access: 1.** There is no mention of struggling readers or English learners anywhere: no audiobook, reading guide, chunked passages, vocabulary support, sentence frames for the thesis, or model paragraph.
- **Overall: 2.** A strong teacher would take the essay-writing back half and the Day 1 opener, but would have to rebuild the reading schedule, add context and Syme/Newspeak teaching, supply the secondary sources, and fix the misattributions before using this.

## 3. Strengths

1. **The writing pipeline is real and well sequenced.** Days 12–18 move from rereading one's own starter entries, to a topic, a thesis (with a "Is it arguable? Is it specific?" partner check), evidence and analysis, drafting, a peer conference and revision. Day 17's check ("highlight every sentence that comes from a secondary source — do you have at least two?") is exactly the kind of concrete self-check that works.
2. **Day 1 follows Kevin's arc.** It opens on students' experience of being watched or managed by language and briefly surfaces prior knowledge, and Day 12 and Day 20 close the loop back to it.
3. **Good discussion questions.** "Is Winston already a victim of doublethink before he rebels?" (Day 5), "why does the Party allow this book to exist?" (Day 8), and "Is Winston's breaking a failure of will or a proof of the Party's thesis?" (Day 9) are worthy of a 12th-grade seminar.

## 4. Problems (most serious first)

1. **The unit summary contradicts the unit map.** In the prose, Week 1 Day 2 / Day 4 match the map. But Week 2 Day 2 (Day 7) is listed as "History is just whichever version of the past won", and Day 7 has no starter. Week 2 Day 4 (Day 9) is listed as "The crowd believes the crowd", while Day 9 actually uses "We believe what we want to believe". Week 3 Day 1 (Day 11) is listed as "We believe what we want...", while Day 11 uses "Privacy is already gone". Week 4 Day 1 is listed as the rereading day, but the map does that on Day 12 (Week 3 Day 2). *Why it matters:* the teacher cannot trust either the summary or the map, and assigns the wrong starter on the wrong day. *Principle:* Brian's "names and quotations exactly right"; teacher usability.
2. **Key chapters of the novel are skipped, including the central language-and-power scene.** Part 1 Ch. 4–5 are never read: Winston falsifying the Times, and Syme's "Don't you see that the whole aim of Newspeak is to narrow the range of thought?" Neither are Part 1 Ch. 8, Part 2 Ch. 4–8 (O'Brien's flat, the induction) and Ch. 9's Hate Week speech where the enemy switches mid-sentence, or Ch. 10 (the arrest). The unit "focus on language and power" omits its best evidence. Day 4 teaches Newspeak from the appendix alone, before students have met Syme. *Why it matters:* students write the research essay without having read the novel's core passages on the topic. *Principle:* R1 (think like an English teacher); "the text at the center"; close reading of the right passages.
3. **The "research-backed" essay has no research instruction or sources.** There is one day of two "teacher-selected" excerpts, with no titles, critics or suggested search. Nothing covers finding or judging sources, signal phrases or integrating quotations. "Politics and the English Language" (Orwell, 1946) is not proposed. *Why it matters:* the culminating assessment requires a skill the unit never teaches. Students will paste in quotes or rely on SparkNotes-level sources. *Principle:* clear objective and alignment; R10 (leave a labeled slot *and name what goes in it*); worked examples and modeling.
4. **Pacing is not feasible on several days.** Day 2 reads Ch. 1 aloud plus a starter and pair share in 50 minutes. Day 4 reads the full appendix in class. Day 9 assigns the whole of Part 3 overnight. Meanwhile Days 15–20 have no reading. Page ranges are given without an edition and do not match common editions (Ch. 1 is not "pp. 1–10"). *Why it matters:* teachers lose days to overrun, and students fall behind on the hardest reading. *Principle:* feasibility; spacing.
5. **There is no context day, vocabulary or access support, and the stack and plan are empty.** There is no Orwell, no 1948/1949, no Stalin or Hitler, no WWII ration-era London, no map of Oceania/Eurasia/Eastasia, and no Newspeak glossary. There are no scaffolds for weaker readers or English learners. The unit's stack and printable plan PDF are blank ("Untitled lesson", "0 parts"). *Why it matters:* students meet "Airstrip One", "Ingsoc" and "the Two Minutes Hate" cold, and the teacher has nothing printable for the unit. *Principle:* R1–R5; scaffolding and access; Yawp!'s "ready to print and use".

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **Day 7:** "Julia says she only rebels from the waist down." In the novel it is Winston who says it to Julia: "You're only a rebel from the waist downwards" (Part 2, Ch. 5). The line is also outside the assigned reading (Part 2, Ch. 1–3). High confidence.
- **Day 5:** "Close reading of the doublethink passage" is set on Part 1, Ch. 6–7. The doublethink passage ("To know and not to know...") is in Part 1, Ch. 3, which was Day 3's reading. Doublethink is fully defined in Goldstein's book (Part 2, Ch. 9). Ch. 7 has the related "two plus two make four" passage. High confidence on the Ch. 3 location.
- **Page numbers** (Ch. 1 "pp. 1–10", Part 3 "pp. 237–297", appendix "pp. 309–323") have no edition named and do not match the common Signet Classic paging as far as I recall. Part 1 Ch. 1 alone runs well past p. 10 in any standard edition. Medium confidence on the specific numbers; high confidence that Ch. 1 is longer than 10 pages.
- The Day 9 O'Brien paraphrase ("not interested in the overt act — only in the mind") is a fair paraphrase of "The Party is not interested in the overt act: the thought is all we care about" (Part 3, Ch. 2). Correct. "He loved Big Brother." is correct as the final line of the narrative.
- The prose/map class-starter mismatch is described in Problem 1.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Generate the unit summary from the unit JSON, not separately.** Any list of "Week N, Day M" prompts in the prose should be derived from the `days` array, or validated against it before sending. *(Brian: names exactly right; teacher usability)*
2. **Check reading coverage for whole-novel units.** When a unit covers a whole novel, require that every chapter is assigned on some day and that nightly homework stays under a page cap (for example, ~30 pages). For theme-focused units, require that the theme's canonical passages are placed (for 1984 language and power: Syme in Part 1 Ch. 5, the Ministry of Truth in Ch. 4, Hate Week in Part 2 Ch. 9, the appendix). Never give page numbers without naming the edition; prefer chapter references. *(R1, R11, feasibility)*
3. **Add a research strand to "research-backed" units.** Require named, suggested secondary sources, or a labeled slot with what to look for (R10). Require a day on finding and judging sources and a day on integrating quotations with signal phrases. Suggest the obvious companion text when one exists (Orwell's "Politics and the English Language"). *(alignment; R10; modeling)*
4. **Always open a whole-book unit with a context day plus a vocabulary plan.** That means R1–R5 context (author, period, map, timeline, faces) and the book's coined terms, plus a standing access line for each reading day (audio, chunked passage, guiding questions). *(R1–R5; scaffolding and access)*
5. **Give unit conversations a real stack and printable plan.** Either a unit overview PDF or a clear "build Day 1" path, instead of an empty "Untitled lesson" and a plan PDF titled with the raw request and "0 parts". *(Yawp! materials ready to print; feasibility)*
