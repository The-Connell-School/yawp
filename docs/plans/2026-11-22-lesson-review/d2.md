# d2: Hamlet, "To be, or not to be" (12th grade, 50 minutes)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Teach a 50-minute lesson for 12th grade on Hamlet's 'To be, or not to be' soliloquy (Act 3, Scene 1). Include the slide deck."

**What came back:** One turn took 120 s. The plan is "To be, or not to be — Close Reading Hamlet's Soliloquy" with one objective and five parts adding to 50 minutes:
- Class starter, 5 min, with three options.
- Close reading, 25 min: first read 5, three-pass pair annotation 15, whole-class share 5.
- Short Response, 12 min.
- Closing discussion "Is Hamlet being honest with himself?", 5 min.
- Exit ticket, 3 min, with three options: explain a concept, understand the text, wondering reflection.

The plan includes a one-page Close Reading handout (full soliloquy, three tasks, push question) and a 14-slide deck labeled "55 min" with a "Fit the deck to 50 minutes" link. Because the handout came in the first reply, the planner offered no "Build the student handout" chip, so the second turn did not run. `handouts.pdf` returned **HTTP 500**, so I judged the handout from the reply text.

After "Add everything to the lesson" the stack shows **90 of 50 minutes planned (40 over)** and **56% of class is writing**. It contains:
- a generic Class starter (5) plus all three starters (5 each);
- Close reading (25);
- Short Response (12) *and* a second Short Response "What the speech reveals" (10);
- Closing discussion (5);
- two Exit tickets (3 and 5);
- the Close Reading handout as a 10-minute "Read aloud" step at minute 80, *after* the exit ticket.

The plan PDF is 3 pages with 11 parts. It leaves "Add this lesson's goals" as a placeholder, gives the first exit ticket as "—", and files the handout under "Closing".

## 2. Scores (1 to 4)

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** It opens on the theme in students' experience: "Is it ever braver to endure something terrible than to end it?" The teacher listens for "fear" and says "Hold onto that. Hamlet is about to make exactly that argument." Slides 11 and 13 tie Hamlet's answer back to it. Direct teaching of where we are in the play is missing: Claudius and Polonius are hidden to spy, and Ophelia is the bait.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 3.** The handout prints the full 33-line soliloquy accurately. The three passes (question / every suffering listed / the turn) are specific, real close reading. But slide 6 "First read" has no text and does not say where to find it. Only five lines ever appear on screen (slide 10). The planned answer for "the turn" is pinned to "the undiscovered country" when "ay, there's the rub" is an equally defensible turn.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 2.** There is no glossary for *contumely, bodkin, fardels, quietus, bourn, mortal coil, rub, consummation, disprized*, either on slides or on the handout. "Conscience" as "consciousness" appears only in an early-finisher push question. The slide 9 speaker note claims the listed ills "are all social humiliations, not physical pain". That is wrong for "heartache", "the thousand natural shocks / That flesh is heir to" and "pangs of disprized love".
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R2–R7, R13): 1.** The only image is Hokusai's *Great Wave*, used four times (slides 1, 5, 11, 14). It is a decorative metaphor from 19th-century Japan, with nothing from Hamlet's world. There is no production still, no Elsinore, no Globe, and no faces of Hamlet, Ophelia, Claudius or Polonius. Content slides 5, 6, 7, 9 and 11 have nothing of the thing itself to look at. The image is credited (R11 met for credit).
- **E. Writing: 3.** There is a lot of good writing (56% in the stack): a 4-minute starter, a 12-minute Short Response that asks for a claim grounded in a line, a 60-second written vote before discussion, and three exit tickets aligned to the objective with clear "watch for" notes. The Short Response prompt partly hands students the answer ("he has answered a different question entirely").
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** There are pair annotation with circulation, slide-by-slide report-backs on each task (slides 8–10), a written yes/no vote with a reason, and an exit ticket with a rubric (mustMention, watchFor). The plan never says what to do if pairs miss the turn, beyond "name it".
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The plan adds to 50, but the deck adds to 55 and its own agenda slide (slide 2) adds to 45. "Add everything" produces a 90-minute stack with a duplicated Short Response and the handout placed at minute 80. The stack's suggested fix, "Trim Close reading — the soliloquy to 3 min", would gut the lesson. The handouts PDF fails (500). The speaker notes say the recordings run "about 90 seconds"; the speech runs nearer three minutes in performance.
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** Options are offered, not prescribed: "Pick one", "Run this in class, or assign it for homework", and three exit tickets explained by purpose. The speaker notes are excellent and concrete (slide 8: "Push students past the first line"; slide 12: "You've told me what he says. What does that tell you about him?"). The stack and plan PDF are incoherent after "Add everything" (see G).
- **I. Access: 2.** Pair work and sentence stems on the discuss slides ("We think the question is...", "They all seem to be about...") help. There is no glossary, modern paraphrase, chunked version or audio-with-text for weaker readers and English learners. Differentiation is only offered as a follow-up chip.
- **Overall: 3.** A strong teacher would use the plan and the handout with edits: add a glossary and a dramatic-context slide, replace the Great Wave, and put the text on the reading slides. The stack and printing need cleaning by hand.

## 3. Strengths

1. **Accurate full text and a well-designed annotation task.** The handout gives the whole soliloquy correctly. The three passes make students find the real question ("It is not just the first line"), count the ills, and locate the turn: this is close reading with a purpose.
2. **The arc from students' own thinking to Hamlet's argument is well built.** The starter on endurance versus escape, then "Hold onto that", then the turn, then "Is Hamlet being honest with himself?" lets students set their view beside the text's (R12 step 4).
3. **Speaker notes and exit tickets written by someone who has taught this.** Each exit ticket states what to assess and the typical misreading ("'he is thinking about whether to live or die' is summary, not a claim"). The notes give the teacher exact language for nudging.

## 4. Problems (most serious first)

1. **Decorative, repeated imagery and no picture of the play's world.** Slides 1, 5, 11 and 14 all use Hokusai's *Great Wave*, and every other content slide is words only. There is no Hamlet, no Ophelia, no Claudius and Polonius behind the arras, and no production still. *Why it matters:* students get a mood, not the scene, and the deck teaches nothing visually about who is speaking, where, or who is listening. *Principle:* R4, R6, R7, R13.
2. **The reading slide has no reading.** Slide 6 "First read" shows three verbs ("Listen / Mark / Hold") but not the soliloquy, and does not say "see your handout". Only five lines appear on screen (slide 10). Combined with the handout PDF failing (500), a teacher projecting the deck has no text to point at. *Why it matters:* students cannot annotate or follow along together. *Principle:* R9, R10.
3. **No vocabulary and no dramatic context.** *Contumely, bodkin, fardels, quietus, bourn, coil, rub* are never defined on a slide or the handout. Nothing reminds students that Claudius and Polonius have just hidden to watch Hamlet, which is central to the closing question "Is Hamlet being honest with himself?" *Why it matters:* weaker readers stall on line 16, and every student debates honesty without knowing the speech may be overheard. *Principle:* R5, R1, scaffolding and access.
4. **Timing does not add up across plan, deck and stack.** The plan totals 50, the deck 55, and the deck's agenda 45. "Add everything" yields 90 minutes: four starters, two Short Responses, two exit tickets, and the handout as a 10-minute read-aloud at minute 80, after the exit ticket. The stack's own fix proposes cutting the close reading to 3 minutes. The plan PDF loses the goals and the first exit ticket ("—"). *Why it matters:* a teacher who trusts the stack or prints the plan gets a broken run of show. *Principle:* Brian's "a deck's minutes must add up to the lesson"; feasibility. The "Add everything" behavior is itself a problem: it should add the plan, not every alternative.
5. **Suicide-adjacent starters with no care note.** "Is it ever braver to endure something terrible than to end it? Write about a moment — yours..." and "What... about death actually frightens people most" invite personal disclosure about ending things, with no note to the teacher about framing, opting out, or counselor follow-up. *Why it matters:* some 17-year-olds in the room may be at risk. A department chair would require a framing note and a non-personal alternative. *Principle:* "expert, kind, on the teacher's side"; Brian's framing choices.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **The soliloquy text on the handout is accurate** (a standard modern conflation: "pitch" from Q2, "does make cowards" from F1). There is a small inconsistency: the plan prose says "conscience doth make cowards", while the handout and slide notes say "does". Both are legitimate readings, but the materials should agree. High confidence.
- **Slide 9 speaker note:** the ills "are all social humiliations, not physical pain". This is overstated: "heartache", "the thousand natural shocks that flesh is heir to", and "pangs of disprized love" are not social humiliations. Most of the middle list (contumely, the law's delay, the insolence of office, the spurns of merit) is. High confidence.
- **Slide 8 note:** "The full question runs through line 4." It runs to "And by opposing end them", at the start of line 5. Minor; high confidence.
- **Recordings:** "Ian McKellen's 1971 BBC version" cannot be verified. McKellen played Hamlet on stage in 1971 (Prospect Theatre), and I know of no BBC broadcast of it. The Tennant performance is from the 2009 BBC film of the RSC (2008) production. "About 90 seconds" is short for any full performance. Medium confidence; the teacher should check the links before class.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Ban repeating the same image, and ban decorative art on content slides, before the deck ships.** For a play, require at least a cast slide (characters with names), the scene's dramatic situation (who is onstage or hidden), and a production still or labeled slot (R10). *(R4, R6, R7, R13)*
2. **Require every "read" or "first read" slide to carry the passage or an explicit "Handout, p. 1" pointer**, and put the chunk being discussed on each discuss slide. *(R9)*
3. **Auto-generate a glossary for archaic or low-frequency words in any printed passage**, on the handout margin and on a slide before the first read. Add a one-slide "where we are in the play" for any mid-play scene. *(R5, R1, access)*
4. **Make "Add everything" add the plan as written (one starter, one exit ticket, no duplicate Short Response), and place handouts beside the step that uses them, not at the end.** Validate that deck minutes, agenda minutes and plan minutes match before showing the deck. Never let the stack's "fix" cut the core reading to 3 minutes. *(Brian: minutes must add up; feasibility)*
5. **Flag sensitive topics (suicide, self-harm, trauma) and attach a framing note and a non-personal alternative starter**, such as "Write about a character who chose to stay." *(expert, kind, on the teacher's side; framing choices)*
