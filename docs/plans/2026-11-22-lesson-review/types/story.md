## 2. Fit for a short story

- R12, R30 **fit**.
- R29 **fits**. Craft claims about the narrator ("he rarely moralizes") are the story version of telling, so the rule could name narrator and tone.
- S3 **fits**. For a story it should mean the whole story is read before the questions that need it.
- S4, S6, S9, B1, B2 **fit**.
- R26, R27, R31 **fit**.
- R28 **awkward** for stories with a twist: it guards theme but not plot. Telling the twist on a slide before students read it is a parallel failure the rule doesn't name.
- R32 **awkward** for a one-day story (no unit tracker). It should read "the opening question returns at the close".
- R9, S1 **fit**, and should be stronger: a public-domain short story fits on a few pages, so the whole text should be printed.
- R11 **fits**.
- R24 **awkward**: written for copyrighted works and modern translations. For a translated public-domain story it should require naming the translator and printing from that translation, which is what was missed.
- R25 **fits**.
- R1, R2, R3, R21, R22, R23, R7, R8 **fit**. "The Necklace" lives on what things looked like (the ball, the jewels, the flat), so R22 is especially apt.
- R4 **fits**. A story has three or four characters, and naming them first is cheap.
- R5, R6 **fit**.
- Y1, Y2, B3, S5 (starters), room to write, S5 **fit**.
- R14, R15, R17, B7, B8, Y3, R19 **fit**.

**Starters and R27.** No conflict here: the planner wrote its own three questions and used no library prompt. All passed.

## 3. Proposed rules for a short story
1. Students read the whole story (in class or before) before any question about its ending, and no slide or prompt reveals a twist before students have read it.
2. A short public-domain story is printed whole on the handout with paragraph numbers, from a named translation when it is translated.
3. The characters are introduced by name and relation (Mathilde, her husband Loisel, her friend Mme Forestier) before the first read.

## 4. Overall: 2 (needs major work)
The opening questions, discussion moves ("Does Maupassant seem to agree?") and minutes are good. But students write about an ending they never read, slide 12 gives it away, the translation is unnamed and passage 1 doesn't match it, and the deck is mostly words.

**The one change:** print the whole story from a named public-domain translation and read it to the end, either in class with tighter context or as reading before class, before any question about the fake necklace, so students discover the ending rather than being told it.
