# Review: research (evaluating sources, grade 11, 50 min)

Request: "Teach a 50-minute lesson for 11th grade on evaluating sources for a research paper. Include the slide deck."

**Turn 1 (6 s):** the planner answered only with a question, "What are you reading with them, and what's the research paper on?", plus a class picker (`0-question.png`). The test replied: "It is for the first class you listed, a 50-minute period. If you need a novel, use Of Mice and Men. Please go ahead and build the whole lesson." **Turn 2:** the lesson arrived in 120 s. 12 slides, 7 handout pages, 1 picture (Steinbeck).

The teaching moves are good: four named criteria, a think-aloud on Wikipedia, a real all-class check with a plan for a split room, and nuanced pushes on the blog and the advocacy site ("use it for what, and say what it is"). But the lesson's materials undercut its subject. Students rate one-line *descriptions* of sources that already tell them the verdict ("No author credentials listed. No citations."), never a real source. Source 1 looks invented but is presented as a real citation. And the student handouts print an "Answer Key" page with different questions that use a term never taught ("CRAAP").

## 3. Proposed rules for research lessons

1. A research-skills lesson puts real, checkable sources in students' hands (a printed page or screenshot with author, date and URL visible, or a labelled slot for the teacher's own), never summaries that already judge the source, and never invented books, authors or citations.
2. Source evaluation teaches lateral reading (leave the page to find out who is behind it) alongside any checklist, and relevance to the student's own research question is taught as a criterion before it is assessed.
3. When a skills lesson names no text or topic, the planner builds the lesson at once around a labelled slot for the class's research topic, and never opens by asking what the class is reading.

## 4. Overall

**2: needs major work.** The teacher moves are strong, but invented and pre-judged sources, an answer-key page printed for students, and the question-only first turn mean it can't go to class as is.

**The one change:** replace the four one-line descriptions, including the invented "California Dreaming… Dr. Maria Sandoval", with real sources students examine themselves: Lange's *Migrant Mother* with its FSA/Library of Congress record, the real Wikipedia "Dust Bowl" page, and a real advocacy page. Leave a labelled slot for the teacher's own sources.
