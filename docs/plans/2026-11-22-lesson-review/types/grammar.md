# Review: grammar (semicolons in their own writing, grade 9, 50 min)

Request: "Teach a 50-minute lesson for 9th grade on using semicolons correctly in their own writing. Include the slide deck." Arrived in 102 s, no question asked. 13 slides, no pictures, 3 handout pages (the answer key is correctly left out of the student packet).

The instruction itself is the best I/We/You sequence in this batch: a think-aloud fix on "The coach called a timeout, the players were exhausted", a class fix together, and a portable "Cover Test". But the request said **in their own writing**, and that part gets 7 minutes, no handout and nothing collected. The script measured 18% of the class as writing. The reply opens with narration, and the deck has no visuals at all, not even marked-up sentences.

## 3. Proposed rules for grammar lessons

1. A grammar lesson shows each rule through example pairs, wrong against right, with the structure marked on the slide (clauses bracketed, the mark highlighted), and its practice set mixes correct and incorrect items about evenly and covers the common error patterns.
2. When a request says "in their own writing", at least a third of the period goes to students applying the skill to their own draft, producing a collected before-and-after with one sentence on why the change works.
3. Every grammar lesson teaches when *not* to use the structure as well as when to, and gives students a one-line success criterion they check their own revision against.

## 4. Overall

**3: usable with edits.** The teaching sequence is strong; the part of the request about their own writing is thin.

**The one change:** make the own-writing repair the center: 15 minutes, as a Yawp! writing task in which students paste an original pair of sentences, their semicolon revision and why it works (or why a period or "but" is better), taking the time from the comma-splice practice set.
