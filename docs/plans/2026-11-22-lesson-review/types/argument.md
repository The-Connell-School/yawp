# Review: argument writing (counterclaim and rebuttal, grade 10, 50 min)

Request: "Teach a 50-minute writing lesson for 10th grade on writing a counterclaim and rebuttal in an argument essay. Include the slide deck." Arrived in 114 s, no question asked. 11 slides, 6 handout pages, 1 picture (a Lincoln–Douglas Debates stamp).

Overall this is a sound writing lesson. It defines the move, shows a fair counterclaim next to a strawman, has students label a model, and gives them 18 minutes to write their own counterclaim and rebuttal. The weak points: the teacher only analyses a finished model and never writes one live; there is no success checklist for students to revise against; the deck and handouts follow rules written for literature; and the reply ends with a quotation line that makes no sense here.

## 3. Proposed rules for writing lessons

1. In every writing lesson, the teacher composes an example live (thinking aloud, choosing between options, revising a sentence) before students write, not only analyses a finished model.
2. Every writing task gives students a short success checklist (three items at most, e.g. "objection a reasonable person would make / real concession / rebuttal answers the objection"), and the lesson saves time for students to check and revise their own draft against it.
3. A mentor text the planner writes is labelled as written by Yawp!, is identical wherever it appears, and contains only evidence that is accurate or clearly hypothetical.

## 4. Overall

**3: usable with edits.**

**The one change:** before the 18-minute writing block, have the teacher draft a counterclaim and rebuttal live on the practice thesis, then give students a three-item success checklist to check and revise their own paragraph against before the share.
