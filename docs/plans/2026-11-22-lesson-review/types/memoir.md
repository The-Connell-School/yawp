## 2. Fit for memoir / essay

- R12 **fits**.
- R26 **awkward**: a memoir has a story and an argument. The pair should read "topic / what the author argues or comes to understand", not "theme".
- R27 **fits**.
- R28 **awkward**: modeling analysis needs a model claim. The rule should allow one claim modeled on a passage students won't write about, and forbid stating the author's argument as the answer.
- R29 **fits**: the history (the Aulds, the 1845 risk) is direct teaching.
- R30, R31 **fit**.
- R32 **fits**: it already names nonfiction ("the author's argument").
- R1, R2, R3, R21 **fit**: geography and dates are part of Douglass's argument (the Eastern Shore, Baltimore, the North).
- R20 **fits**, but the place list needs historical US places (Talbot County, Tuckahoe, St. Michaels).
- R4 **awkward**: no likenesses of the Aulds exist. The rule should say "or a labeled slot / a name card when no likeness exists".
- R22 **fits**: 1820s Baltimore is far from students.
- R23 **awkward**: the code counts the compare slide (two marked passages) as words only, though a marked passage is one of R23's own options.
- R11, S1, R9, S7, S9 **fit**.

## 3. Proposed rules for memoir and essay

1. A memoir lesson helps students hear two voices, the child then and the writer now, and asks where the older writer comments on the younger, because that is where the argument lives.
2. A nonfiction lesson names the author's audience and purpose at the time of writing (here, white Northern readers in 1845, published while Douglass was still a fugitive), so students read the piece as an act as well as a story.
3. An excerpt is printed in the author's order, with cuts marked by ellipses, and every passage the lesson discusses, quotes or asks about is on the handout.

## 4. Overall

**2 — needs major work.** The design is strong: a topic opening, modeled annotation, a well-built analysis mini-lesson, and the starter returning at the close. But the handout at its center is corrupted and leaves out the passages the lesson turns on, the warning is placed in the wrong chapter, and the lesson runs 57 minutes.

**Top change:** reprint the handout straight from the source text, in order and uncorrupted, with Mrs. Auld's change, the full inch/ell sentence, the Columbian Orator and "the more I read…" passages, and cut it to fit 50 minutes.
