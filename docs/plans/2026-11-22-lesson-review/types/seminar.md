## 2. Fit for a seminar

- R12 **awkward**: after a whole novel, "find out what they know" and "teach directly" don't apply. The arc for a seminar is: open question → private write → rounds → written claim. The rule should say so.
- R26, R27 **fit**: the starter should be an open topic question ("When does power change people?"), not a claim.
- R28 **awkward**: a seminar legitimately puts a reading on the table to test ("Does the novel prove…?"). The rule should allow a tentative reading posed as a question to evaluate, and forbid stating it as the answer.
- R29, R30, R31 **fit**.
- R32 **fits**: the opening question should return in the last round or in the Short Response.
- S4 **awkward**: "two lines or one sentence" is a close-reading model. A seminar's model is one talk move shown once (cite a page, then build on a peer).
- S6 **awkward**: an everyone-answers question interrupts the talk. A seminar's check is a pre-seminar write plus a talk tracker.
- R9/S1 **fit**, with a seminar twist: students need their passages marked before the talk.
- R10, R24 **fit**: a seminar leans on a few anchor passages, so the copyright slot matters more here, not less.
- R7/R13, R22, R23 **wrong here**: pictures on the question slides compete with the question. The auto checks should exempt discuss slides, and the world slide should never fire after the novel.
- R1–R3, R20, R21, R8 **wrong here**: a context day after the whole novel is a rabbit hole.
- Y1 **awkward**: a seminar is mostly talk. The rule should ask for writing before and after the talk rather than a share of the period.
- B3 **fits**, though a seminar reflection ticket (what moved your thinking, who moved it) would suit it better.
- Room to write **fits**.

## 3. Proposed rules for seminars

1. A seminar gives students a prep sheet: the questions in advance, with room to note one passage and its page for each.
2. A seminar has a structure the teacher can run and assess: inner and outer circle roles, or a tracker of who speaks, cites and builds, with a task for the outer circle.
3. For each question, the teacher's notes list the passages (chapter and page) students are likely to bring, and one follow-up for each.

## 4. Overall

**3 — usable with edits.** The three questions are strong and well-timed, the talk moves and push-backs are what a good seminar leader says, and the Short Response asks students for their own claim. But the starter is a theme claim, a copyrighted passage is quoted from memory, and there is no prep or tracker sheet, which a seminar lives on.

**Top change:** add a student prep-and-tracker handout: the three questions with room for a passage and page for each, plus an outer-circle tracker. Replace "Power corrupts…" with an open question such as "When does having power change a person?"
