## 2. Fit for a novel (day 1)

- R12 **fits**. Step 2 (find out what they know) matters most on a novel's day 1.
- R29, R30, R31 **fit**.
- R26 **fits**.
- R27 **fits**, and it is where the library collides with the rule (see below).
- R28 **awkward**: its list ("slide, title, starter…") doesn't say that a starter which is itself a theme of the book counts as telling. FW-134 shows that is the commonest leak.
- R32 **fits**. A novel is where tracking topics over weeks pays off most.
- S3, S4, S6, S9, B1, B2 **fit**.
- R9 / S1 **awkward** for a copyrighted novel students already hold. "The slide says exactly where to find it" should require the edition page range and first/last words, sized to the minutes given.
- R10, R11, R24, R25 **fit**.
- R1, R2, R3, R20, R21, R22 **fit**. A novel's context day is exactly what they were written for.
- R4 **awkward**: "the author is on the title slide" competes with the cover on a novel's title slide. Either is fine; the rule should allow the cover with the author nearby. "Main characters introduced by name" is essential on a novel's day 1.
- R5, R6, R7, R23, R8 **fit**.
- Y1, Y2, B3, S5 (starters), room to write **fit**.
- S5 (access) **fits**. "Dialect" should be read to cover vernacular dialogue in prose.
- S7 **awkward**: it triggers on what the lesson touches. On day 1 of a unit whose book contains racial slurs, a mercy killing and ableist language, the teacher needs the care note on day 1. The plan even affirms "Lennie is dumb" ("You're onto something").
- R14, R15, R17, B7, B8, Y3, R19 **fit**.

**Starters and R27.** The conflict is real in practice. Two of the three library starters this run drew are agree/disagree claims. The planner put one on the slide and described all three as opening the topics "without giving anything away". It doesn't see a declarative prompt as a claim. Anticipation-guide statements are a respected pre-reading move, but R27 forbids exactly this form. FW-134 is worse: it is close to a theme of this book.

## 3. Proposed rules for a novel
1. On a novel's day 1, introduce the main characters by name with one fact each before the first read, and give students a topic-tracker page with one column per thematic topic and their first entry (a line and its page) before they leave.
2. A reading span in a copyrighted novel names the chapter, its first and last words and the pages in the class edition, and fits the minutes given.
3. When a character is disabled or a target of slurs in the book, the notes give the teacher respectful words to use and how to answer a student who reaches for the slur or "dumb".

## 4. Overall: 3 (usable with edits)
The discussion moves ("which sentence?") are strong, the facts are right and the minutes agree. But two starters hand students the book's themes, the close reading has no model and no check, and the reply narrates its process.

**The one change:** put the passage in students' hands (teacher's pages, or a labeled page-range slot on the read slide and handout) and model marking one detail aloud on the first paragraph before students mark their own.
