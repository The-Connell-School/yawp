## 3. Strengths

1. **The starter and its return.** Students commit in writing to a view on dying for one's country before they know the poem exists, and slide 17 sends them back to that page. This is R12 done properly, and the plan tells the teacher what to say when she gets there.
2. **Speaker notes a teacher can teach from.** How to voice each stanza (slide 8), what counts as a good answer (slide 10: "beggars", "hags", "lame", "blind", "drunk"), the gloss on "misty panes" as the mask's eyepieces (slide 12), and the next question to ask when a student says "the reader" (slide 15).
3. **Sentence stems and a real vocabulary slide.** Every pair question comes with a stem ("The word '___' is strange here because it usually means ___, but Owen uses it to mean ___"), and slide 6 defines guttering, blood-shod and ecstasy with the right line numbers.

## 4. Problems, most serious first

1. **The poem's line numbers are wrong on the handout, the plan and the diagram.** The poem is 8 + 6 + 2 + 12 lines. The lesson says stanza 2 is lines 9 to 16, stanza 3 is 17 to 18 and stanza 4 is 19 to 28 (plan step 4, handout questions, slide 9). On the printed handout the margin numbers restart at stanza 4 (5, 10, then 5, 10 again) and stanza 3 is set flush left with no numbers. Slide 6 meanwhile correctly calls "He plunges at me" line 16. In a close reading, a student told to look at line 19 has no way to find it. Breaks R11 and "materials ready to use".
2. **Names and facts in the context are wrong.** "Craigie Hospital" appears in the plan, on the map label and on Owen's slide; the hospital was Craiglockhart. The map note says Owen "fought near" Ypres; his front-line service was on the Somme and at St Quentin in 1917 and on the Sambre canal in 1918. The planner's "Check before class" list did not catch either. Breaks R11.
3. **58 minutes for a 50-minute class.** The title says 50, the parts say 58, and the stack accepts 58 without comment. The teacher finds out at the bell, and what falls off the end is the writing the lesson was built toward. Breaks Brian's rule that the minutes must add up to the lesson.
4. **The two things a 10th grader most needs are never put in front of them: what the Latin means and what irony is.** The translation exists only as something the teacher writes on the board in plan step 2; no slide and no line of the handout carries it, and the title slide's notes tell the teacher to hold it back for a reveal that never comes. Slide 17 offers "That is what irony does: it uses the enemy's words against them", which is not a definition. Breaks R5.
5. **The poem's world is not shown, and the poem is flattened on screen.** There is no picture of a gas helmet ("clumsy helmets", "misty panes"), a trench or a recruiting poster, and only four pictures were ever asked for. Stanzas appear as prose with slashes, so line endings, the point of a poem, are lost; stanza 3 and most of stanza 4 are not on any slide, and the question slides come before the text slides they refer to. Breaks R6, R7, R13 and R9 for slides 14 and 15.

Also: the same question is written three times (handout box, Short Response, exit ticket 1); the handout's "Stanza 1" heading is stranded at the bottom of page 2 with its question on page 3; the reply shows stray asterisks in the "Check before class" list.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **"Craigie Hospital"** (plan step 2, slides 4 and 5): should be Craiglockhart War Hospital, Edinburgh. High confidence.
- **Stanza line ranges** (plan step 4, handout, slide 9): stanza 2 is lines 9 to 14, stanza 3 is 15 to 16, stanza 4 is 17 to 28. High confidence. The handout's printed margin numbers also restart at stanza 4.
- **"Ypres … Owen fought near here"** (slide 4): not so; the note belongs with the Somme, if anywhere. Fairly confident.
- **Slide 16 title**, "The last two lines", over four lines. Minor.
- **Irony** as "using words that mean the opposite of what the speaker believes" (exit ticket 2) and "uses the enemy's words against them" (slide 17): loose to the point of misleading for the lesson's key term.
- **Correct as given:** the whole poem on the handout (I checked it line by line from memory; it is the standard text with "Of gas-shells dropping softly behind"); the three quotations and line numbers on slide 6; Owen 1893 to 1918, killed 4 November 1918, a week before the Armistice; Horace, Odes III.2; "It is sweet and fitting to die for one's country"; the Somme and Verdun figures as round numbers. High confidence on the poem.

The planner's checks say it could not reach its source text (two 503s and a timeout), verified no quotations, and flagged five facts for the teacher. All five flags are non-issues.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Count the lines of a poem from the text, never from the model.** Compute stanza ranges from the stored poem and use the same numbers in the plan, the handout, the diagram and the define slide; number the handout's lines continuously across stanzas. (R11)
2. **Enforce the period length.** If the parts exceed the minutes asked for, cut or mark a part "homework" and subtract it, and have the stack warn when planned minutes exceed the period. (Brian: minutes add up)
3. **For any text in another language, or any lesson that names a literary term in its objective, require the translation and the definition on a slide and on the handout.** (R5)
4. **Check proper names against a source and make "Check before class" list only what needs checking.** A flag that ends "no real error" should not be shown; a hospital name that matches no real hospital should be. (R11; teacher usability)
5. **Show a poem as a poem, and ask for the pictures the poem itself points to.** Keep line breaks on excerpt slides, put the text slide before its question, give every stanza a slide, and derive picture requests from the poem's own nouns (gas helmet, flares, wagon) before reaching for manuscripts. (R6, R9, R13)
