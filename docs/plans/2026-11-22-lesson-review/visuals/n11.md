## 3. Strengths

1. **The lesson is built on the right idea: charge, move, name.** Framing the letter as three answers to three charges ("untimely", "outsider", "extreme") gives 11th graders a way into a long text and matches how the letter is argued.
2. **A real worked example with real words.** Slide 8 puts two exact sentences on screen, marks "well timed", and the notes script the think-aloud ("well timed by whose clock? He is not denying the charge"). This is I do before you do.
3. **Writing before talking, and disagreement invited.** The Short Response comes before discussion so every student has a position, the discussion opens with "Who argued it doesn't work? Make your case", and the three exit tickets are the three kinds Brian asks for.

## 4. Problems, most serious first

1. **No reading in the close reading.** Step 3 says "Students read their passage, annotate"; slide 7 says "Read your passage. Find where King answers it"; the handout says "Read the passages below" and "Find a line in the letter". The only passages below are the clergymen's. King's letter is not on the handout, no paragraphs are named, and the reply never tells the teacher to supply it or says why it is missing (the letter is in copyright, which is presumably the reason; the d3 run said so, this one did not). Students who have not already read the letter have nothing to analyze. Breaks R9 and R10.
2. **Two of the three source quotations are under the wrong charge.** Under "Charge A — Untimely" the handout prints the passage that ends "…days when extreme measures are justified in Birmingham", which is the extremism charge. Under "Charge C — Extremist" it prints "…we are convinced that these demonstrations are unwise and untimely", which is the timing charge, with a note that extremism "is developed more fully in the letter itself". A pair assigned "Extremist" is handed the word "untimely" as their evidence. Breaks R11.
3. **Students must name strategies nobody has taught, and the handout gives away the rest.** The chart's third column is "The strategy (what to call it)" and the exit slide says "name the strategy", but no slide defines any (concession, redefinition, appeal to shared authority, turning an opponent's word). The teacher's key has a name only for Charge A, in three different wordings, one of them wrong ("reductio"). Meanwhile the "King's move" column is printed on the handout as "King's answer (paraphrased)", and the extra sheet prints the full answer for Charge A. What is left for students is to copy a paraphrase and guess a label. Breaks R5, and objective and alignment.
4. **The stack reports 76% writing; the lesson has 40%.** The 18-minute pair reading was added as a "Quick write" assignment and the handout row is tagged "Writing", so 38 minutes are counted. A teacher or administrator reading the stack gets a number that is nearly double the truth, on the measure Yawp! cares most about. Breaks Yawp!'s own value (the stack shows how much students write) and Brian's "minutes must add up".
5. **Handout and deck are not ready to run.** Eight printed pages for 50 minutes, two of them almost blank, with a generated "Close reading" sheet (pages 2 to 3) that repeats the handout and adds a page and a half of lines; a chart whose answer row is one thin line; "Your charge: [__________]" with brackets. In the deck the model (slide 8) comes after the "Your turn" steps (slide 7), and the ten minutes of pair work sit under a slide labeled "Share out, Whole class, 14 minutes". Breaks "materials ready to print and use" and I do before you do.

Smaller: the map gives no wider view and drops two of its four places (R2); the board question is not returned to; slide 8's citation prints "p. April 16, 1963"; both photo and credit are cut off; the printed plan has no class length and truncates its steps.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **Clergymen's quotations misfiled** (handout, Charges A and C), as described above. The wording of all three quotations from "A Call for Unity" is accurate; the first is lifted from the middle of a sentence and capitalized without a mark. High confidence.
- **"That's called *reductio*"** (plan step 3): King's answer on timing is not a reductio; he turns the clergymen's word back on them and shows who gets to decide what is "well timed". High confidence.
- **"scraps of paper passed by a sympathetic guard"** (plan step 2): King's own account is that the paper came from a friendly Black trusty (a prisoner), and the rest was written on a pad his lawyers were later allowed to leave. Fairly high confidence.
- **Timeline, April 16: "King begins writing in the newspaper margins"**: April 16 is the date on the letter; he began once he had seen the statement, a few days earlier. **April 3: "Marches begin"**: the campaign opened that day with sit-ins; marches followed. **April 12 for the statement's publication**: the statement is dated April 12 and was printed in the Birmingham papers on or just after that date, so the note's question "Why do you think the clergymen published their letter the day he was arrested?" rests on a coincidence I would not teach as intent. Medium confidence on each.
- Checked and correct: arrest on April 12, 1963, and solitary confinement; eight white Alabama clergymen including bishops and a rabbi; "unwise and untimely"; the April 10 state-court injunction; Project C for "Confrontation"; "nearly 7,000 words"; invited by a local affiliate; "injustice anywhere is a threat to justice everywhere"; Jesus, Paul, Lincoln and Jefferson among King's "extremists"; the March on Washington in August 1963; and both sentences on slide 8 ("We know through painful experience… disease of segregation"), which match the letter as first circulated. High confidence.
- The server's own check reports `"work": "none"`: it had no source text for the letter or the statement, so none of these quotations were machine-checked.

## 6. Fixes for the planner

1. **A close reading step must carry its text or a precise pointer, and say which.** Before replying, check that every "read your passage" step has either an excerpt in the handout or a named location (paragraph numbers or first and last words) plus a line telling the teacher what to bring and why. For in-copyright texts, say so every time, as the Hurston lesson did. (R9, R10)
2. **Check each quotation against its heading.** When a handout files quotations under labels ("untimely", "extremist"), verify that the label's key word, or a synonym the planner names, appears in the quotation; flag a mismatch. Add "A Call for Unity" and the letter to the source texts the accuracy check can match. (R11)
3. **If the objective says "name the strategy", teach the names.** Require a define slide and a word bank on the handout for any task that asks students to label a technique, and give the teacher one agreed name per item in the key, the same in plan, deck and handout. (R5; alignment)
4. **Count writing honestly in the stack.** Only steps whose main activity is individual writing should count toward "N% of class is writing"; a pair reading step with a chart should not become a "Quick write" assignment by default. (Yawp! values; Brian: minutes add up)
5. **Do not print the answer column, and order the deck as it will be taught.** Keep paraphrases of the author's moves in the teacher's key, not on the student sheet; place the model slide before the "your turn" steps; give pair work its own timed slide; and collapse generated duplicates so the packet is the handout plus one page each for the writing pieces. For maps, start from a view that contains every listed place. (I do, we do, you do; R2; materials ready to use)
