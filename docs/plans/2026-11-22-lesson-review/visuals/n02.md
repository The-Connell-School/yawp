## 3. Strengths

1. **Modeling is written in.** Step 3 scripts the teacher's think-aloud on the first two sentences ("He says it's hard to remember… What does that tell me about how reliable this account is?"), then a class step, then independent work, with sentence starters. Few generated lessons show the teacher how to go first.
2. **"The monster that I am: whose word is that?"** Slide 9 picks the one phrase in these chapters that carries the whole question and asks the right thing about it. The complication on slide 11 (is untested goodness the same as innate goodness?) is a senior-level question.
3. **Writing with real choices.** Three starters, three Short Responses, three exit tickets of the three kinds, each with what to look for and what to watch for; 42% of the period; timing that actually adds up.

## 4. Problems, most serious first

1. **A passage on the student handout is not what Shelley wrote where the handout says she wrote it.** The second "From Chapter 12" block begins "The winter advanced, and an entire revolution of the seasons had taken place since I awoke into life", which is chapter 15; continues "My attention at this time was particularly attracted to the working of one human mind; it was that of Felix", which I do not find in the novel (the real sentence goes on "solely directed towards my plan of introducing myself into the cottage of my protectors"); then repeats the pool passage's opening with an ellipsis; then ends with a genuine chapter 12 sentence. It is labeled "public domain text". Students are asked to argue from quotations; one of their sources is assembled. Breaks R11.
2. **The lesson's central complication rests on something the chapters contradict.** Plan step 4 and slide 11 ask whether his gentleness shows only that "he had not yet been rejected". By chapter 12 he has been abandoned by Victor, fled by the shepherd and stoned by a village (chapter 11). The sentence starter "When the De Lacey family reacts by ___, the creature becomes ___" cannot be completed: the De Laceys do not know he exists until chapter 15. A senior who has done the reading will say so. Breaks R11 and alignment of the task to the text assigned.
3. **The closing sends the class to the wrong sentence and the wrong chapter.** The plan describes the last sentence of chapter 12 as the creature at the chink in the wall feeling "sensations of a peculiar and overpowering nature"; that is chapter 11, and chapter 12 ends on spring and "anticipations of joy". The planner noticed, told the teacher in a note, and left the plan and slide 13's notes as they were. The plan and slide 13 also promise that the creature "finally speaks to them" in chapter 13; that is chapter 15. Breaks R11.
4. **The debate is one-sided because of the passages chosen.** The handout offers the "born monstrous" pairs nothing but his appearance, and leaves out the scenes where the chapters do the arguing: the village attack, his decision to stop taking the cottagers' food, the wood he cuts for them at night. "I was benevolent and good; misery made me a fiend", one chapter earlier, is never set beside today's reading. Half the pairs will have no quotation to find. (Discussion that requires evidence from the text.)
5. **Nobody the students are about to meet is introduced, and nothing shows the creature.** The De Laceys, Felix and Agatha are named only in passing on slide 6; no slide shows the 1831 frontispiece or any image of the creature the class is asked to judge. The one picture on a content slide is a title page. Breaks R4, R6, R13. The lesson also never returns to the starter (R12 part 4).

Also: the handout's claim frame prints as "the creature is [] because the text shows []"; slide 8's task lines overlap; the exit slide's prompt matches none of the three exit tickets; the stack places the handout after the step that uses it; the plan says to read to "I was a poor, helpless, miserable wretch", which is past the end of the handout's passage.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **Handout, second chapter 12 block:** a chapter 15 sentence, a sentence I cannot find in the novel, and a repeat, presented as one chapter 12 passage. High confidence on the chapter 15 sentence; fairly confident (not certain: I could not reach a copy of the text from this machine) that the "working of one human mind… Felix" sentence is not Shelley's.
- **"Sensations of a peculiar and overpowering nature"** placed at the end of chapter 12 (plan step 6): it is in chapter 11. High confidence. The planner's own check caught this and passed it to the teacher.
- **"He hadn't been rejected yet"** (plan step 4, slide 11) and the De Lacey sentence starter (plan step 3): contradicted by chapter 11 and by where the De Laceys' reaction falls (chapter 15). High confidence.
- **"Chapter 13 — the creature speaks"** (slide 13, plan step 6): chapter 13 is Safie's arrival; he speaks to old De Lacey in chapter 15. High confidence.
- **"Both excerpts are from the 1818 first edition"** (reply): the chapter numbers 11 and 12 are the 1831 numbering (1818 has volume II, chapters III and IV). Minor.
- **Correct as given:** the chapter 11 opening (one colon where most printings have a semicolon), the pool passage on the handout and slide 9, "I was a poor, helpless, miserable wretch", "accursed creator" as chapter 15, Walton writing to his sister, the gloss of "benevolent" with the wood-gathering.

The planner's checks report 20 quotations verified, 1 unverified, 1 misplaced, 1 left for the teacher. The unverified one was not shown to the teacher.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **A block quotation is one continuous run of the source or it is not printed.** Verify each handout passage as a whole against the stored text; a block that matches only in pieces, or spans chapters, is rebuilt from the source at the stated chapter. Never ship an unverified sentence inside quotation marks without telling the teacher which one. (R11)
2. **When a check finds a misplaced quotation, fix the lesson, not just the footnote.** Rewrite the step and the slide notes that depend on it. (R11; teacher usability)
3. **Give the planner a chapter-by-chapter event list for stored works and test the lesson's claims against it**: who has met whom by this chapter, what happens next chapter. "Not yet rejected" and "Chapter 13: he speaks" would both fail. (R11)
4. **For an argument lesson, choose passages that give each side something to quote**, and say which side each passage serves; prefer scenes of action from the assigned chapters over a second helping of the same mood. (evidence-based discussion; alignment)
5. **Introduce the people of the day's chapters on a slide, and close the loop on the starter.** A "who you will meet" slide for the De Laceys, a picture of the creature from the book's own era, and a closing step that asks students to reread their starter beside the text. (R4, R13, R12)
