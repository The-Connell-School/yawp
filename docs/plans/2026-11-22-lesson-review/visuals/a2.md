# a2: To Kill a Mockingbird, day 1 (Maycomb before we read)

## 1. The request and what came back

> "Build day 1 of a unit on To Kill a Mockingbird for 9th grade, 50 minutes: Maycomb, the Great Depression and Jim Crow, the world of the novel before we read. Include the slide deck."

The planner did not ask a question first; it planned straight away (120 seconds). It returned a six-part plan that adds up to 50 minutes: Class starter (5, three prompts to choose from), What do you already know? (3), Context: Maycomb's world (18, with three framings to choose from), Discussion: What kind of place is Maycomb? (10), Short Response (10), Closing (4, with three exit-ticket options). The deck has 14 slides whose minutes total 50. No handout was written (the planner offered one as a follow-up); the handouts PDF is 3 pages of writing prompts with lines. The reply ends with a "Check before class" note about Scout's age. The stack shows 50 minutes planned and "38% of class is writing" (19 minutes: starter, Short Response, exit ticket).

## 3. Strengths

1. **The writing is ready to use.** The starter "A rule that felt wrong" puts the novel's question in students' own lives; the Short Response ("What does the law in Maycomb seem designed to protect, and who does it leave out?") requires a claim and a detail; exit ticket 1 watches for the right confusion (Jim Crow as slavery, or as attitude rather than law).
2. **The context is tied to the book in words.** Each defined term has an example that names Maycomb or Scout (slide 8); the timeline places "Scout's story begins" among the events (slide 6); slide 10 introduces Lee, Atticus and Scout before students open the book. This is R8 done on screen.
3. **Where, when and who are all shown.** A map, a timeline and the author's face, plus one photograph (slide 9) that is exactly the thing being taught. The planner also reported its own doubt about Scout's age to the teacher.

## 4. Problems

1. **Wrong or unsupported statements on slides students will read.** Slide 10: Scout is "Six years old when the story begins" (she is "almost six"; the planner's own note says so, and the plan text gets it right, but the slide was left wrong). Slide 10: Atticus "takes a case no one else will touch" (Judge Taylor appoints him). Slide 9: "In Alabama courtrooms, Black witnesses testified from a separate entrance", a sentence that does not make sense as written and that I cannot support. The same slide leaves out the Jim Crow fact the trial turns on: Black citizens were kept off juries. Students will carry these into the reading. Breaks R11.
2. **The Depression picture shows the wrong place.** Slide 7's text says poverty in Maycomb is "older than the crash"; the photograph is a Chicago soup line in 1931, and the cut-off credit does not tell students where it is. Photographs of Alabama farm families from 1936 are in the public domain and are the thing itself. Slide 12 uses a present-day color photo. Breaks R6, R13 and R11 (credits).
3. **A day about the world of the novel with none of the novel in it.** No passage, and no labeled slot such as "read aloud the description of Maycomb from chapter 1 in your copy". Only two characters are named; Jem, Dill, Calpurnia, Boo Radley and Tom Robinson are not. The Scottsboro trials (Alabama, 1931), the closest real event to the book's trial, are absent from the timeline. Students are told about Maycomb and never hear Scout describe it. Breaks R1, R4, R8, R10.
4. **The map and timeline stop short of their rules.** The map begins at Alabama, so a student who cannot place the state learns nothing about where it is, and it presents a fictional town as a real point (R2). The timeline's even spacing hides the gap the notes ask the teacher to dwell on ("why set the story 25 years in the past?"), and its pictures do not print (R3).
5. **Twenty minutes of teacher talk with nothing in students' hands, and the ending is told rather than done.** There is no note page for the terms, map and timeline, no sentence starters for the Short Response, and the hands-up check is not on a slide. In the closing, the teacher tells students the link back to the starter instead of having them write or say it. Breaks scaffolding and access, cognitive load, and R12 part 4.

Smaller: the plan says "pick one framing, or stack the first two", but the deck covers all three, so the choice changes nothing. The plan says struggling "since the 1890s" and slide 7's notes say "since Reconstruction". In the stack, the period length is blank ("-minute period") though the request said 50 minutes. The printable plan renames every step ("Map what the room knows"), so it does not match the stack or the agenda slide.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

Checked against my own knowledge of the novel and the history.

- **Slide 10, "Six years old when the story begins."** She is "almost six" (chapter 1). High confidence. The planner flagged this in "Check before class" but did not change the slide. Its note ("she turns six the first summer when Dill arrives") is also loose: she is almost six that summer.
- **Slide 10, Atticus "takes a case no one else will touch."** He is appointed by Judge Taylor (chapter 9). High confidence.
- **Slide 9, "Black witnesses testified from a separate entrance."** Garbled and, as far as I know, unsupported. Moderate confidence. Segregated seating and all-white juries are well documented.
- **Plan, Author framing: "Lee said she wanted to write the novel she wished she had been able to read as a child."** I cannot place this in anything Lee said; her known remark is that she wanted to be "the Jane Austen of south Alabama". Treat as unverified. Moderate confidence.
- **Slide 7's photograph** is Chicago, not the South. High confidence.
- **Slide 6:** "One in four Americans is unemployed" should be one in four workers; "millions lose jobs, savings, and farms within months" compresses three years into months. Minor.
- **Slide 1 notes** call the book jacket "the painting". Minor.

Correct as given: the story's years (1933 to 1935), publication in 1960, the Greensboro sit-ins in 1960, the Civil Rights Act four years later, the Pulitzer in 1961, Monroeville as the model, A. C. Lee's defense of two Black men who were hanged, Rosa Parks in 1955. The checks found no quotations because the lesson has none.

## 6. Fixes (to the planner)

1. **When the accuracy check doubts a statement, change the slide, then tell the teacher.** Here the check knew slide 10 was wrong and left it. Extend the check to claims about characters ("takes a case no one else will touch") and to history bullets. (R11)
2. **Match pictures on place as well as period, and print a readable caption** (what, where, when) on the slide rather than a cut-off credit. A request for "the American South, 1930s" should not accept Chicago. (R6, R11, R13)
3. **Maps start one step out and mark invented places.** Show the United States first or as an inset, name the neighbors, and label "Maycomb (fictional; based on Monroeville)". Draw timelines to scale or with a break mark, print their pictures, and include the real event closest to the plot. (R2, R3, R8)
4. **A context day includes the book's first words or a labeled slot for them,** and names the characters of the first chapters. For a copyrighted novel: "Read aloud from your copy: chapter 1, the paragraph beginning 'Maycomb was an old town'." (R1, R4, R9, R10)
5. **Any stretch of teaching over about 10 minutes comes with a one-page note sheet by default** (terms, a blank map or timeline to label, the Short Response starters), **and the closing has students answer the starter's question again themselves.** (scaffolding and access, R12 part 4)
