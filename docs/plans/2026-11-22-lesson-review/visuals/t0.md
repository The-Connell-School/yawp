## 3. Strengths

1. **The passage is real, complete and exact.** The aside on handout page 2 and slide 8 matches the play word for word, with line numbers every five lines so pairs can cite. The planner's own check corrected two quotations before the teacher saw them.
2. **One good question drives the lesson.** "The witches never said 'murder.' Where did that word come from?" appears in the plan, the pair-work notes and slide 11, and the teacher is told not to answer it before the pair work.
3. **The opening and closing belong together.** The starter ("captain of my destiny") is about the students, the notes say "hold what they say", and slide 13 brings the same words back and asks whether the scene agrees with Macbeth.

## 4. Problems

1. **Questions and directions point at lines students do not have, and one misstates the play.** Question 3 (plan step 4, handout page 3) says "Banquo notices Macbeth is 'rapt.' Macbeth says nothing to correct him." The handout's passage stops one line before Banquo says it, and Macbeth does answer: "Give me your favour: my dull brain was wrought / With things forgotten." That cover story is the best evidence for the public and private gap the lesson is about, and the lesson says it does not exist. The read-aloud step also tells the teacher to read "Macbeth's first public response" and give "Banquo's lines to a volunteer", but neither is on the handout. Slide 7 asks students to circle and underline with no text on it. Breaks R9 and R11, and discussion that requires evidence from the text.
2. **Most content slides are words only, and missing pictures leave no trace on the slide.** Slides 4, 5 and 7 have nothing to look at. The planner asked for Shakespeare's portrait and Holinshed and found neither; a portrait of Shakespeare is the easiest picture in the subject to supply. The teacher only learns this from the speaker notes of slides 1 and 4. Nothing shows the witches or the scene, though public-domain images of exactly this moment exist (Fuseli's three witches; the Holinshed woodcut of Macbeth and Banquo meeting the three women). Breaks R4, R7, R10, R13.
3. **The deck gives away its own answers and the lesson has one approved reading.** On slide 8 the colored phrases are the answers to the annotation tasks beside them. On slide 9 the third choice says "and that is the point". The objective states the conclusion ("awakens in him rather than plants"), and exit ticket 1 lists "the witches 'made' Macbeth want the crown" as a mix-up to watch for. For seniors, whether the witches plant or awaken the thought is the argument worth having, and "why do I yield to that suggestion" can be read both ways. Breaks checking for understanding (a check that shows its answer checks nothing) and evidence-based discussion.
4. **Minutes and paper do not line up.** Deck and plan total 50, but pair work is 12 minutes in the spoken directions and 15 on slide 10, read aloud is 12 against 9, Short Response is 10 against 8, and the exit ticket gets 2 minutes for "two or three sentences". The handouts PDF is 6 pages per student: question 1 sits at the bottom of page 2 with its answer lines on page 3, page 4 holds three stray lines, and the Short Response is printed twice (page 3 and page 5). The printable lesson plan stops each step mid-thought and drops the glossary, the check, the three questions and the closing discussion. Breaks Brian's point on minutes, and "materials ready to print and use".
5. **The support is on the wrong words, and nobody models the reading.** The glossary gives "rapt" (not in the printed passage), "imperial", "surmise" and "fantastical", and "imperial" is on the handout but not on slide 5. Students get no help with "earnest of success", "supernatural soliciting", "seated heart", "single state of man" or "function is smother'd in surmise", which are the phrases that stop a struggling reader. The teacher reads twice and then pairs start; no one shows how to work one clause. Breaks R5, scaffolding, and worked examples (I do, we do, you do).

Smaller: the closing asks whether Macbeth thinks he is captain of his destiny, and the lines just after the handout's passage ("If chance will have me king, why, chance may crown me / Without my stir"; "Come what come may") are his answer; they are not printed (R12 part 4). In the chat reply, the sentence "And here is the slide deck:" is followed by notes and a question before the deck appears. In the stack the handout shows as a 0-minute row tagged "Read aloud", and the print panel says "4 pages" where the PDF has 6. Line 15 of the aside is in italics on the handout for no reason. Slide 8's source line reads "p. Act 1, Scene 3".

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

Checked against my own knowledge of the play. The aside and the three greetings are exact; I am highly confident of that.

- **"knocking at his ribs"** in quotation marks (plan question 2; handout page 3). The text is "knock at my ribs". Not an exact quotation.
- **"Macbeth says nothing to correct him"** (question 3). False; see problem 1. High confidence.
- **"smothered in surmise"** in quotation marks (slide 5, and "Macbeth's mind is"). The text is "function is smother'd in surmise". Minor.
- **Handout heading "lines 48-50, 65-68".** Lines 65 to 68 are the witches' words to Banquo ("Lesser than Macbeth, and greater..."), which the handout does not print. Fairly confident; numbering varies by edition.
- **"Duncan is about to award it"** (plan step 2). Duncan gave the order in Act 1, Scene 2 ("with his former title greet Macbeth"); Ross is delivering it. Minor.
- **Exit ticket 1, "imagining murder before the witches suggest it".** The witches never suggest it, which is the lesson's own point. Loose wording.
- **Slide 1 notes** describe a wave that is not on the slide.

## 6. Fixes (to the planner)

1. **Check every question and direction against the printed excerpt.** Any word in quotation marks, and any line the read-aloud step names, must be inside the passage on the handout; if it is not, extend the passage or drop the reference. Here that would have added Banquo's "rapt" line and Macbeth's reply, and caught "knocking at his ribs". (R9, R11)
2. **A reading slide always carries the lines or says where they are** ("on your handout, top of page 2"). On a marked passage, do not color the phrases the tasks ask students to find; reveal the marks on a second step. (R9, checking for understanding)
3. **When a picture is not found, put a labeled slot on the slide, and keep a small checked set of pictures for the most-taught authors and scenes** so Shakespeare's portrait can never come back "not found". For a scene lesson, look first for a picture of the scene itself. (R4, R7, R10, R13)
4. **Build the deck's minutes from the plan's steps, part by part,** and make any minutes spoken in directions match the slide. (Brian: minutes add up)
5. **Assemble handouts as a teacher would print them:** no prompt printed twice, a question stays with its answer lines, no nearly empty pages; and print the lesson plan's steps in full. (ready to print and use)
6. **Choose glossary words from the printed passage by difficulty for the grade,** and put the same list on the define slide and the handout. Add one modeled clause before pair work. (R5, scaffolding, worked examples)
