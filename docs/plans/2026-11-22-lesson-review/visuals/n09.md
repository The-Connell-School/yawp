## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** The four moves are there in order: a starter in the students' own lives ($10,000 that belongs to your whole family), a 5-minute inventory, short direct teaching with key terms, then text and a discussion on the big question. The starter is never explicitly brought back, and the direct teaching answers the lesson's question before the reading (see problem 2).
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R10, R11): 1.** The handout's eight "excerpts" (page 4) are printed in quotation marks under each character's name; by my reading two are invented, one is from Act 2, one carries a stray editing note, and one says nothing about the check (part 5). The planner's accuracy check matched no source text (`"work": "none"`, 0 quotations verified). No line appears on any slide and no excerpt has an act, scene or page reference.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 2.** The vocabulary slide (slide 6) is good: three well-chosen words, plain definitions, each tied to a character. But slide 4 dates the Great Migration to the "1930s" (the planner flagged this itself and left it on the slide), slide 11 says "Next time: Act 2, and the check arrives" (it arrives in Act 1, Scene 2), and slide 4's notes misstate the 1948 ruling.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R2, R3, R4, R6, R7, R13): 2.** Three of five content slides are words only (slides 3, 4, 6). Slide 4 is a list of four dates, not a timeline. The map (slide 5) shows the Great Lakes with one dot for Chicago and tells students the second place is "not on the map". The only picture in the deck is the author on the title slide; there is nothing of the South Side, a kitchenette apartment, or the 1959 production.
- **E. Writing: 3.** The starter and the Short Response are good prompts and fit the objective ("what does getting it represent for them beyond the money itself? Use a specific moment or line"). Solo writing is really 12 minutes (24%), not the 54% the stack shows; the exit ticket asks for all four characters with text evidence in 2 minutes, while slide 11 asks a different, smaller question.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** A 3-minute quick check where each pair reports one character onto a board chart, a ready follow-up ("What does getting that thing actually give them?"), 60 seconds of silent think time before discussion, and an exit ticket with a sharp watch-for note.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** Totals are 50 everywhere, but slide 8 tells students "21 minutes" for a step the plan gives 15, there is no slide for the class starter, and the main worksheet is not ready to print: the chart is one line high with no columns and breaks across pages 4 and 5, three literal "[__________]" strings are printed on page 5, and page 2 is a stray instruction page ("Use the handout your teacher gives you").
- **H. Teacher usability: 2.** Kind, on the teacher's side, three starters, homework option, useful think-time and push questions. But only one exit ticket is offered (Brian asks for three), slide 5's notes tell the teacher to "point out how close these two places are on a map" when the map shows one of them, and the modelled think-aloud (plan step 4) is built on a line Walter does not say.
- **I. Access: 3.** Vocabulary taught before reading with examples from the play, short excerpts, a chart organizer, a discussion stem ("I think _____ has the strongest claim because..."), teacher modelling. "Meet the notes" in Mama's excerpt is not glossed, and there are no frames for the Short Response.
- **Overall: 2.** The design is sensible and the prompts are good, but a teacher cannot hand out the excerpt sheet as it is, and the deck gives away the answers and has two errors on screen. It needs rebuilding against the book before use.

## 3. Strengths

1. **The vocabulary slide does what R5 and R8 ask.** Slide 6 defines insurance check, deed and assimilationist in plain words before students read, and ties each to the play ("Big Walter paid into his policy for years; now $10,000 goes to Lena").
2. **The central distinction is the right one and is taught, not just hoped for.** Want versus "the feeling or need underneath it" runs through the chart, the modelled think-aloud, the circulating question ("What does getting that thing actually give them?"), the Short Response and the exit ticket's watch-for ("A student who writes 'Walter wants $10,000' has not yet gotten there", slide 11 notes).
3. **The discussion is well built.** Sixty seconds of silent think time, a stem on screen, and two follow-up pushes that complicate first answers ("Is there a way to give everyone what they want, or does someone have to lose?").

## 4. Problems

1. **The handout presents invented and misplaced lines as Hansberry's words.** Handout page 4 gives, in quotation marks: for Walter, "A man needs a business of his own… I want to be able to hand something over to my son"; for Ruth, "I would just like to know if you, if I could have… I mean, is it possible that we could just move? Be somewhere different for once?" Neither is in the play as far as I can tell. Mama's "I just seen my family falling apart today…" is from Act 2, Scene 1, said after she has bought the house, so it is both outside Act 1 and a spoiler. Beneatha's second line prints an editing note to students: `"I want to be a doctor." (Act 1, Scene 1, Beneatha says she will be a doctor.)` The chat note says the excerpts are "paraphrased closely" and, in the next sentence, "brief quotations". Students will quote these in the Short Response as evidence. Breaks R11 and R10 (a substitute was made quietly: the page itself gives no sign).
2. **The deck gives the answers before the reading.** The objective is to "identify what each member of the Younger family wants"; slide 7 tells them (a house with a garden, a business of his own, stability and peace, medical school) one slide before the close reading whose chart asks "What do they want from the money?" The 15-minute task becomes copying. Ruth's entry, "Stability and peace", is the planner's own label, not something Act 1 shows; in Act 1 Ruth argues Walter's case to Mama, and her position is the most interesting one to find. Breaks objective and alignment, and close reading.
3. **Errors on screen that the planner already knew about or should have caught.** Slide 4: "1930s Great Migration brings families north" (it began around 1916; the planner's own "Check before class" says so and the slide is unchanged). Slide 11: "Next time: Act 2, and the check arrives" (the check arrives in Act 1, Scene 2; slide 3 of the same deck says it "arrives"). Slide 4 notes: the 1948 ruling "made it legal to buy in white neighborhoods" (Shelley v. Kraemer stopped courts enforcing covenants; buying was not illegal before). Breaks R11.
4. **The main worksheet cannot be handed out as printed.** The chart on pages 4 and 5 has rows one line tall, no column rules, and splits after the first row; three "[__________]" placeholders print literally under "One more question"; page 2 is an instruction page telling students to "use the handout your teacher gives you" followed by 20 empty lines; the packet runs 7 pages for about 2 pages of content. Breaks Yawp!'s own value of materials ready to print and use.
5. **Context slides give students almost nothing to see.** Slide 4 is a list of dates (R3 says never), slide 3 is a sentence, and the map is drawn at a scale where the play's whole geography (a South Side apartment and a white neighborhood a few miles away) is one dot, while the notes tell the teacher to point at two places. Both missing pictures are reported only in the speaker notes. No photograph of the South Side, a kitchenette building, or the 1959 cast appears. Breaks R2, R3, R6, R7, R13.

Smaller things: the request says "each member"; Travis is left out without a word. Slide 8 shows "21 minutes" against the plan's 15. There is no class starter slide. Slide 11 ("Name two Younger family members…") and the Yawp! exit ticket ("explain what each Younger family member wants… point to what in the text") are different tasks. Only one exit ticket is offered. The stack counts the pair chart as 15 minutes of writing, which is how it reaches 54%. Slide 6's example for "assimilationist" names George Murchison (Act 2); in Act 1, Scene 2 the word comes up with Asagai. "Meet the notes" is not glossed.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- Walter, "A man needs a business of his own… I want to be able to hand something over to my son": not a line from the play (fairly confident; the nearest thing is Walter's "I hand you the world" to Travis in Act 2). Also labelled "Later, to Ruth", though his talk with Ruth comes before the "I want so many things" speech to Mama.
- Ruth, "is it possible that we could just move? Be somewhere different for once?": not a line from the play (confident).
- Mama, "I just seen my family falling apart today… just falling to pieces in front of my eyes": genuine, but Act 2, Scene 1, not Act 1 (confident).
- Beneatha, "I want to be a doctor": not her wording (she says she is going to be a doctor); printed with a stray note.
- Mama, "I been thinking that we maybe could meet the notes…": the play has "Been thinking…"; otherwise right.
- Exact as far as I can check: Walter's "I want so many things that they are driving me kind of crazy… Mama, look at me" (Act 1, Scene 2); Beneatha's "That money belongs to Mama, Walter, and it's for her to decide how she wants to use it"; Ruth's "Eat your eggs, Walter" (which is not evidence of what she wants from the check).
- Slide 4: Great Migration dated "1930s". Slide 11: the check said to arrive in Act 2. Slide 4 notes: 1948 ruling misdescribed.
- Plan step 5 and slide 9 notes, "Lena says the money is her husband's life": a paraphrase, not her line; acceptable only if the teacher knows that.
- Right: $10,000; Big Walter's policy; Walter a chauffeur; Chicago's South Side; 1919 riots; 1948 covenant ruling's year; 1959 Broadway opening; Clybourne Park as the white neighborhood (though Mama names it only in Act 2).
- Confidence: high on the misplaced and invented lines, moderate on exact wording of the genuine ones (I am working from memory of the play, not a copy).

## 6. Fixes for the planner

1. **When no source text is matched for a work, the planner must not write lines in quotation marks.** Give a labelled slot instead ("Walter to Mama, Act 1, Scene 2, the speech that begins 'I want so many things…', page ___ in your edition") and say on the handout that students read from their books. A paraphrase must be marked as one. (R10, R11)
2. **Never print on a slide the answer the reading task asks students to find.** The cast diagram should give names and relationships and leave the "wants" for the chart. (Objective and alignment)
3. **A fact the checker flags must be corrected on the slide or the slide withheld, not footnoted in chat**; and check "next time" lines against the act being taught. (R11)
4. **Fix the handout renderer for tables and placeholders**: ruled columns, rows tall enough to write in, the chart kept on one page, no literal "[____]", and no auto-made page for a step that already has its handout. (Materials ready to print)
5. **Context slides need the thing itself**: draw dates as a timeline (R3); draw maps country first, then city, at a scale that shows the places named (R2); when a picture search fails, fall back to known public-domain sets from the work's world (for this play, the 1941 Farm Security Administration photographs of South Side Chicago) and put a visible labelled slot on the slide when nothing is found. Also keep slide minutes equal to the plan's step minutes and offer three exit tickets every time. (R2, R3, R6, R7, R10; Brian)
