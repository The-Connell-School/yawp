## 3. Strengths

1. **The opening pair of moves.** Listing what students "already know about Africa" and where it came from, keeping the list on the board, and returning to it after teaching Conrad is exactly what a day 1 should do (R12 parts 1 and 2), and the starter about a rule that makes sense from the inside sets up the handout well.
2. **Where and when are shown, and right.** Slide 4 maps Nigeria and the southeast; slide 5 is a real timeline with five correct events, and the notes use 1958 against 1960 to make a point (R2, R3).
3. **The last move.** Reading "Okonkwo was well known throughout the nine villages and even beyond" and asking what "solid personal achievements" already says about this society ties the context to the book in the last five minutes (R8), with the words exactly right.

## 4. Problems, most serious first

1. **The lesson teaches things about the novel that are wrong.** Slide 8 and handout item 4 say the earth goddess Ani's "priestess" held real authority; in the novel Ani's priest is Ezeani, a man, and the priestess is Chielo, who serves Agbala. Slide 7 and handout item 3 say the Oracle settled major disputes; in the novel the egwugwu and the elders do that (chapter 10). Slide 10 puts "a thoroughgoing racist book" in quotation marks as Achebe's words; he called Conrad "a thoroughgoing racist" and the book "offensive and deplorable". Students will meet Ezeani in chapter 4 and the trial in chapter 10 and find their notes contradict the book. Breaks R11.
2. **Igbo society is never shown.** Slides 6, 7, 8 and 10 are sentences on a plain background. The request was "Igbo society before the colonizers", and R1 asks what it looked like. The planner did ask for the right pictures and says in the notes that none were found (R10 is honored in the notes, though the slide itself has no labeled slot), but the result is a context day without the context's pictures. Breaks R6, R7, R13.
3. **None of the book's words are taught, and its people and places are barely named.** No slide defines obi, egwugwu, chi, kola, title or clan; Umuofia is never named; Okonkwo first appears on slide 15 of 16. A 10th grader opens chapter 1 tonight and meets all of them cold. Breaks R5, R4, R8.
4. **"Who Achebe was writing back to" is told, not shown.** There is no passage from Heart of Darkness (or Joyce Cary), no picture of Conrad or his book, and students are asked what Conrad "never did" on the teacher's word alone. Two sentences of Conrad beside Achebe's two sentences would have been the lesson. Breaks R7, R9 and the principle that discussion needs evidence from a text.
5. **No exit ticket, and very little writing.** The closing is one sentence of reaction; slide 16 is a homework reminder. Nothing checks the objective, there are no three exit options (Brian), and the stack shows 10% writing (Yawp!'s value that the exit ticket is a writing assignment; alignment of task to objective).

Smaller, but a teacher would notice: the garbled copyright sentence in the reply; "Picture to add: Chinua Achebe" under a slide that shows him; the map legend's "not on the map"; the printable plan cut off mid-step; the handout stranded at minute 50 in the stack; the plan glosses obi as "compound" (it is the man's own hut inside the compound).

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **Ani's "priestess"** (slide 8, handout item 4): wrong for the novel. Ezeani, priest of Ani, is a man (chapter 4). High confidence.
- **"The Oracle … settled major disputes"** (slide 7, handout item 3): in the novel the Oracle rules on war and on Ikemefuna; disputes go to the egwugwu. Fairly confident.
- **"Achebe called it 'a thoroughgoing racist book'"** (slide 10, plan step 3): not his words. In "An Image of Africa" he calls Conrad "a thoroughgoing racist" (first printed as "a bloody racist") and the book "offensive and deplorable". Also from 1975, long after 1958. Fairly confident.
- **"Ani was the most powerful deity"** (slide 8, handout): overstated. The novel says she "played a greater part in the life of the people than any other deity"; Chukwu is the supreme god. Fairly confident.
- **"The obi (compound)"** (plan step 3): the obi is the man's hut within the compound. High confidence.
- Correct as given: the first two sentences of the novel (slide 15); the Yeats line; 1900, 1914, 1958, 1960; Achebe 1930 to 2013, raised in Ogidi; Chielo as priestess of Agbala; cocoyam as a women's crop.

The planner's own checks report 6 facts flagged and "repaired" and 1 corrected; none of the errors above was caught, and nothing was passed to the teacher.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Check claims about a novel's world against the novel, not only against general knowledge.** For a copyrighted book, hold a short verified fact sheet (who is priest of what, who judges, what the key words mean) and refuse to print a named role that is not on it. (R11)
2. **A context day must carry a "words of this book" slide and name the protagonist and the setting early.** Make the define slide and a "who you will meet" slide required for day 1 of a novel, filled from the same fact sheet. (R5, R4, R8)
3. **When pictures cannot be found, change the slide, do not just leave it bare.** Put a visible labeled slot on the slide, and fall back to something Yawp! can draw: a diagram of the compound or of who holds authority, or a public domain passage. For "writing back to", the fallback is the other text itself, quoted beside this one. (R7, R10, R13)
4. **Every lesson ends with an exit ticket with three options, and the closing slide shows the exit question.** Do not let a context day skip it; count handout writing in the stack's writing share. (Brian; alignment)
5. **Run a last consistency pass over what the teacher reads:** no "picture to add" note on a slide that has its picture, no "not on the map" for the shaded country, no truncated steps in the printable plan, the handout placed with its activity in the stack, and the reply's own notes re-read for sense.
