## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** Opens in the students' own experience (someone who tells a story about themselves and leaves something out), takes a 3-minute inventory, teaches briefly, then reads closely and argues. The fourth move is only half done: the teacher promises "we're going to come back to exactly that idea when we read Nick" (plan step 1, slide 3 notes) and nothing in the discussion, the Short Response or the exit ticket ever sets the students' starter answers beside Nick.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 3.** The real opening is printed in full on the handout (page 2) and excerpted accurately on slides 6 and 7, and the reading is specific ("snobbishly" twice, "unjustly accused"). - **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 3.** Slide 4's three facts are right and short; slide 9 defines "unreliable narrator" clearly and usefully ("not a liar"). - **E. Writing: 3.** 15 minutes, 30%. The starter fits the theme. The Short Response and the exit ticket ask nearly the same question back to back, the exit ticket gets 2 minutes, and the Short Response prompt states the answer before students write ("telling us he doesn't judge people, and then immediately judges them").
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** Pair annotation with a share-out, a pair talk with a stem (slide 8), a group sort with a spokesperson (slide 10), then the exit ticket with a useful "mix-ups to watch for" note. No cold check that students understood the literal sense of the passage before analysing it.
- **G. Feasibility: 3.** Plan, deck and stack all total 50. The agenda slide (slide 2) prints "3 minutes" beside "Class starter, what students already know" (it is 8), so the agenda on screen adds up to 45. The printable lesson plan cuts each step to its first sentence: the 15-minute close reading reads only "Distribute the close-reading handout (below)."
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** Real options (three starters, three exit tickets, "in class or as homework", "you don't have to use all of it"), one closing question, and speaker notes that give actual teacher talk (slide 6's modelled annotation, slide 10's push question). Marked down for notes that send the teacher listening for quotations students cannot find, and for "The stem is on the handout too" (slide 8 notes; it is not).
- **I. Access: 2.** One sentence stem on a slide and a modelled annotation. No glossary, no chunking or line numbers on a dense 190-word paragraph of 1925 prose, no frames on the handout or for the Short Response, nothing for English learners.
- **Overall: 3.** A strong teacher would use the skeleton and the handout tomorrow, after correcting the answer key, adding a glossary and a map, and extending the passage by one paragraph.

## 3. Strengths

1. **The passage is real, whole and exact.** The handout prints the novel's first three paragraphs word for word (I checked them against the text), and slides 6 and 7 show excerpts with honest ellipses and the key phrases highlighted in colour.
2. **The close reading is specific and modelled.** Slide 6's notes give the teacher a think-aloud on "younger and more vulnerable" before students annotate (I do, then you do), and slide 7 sends students to "snobbishly" twice in one sentence and to "unjustly accused", which is where an experienced reader would send them.
3. **Slide 9's definition is better than most textbooks'.** "An unreliable narrator is not a liar. They are someone whose account is shaped by who they are, and who they need to be," followed by "Nick may believe everything he tells us. Does that make him trustworthy?" heads off the commonest student mistake, and the exit ticket's watch-for note names it too.

## 4. Problems

1. **The answer key points at words that are not in the reading.** Plan step 4 ("Share out") tells the teacher the richest finds are usually "one of the few honest people" and "I'm one of those men"; slide 8's notes say "Listen for: 'one of the few honest people'"; slide 10's notes and plan step 5 have the teacher say "He says he's honest"; the first exit ticket's main points list "the claim to rare honesty". The first phrase is from chapter 3; the second Nick does not say in this passage at all. The planner's own check caught the first one and printed "Check before class: 'one of the few honest people' is in Chapter 3", then left it in four places. A teacher who trusts the notes will prompt students for evidence they cannot find, and a class that has not read chapter 3 gets a spoiler presented as today's text. Breaks R11 and discussion that requires evidence from the text.
2. **No vocabulary support for a hard 1925 paragraph.** Handout page 2 gives 11th graders "privy to the secret griefs of wild, unknown men", "feigned sleep, preoccupation, or a hostile levity", "plagiaristic and marred by obvious suppressions", "parcelled out unequally" in one unbroken block, with no glosses, no line numbers and no chunking, and no slide defines any of them. Students who cannot parse the literal sense cannot judge the narrator's tone; English learners and struggling readers are shut out of the main task. Breaks R5 and scaffolding and access.
3. **The debate is rigged, and the passage stops just before the best evidence.** The objective promises "a case for or against trusting him", but slide 5 ("he needs us to believe him"), the Short Response prompt ("telling us he doesn't judge people, and then immediately judges them") and every "listen for" in the notes are on one side, and the teacher is given no example of evidence that Nick is trustworthy. The very next paragraph of the novel has Nick say "after boasting this way of my tolerance, I come to the admission that it has a limit", which is the strongest evidence for both sides, and the request asked for "the opening pages". Breaks objective and alignment, and discussion that requires evidence.
4. **The context slide teaches a place with no map, and two content slides have nothing to look at.** Slide 4 names East Egg and West Egg as text on a blue panel; the missing map is mentioned only in the speaker notes, so the projected slide shows no slot and a teacher who does not open the notes never learns one was wanted. Slide 9 is words only. Breaks R2, R7, R13, and R10 in spirit (the gap is labelled, but not where it shows).
5. **The opening question is never returned to, and the last ten minutes ask the same thing twice.** The starter's "someone who leaves something out" is promised a return and gets none (R12 step 4). Then the 8-minute Short Response ("what does that moment show about the kind of narrator he is?") is followed at once by a 2-minute exit ticket ("explain what Nick's opening self-portrait reveals about his reliability… point to what in the text"), which is the same task with less time. The exit ticket therefore tells the teacher nothing new. Breaks R12 and clear objective and alignment.

Smaller things: the starter's title on the handout and stack is "Someone who sees themselves clearly", the opposite of its prompt; the second starter option garbles its quotation (`Nick's father told him: "Whenever you feel like criticizing anyone," he told me, "just remember…"`); slide credit lines read "p. Chapter 1"; plan step 4 calls the passage "the first two paragraphs" (it is three); handout tasks 1 and 2 (underline, circle) are followed by blank writing lines with nothing to write; task 4's question sits at the foot of page 2 with its lines on page 3; the stack's print panel says "4 pages" for a 5-page packet.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- "one of the few honest people" offered as a find in the chapter 1 passage (plan step 4, slide 8 notes): it is chapter 3 ("I am one of the few honest people that I have ever known"). Flagged by the planner but not removed.
- "I'm one of those men" (plan step 4) presented as Nick's words in the opening: not in the passage. The nearest thing in chapter 1 is Nick on Tom, "one of those men who reach such an acute limited excellence at twenty-one".
- Plan step 4's model think-aloud puts a paraphrase in quotation marks: "My father gave me some advice, and I've been turning it over ever since."
- "the first two paragraphs of chapter 1": the printed passage is the first three.
- Everything on the slides and handout that students see is exact: the handout passage, slide 6 and slide 7 match the text (British "judgements" on the handout, "judgments" on slide 7; a spelling variant only). Context facts (East Egg and West Egg, Prohibition, Nick from the Midwest, Yale, the bond business) are right. Confidence: high; I know these paragraphs closely.

## 6. Fixes for the planner

1. **When the accuracy check finds a quotation outside the lesson's passage, remove or replace it everywhere, not just list it under "Check before class".** A misplaced quotation in teacher notes should block or be repaired in plan, notes and exit-ticket keys together. (R11)
2. **Every "listen for" and "richest finds" list must be drawn from the passage the planner itself printed**, and for a "for or against" question must give at least one piece of evidence on each side. (Discussion with evidence; objective and alignment)
3. **Any printed passage gets a short glossary (4 to 8 words, chosen for the grade) on the handout and on the excerpt slide, plus line numbers or paragraph breaks.** (R5; scaffolding and access)
4. **When a wanted picture is not found, put a visible labelled slot on the slide itself ("Map of Long Island's North Shore: add here"), and for places try Yawp!'s own drawn map before giving up.** A definition slide should get a diagram or a marked example sentence rather than nothing. (R2, R7, R10, R13)
5. **Do not let the Short Response and the exit ticket ask the same question; make the closing writing return to the class starter** ("Look back at the person you wrote about at the start. How is Nick like them, or not?"). Also make the agenda slide's minutes come from the same numbers as the plan, and keep the full step text in the printable plan. (R12; Brian: a deck's minutes must add up)
