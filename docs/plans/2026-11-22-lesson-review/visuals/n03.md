## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** Opens in the students' experience (something frightening that felt calm), teaches briefly, reads twice, models, practises, writes. The return to the opening is implied rather than made: slide 1's notes promise "you will come back to them at the end", and the closing question ("If Death had been rude and terrifying in this poem, what would we have lost?") is good, but nobody is asked to look back at what they wrote in the starter.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 3.** The whole poem is on handout page 2 in the scholarly text (dashes, capitals), numbered every fifth line, and I found it exact. The reading is specific to words ("kindly", "Civility", "Gossamer", "Tulle", "Or rather – He passed Us"). But slide 7 projects stanzas 1 and 2 in the 1890 editors' version (commas, a semicolon, lower-case "his civility"), which is not the text in students' hands and is the very regularising the plan's own Frame 3 tells teachers was undone.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 3.** Accurate and short; the three context facts on slide 4 are right. "Conceit", "gentleman caller", "personification" and "chaperone" are explained in the plan's script but never appear on a slide, and the poem's hard nouns (Gossamer, Tippet, Tulle, Cornice, surmised) are defined nowhere students can see.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R4, R6, R7, R13): 3.** Three of five content slides have something true to look at: the Dickinson daguerreotype (slide 4), a marked passage (slide 7), and a diagram of the journey as a life (slide 8) that genuinely teaches the poem's structure. Slides 5 and 9 are words only, nothing shows the lesson's central image (a caller, a carriage, a tippet), and slide 4's credit reads "s3.amazonaws.com, Black-white photograph of…".
- **E. Writing: 3.** Good amount (starter, framed pair sentences, an 8-minute Short Response, exit ticket) and all on the objective. But the pair task (handout Part 3), the Short Response and the first exit ticket are one question asked three times in 20 minutes, and the handout prints the Short Response twice (page 3 Part 4 and page 4).
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** A share after the first read with named look-fors, a three-item list after the second read, circulating with a stated insight to listen for (slide 10 notes), a share-out with a stem, an exit ticket with a useful watch-for. The share-out is only 2 minutes for a class of pairs.
- **G. Feasibility: 3.** Plan, deck and stack all total 50. Per-step minutes differ between plan and deck (read aloud 8 in the plan, 4 on slide 6; pair work 10 in the plan, "13 minutes" on slide 10), the agenda slide shows 7 minutes for "Class starter, context" (it is 11) so the agenda adds to 46, and the handout's title is printed cut off: "How Dickinson makes Death a ge…".
- **H. Teacher usability: 4.** The best of the options I saw: three starters, three context framings each with its own board question and advice on which pair well, three exit tickets with a sentence on what each one tells you, a homework-free plan that still fits, one closing question. Speaker notes give real teacher talk (slide 7, slide 8's "Or rather" question, slide 11's "why is polite scarier than violent?").
- **I. Access: 3.** Teacher reads aloud, two reads with different jobs, line numbers, sentence starters on the handout and a stem on slide 11, a worked example on slide 9. No glossary for the archaic words, which is the first thing a struggling reader or English learner needs with this poem.
- **Overall: 3.** A high 3. A strong teacher would use the handout and most of the deck tomorrow, after adding a glossary, swapping slide 7's text for the handout's, and holding back some of the stanza-by-stanza explanation so the pairs have something left to find.

## 3. Strengths

1. **The handout is the real poem, done properly.** Page 2 gives all six stanzas in the text scholars use, with Dickinson's dashes and capitals and line numbers, followed by tasks that step up: circle, underline and list three, framed pair sentences, then the Short Response.
2. **The "analysis move" is a worked example students can copy** (slide 9 and plan step 4): name the word, say what it means in the social world, say what it means in the death world, explain the gap. The handout's starters ("Dickinson's word '____' belongs to the world of ____, where it means ____. In the context of death, it suggests ____") carry the same move into their own writing.
3. **The context step gives a teacher real choices.** Three framings (historical, literary, the author), each two or three minutes and each ending in a different question to leave on the board, with advice that the first and third pair well. Slide 8's diagram and its note on "Or rather – He passed Us" are what an experienced reader of this poem would want.

## 4. Problems

1. **The teacher does the interpreting before the students get a turn.** Plan step 4 walks all six stanzas and tells the class what each key phrase means: "kindly stopped" (he is doing her a favor), "His Civility" (Death has manners), "Gossamer" and "Tulle" (a party dress, she is underdressed for eternity), "Centuries" (she is already in the afterlife). Step 5 then offers pairs exactly those four phrases to "analyse". With slide 9's model on top, the pair task and the Short Response become writing down what was just said. Students practise the frame, not the reading. Breaks worked examples and modeling (I do, we do, you do needs a "you do" on fresh material) and close reading.
2. **The hard words are not defined anywhere students can see.** Gossamer, Tippet, Tulle, Cornice, surmised and Civility are not glossed on the handout or any slide, and "conceit", "gentleman caller", "chaperone" and "personification" (which the objective and the second exit ticket depend on) never appear on screen. A pair that picks "Gossamer and Tulle" has only what they caught of the teacher's sentence. Breaks R5 and scaffolding and access.
3. **The slide and the handout show two different texts of the poem.** Slide 7: "He kindly stopped for me; / The carriage held but just ourselves… For his civility." Handout: "He kindly stopped for me – / The Carriage held but just Ourselves –… For His Civility –". The notes and the annotate task say "Civility" with a capital that is not on the slide. Plan Frame 3 tells teachers the first editors "changed her dashes to commas" and that today's text is "as close to her hand as scholarship can get", then the deck projects the editors' version without saying so. Breaks R11 (and next lesson is on "the dash").
4. **One question is asked three times, and the exit slide asks a fourth.** Handout Part 3 (pairs), the Short Response (slide 12, and printed twice on the handout) and the first exit ticket all ask students to take a word from the poem and say what the gentleman-caller reading does. Slide 13 is headed "Exit ticket: open it in Yawp!" but shows the oral closing question, which is not what the ticket asks. The teacher learns little from the third pass and students see repetition. Breaks objective and alignment; Brian's "no rabbit holes".
5. **The lesson's central picture is never shown.** The request is about Death as a gentleman caller; no slide shows what a caller, a courting carriage ride, or a tippet and tulle gown looked like in 1860s New England, though the reading depends on students picturing them. Slide 5 (big idea) and slide 9 are words only; the missing manuscript is reported only in the notes; slide 4's portrait is credited to a web host. Breaks R6, R7, R13, and R11 (every picture credited).

Smaller things: the truncated title "How Dickinson makes Death a ge…" prints at the top of the handout, the deck export and the stack. Slide 10 says "13 minutes" and slide 6 implies 4 for steps the plan gives 10 and 8. The agenda's first line shows 7 minutes for two steps that take 11. Slide 12's notes send the teacher to "the compare slide", which is the planner's name for a layout, not anything a teacher sees. Slide 8 has "Children playing at recess" where the poem's word is "strove". The share-out gets 2 minutes.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- Handout poem: exact against the standard text as I know it (one capital differs: "chill" for "Chill" in line 14). Confidence: high.
- Slide 7: an accurate transcription of the 1890 first-edition wording of stanzas 1 and 2, so not misquoted, but a different edition from the handout and unlabelled.
- Dates and facts: written about 1863, first published 1890, 1830–1886, nearly 1,800 poems with almost none published in her lifetime, fascicles, Lavinia finding the poems, editors regularising punctuation and rhyme: all right.
- Plan Frame 1, "Funerals were held in the home. Dickinson attended many": doubtful as stated; as an adult she largely stayed away, famously listening to her father's funeral from her room. Low stakes, teacher-facing only. Moderate confidence.
- Slide 4 picture credit ("s3.amazonaws.com…") is not a credit.
- Interpretive claims (Immortality as chaperone, the journey as the stages of life, the house as the grave) are standard readings, presented a little too much as fact.

## 6. Fixes for the planner

1. **In a close-reading lesson, model on one phrase and give students different phrases to work on**; the planner should check that the phrases it hands to pairs are not ones its own mini-lesson has already explained. (Worked examples and modeling; close reading)
2. **Any poem or passage handout gets a glossary of the words a student of that grade will not know, and every key term in the objective gets a define slide** before first use. (R5; access)
3. **Excerpt slides must be cut from the same source text as the handout**, character for character, so highlights and capitals match. (R11)
4. **The pair task, the Short Response and the exit ticket should be three different steps up, not one prompt reworded; the exit slide must show the exit ticket's actual prompt; print each prompt once.** (Objective and alignment)
5. **Look for a picture of the central image, not only the author and her house** (here: a period engraving of a carriage call, a tippet), put a visible labelled slot on the slide when a wanted picture is not found, and build credits from the holding institution rather than the file's web host. Also fix the agenda slide's minutes for merged steps and stop truncating titles on printed pages. (R6, R7, R10, R11, R13; Brian: minutes must add up)
