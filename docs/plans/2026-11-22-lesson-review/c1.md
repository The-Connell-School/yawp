# c1: The Crucible, day 1 (context day)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Build day 1 of a unit on The Crucible for 11th grade, 50 minutes: Salem in 1692 and why Arthur Miller wrote about it in 1953. Include the slide deck."

**What came back:** A chat plan of six steps that adds to 50 minutes: Class starter (5, three library prompts to pick from), Salem in 1692 (15), Arthur Miller in 1953 (10), The connection (10, pairs then whole class), Short Response "Why 1692?" (8), Closing (2, one spoken question), plus three exit-ticket options (explain the allegory, check Salem as a social event, open "wondering" reflection). The deck has 16 slides whose minutes add to 41: title, agenda, a map, "Puritan Salem", a 1692 timeline, a big idea, a "who gets accused" discussion, an "allegory" definition, "America 1950–1954" with a McCarthy photo, a McCarthy/Miller timeline, a Miller person slide, a big idea that reuses the Miller photo, a compare slide, a pairs discussion, the 8-minute write, and an exit slide. There was no class-starter slide. The handout (7 pages, built even though no handout was asked for) has a Quick Write page whose prompts differ from the plan's, one page for each of the three starters, the Short Response twice (pages 5 and 6), and the "wondering" exit ticket. After "Add everything" the stack showed **80 minutes planned for a 50-minute lesson** and "54% of class is writing (43 min)". It held four class-starter rows (20 min), two Short Response rows (8 + 10 min), a closing and one exit ticket. The printable plan PDF is 3 pages.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 2.** It opens with a personal writing prompt and then teaches directly, and that part works. But the starters are generic library prompts ("There's no such thing as a harmless lie"), and the only check of prior knowledge is a show of hands about McCarthy on slide 9. The Salem history gets none. The lesson never returns to the opening question: step 4 sets 1692 beside 1953, not the play beside the students' own answers.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** Not a word of *The Crucible* and nothing from Miller's own writing about it. The obvious short texts would be his "A Note on the Historical Accuracy of This Play" or the Act 1 overture. The only quotation, "The Crucible was an act of desperation" (slide 11), has no source.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 2.** The pieces are short and punchy (slides 4, 6, 9). But several facts are wrong (see section 5), and only one term is defined: *allegory* (slide 8), with a narrow definition. *Spectral evidence*, *inadmissible*, *HUAC*, *Communist*, *censured*, *contempt of Congress*, *Puritan*, *pressed to death* and *enter a plea* are all used and never defined.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R2, R3, R4, R6, R7, R13): 2.** The two timelines (slides 5 and 10) properly meet R3, and McCarthy and Miller have faces. But the map on slide 3 shades the whole modern United States, Alaska and Hawaii included, and labels it "Massachusetts Bay Colony, 1692". "Puritan Salem" (slide 4) is an empty navy panel, with no picture and no labeled slot. The Miller photo is used twice (slides 11 and 12). Nothing shows Salem itself: no meetinghouse, no trial record, no 1692 broadside.
- **E. Writing: 3.** The 8-minute "Why 1692?" Short Response matches the objective exactly, and so does exit ticket option 1. But the stack kept only the "wondering" exit ticket, which does not test the objective.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** There are good live checks: the "who gets accused?" question (slide 7, with a stem), the pairs talk with notes on what to listen for (slide 14), and circulating during the write (slide 15). There is no quick factual check, such as a two-question hinge, on the history before students are asked to connect it.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The chat plan adds to 50, but the deck adds to 41 and has no starter slide. The plan's slide references are wrong: it says the compare slide is slide 15, but it is slide 13. The stack is 80 minutes. The printed plan says "Students will… Add this lesson's goals" instead of the objective. It drops every key teaching point and garbles step 6 ("…once they know Salem. S. government, Hollywood, and universities.").
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** It gives real options: three starters, three exit tickets, and "send it home if the pairs run long". The speaker notes are the best part, specific and in a teacher's voice ("which women?", "call on a pair who said something sharp — not the first hand up"). But the stack and the printed plan undo much of that.
- **I. Access: 2.** Discussion slides have sentence stems (slides 7 and 14), and allegory gets an example. There is no notes sheet or graphic organizer for 25 minutes of history, no vocabulary support for the legal and political terms, and no frame for the 8-minute write.
- **Overall: 2.** A strong teacher would keep the arc and the notes, but would have to fix the map, correct four facts, add images and vocabulary, and rebuild the stack and printed plan before using it.

## 3. Strengths

1. **Speaker notes that coach the teacher.** Slide 7 tells the teacher what to listen for and how to push ("If a student says 'women' — yes, and push further: which women?"). Slide 14 says to call on a pair who said something sharp rather than the first hand up. Expert, kind, on the teacher's side.
2. **A real timeline, not a list.** Slides 5 and 10 put 1692 and 1950–56 on step-through timelines, as R3 asks. The final write and exit ticket option 1 test exactly the stated objective (the Salem/1953 allegory).
3. **Options as Brian wants them.** There are three starter choices and three exit tickets (check understanding, explain a concept, ask a question). The Short Response can move to homework. The plan ends on one closing question.

## 4. Problems (most serious first)

1. **The map is wrong and teaches something false.** *Where:* slide 3. *What:* the whole modern United States (Alaska and Hawaii included) is shaded and labeled "Massachusetts Bay Colony, 1692". The "Boston" label floats over the Great Lakes, away from its pin. The notes say "then move to the closer pin", but there is no closer view. *Why it matters:* students who "cannot place Massachusetts" (the notes' own words) now picture a colony the size of the USA. It undoes the notes' point that "there is no America". *Breaks:* R2, R11.
2. **Factual errors in the direct instruction.** *Where:* slide 5 ("Oct 1692 … The trials end"), slide 10 ("1954 … The hearings end"; "1956 … His passport is revoked"; "1950 … The hearings begin"), plan step 3. *Why it matters:* this is the day students build the history they will lean on for the whole unit, and the teacher is told to "deliver the three points directly". *Breaks:* R11.
3. **The stack is 80 minutes for a 50-minute lesson, and the printed plan is unusable.** *Where:* the stack and plan.pdf. *What:* "Add everything" added four class-starter rows (an empty "Class starter" plus all three options, 20 min) and the Short Response twice (8 + 10 min). It added only the "wondering" exit ticket, not the one that tests the objective. The plan PDF has a placeholder goal, no key points, wrong slide numbers and a garbled step 6. The handout repeats the Short Response (pages 5 and 6), and its Quick Write prompts do not match the plan's starters. *Why it matters:* a teacher who trusts "Ready for class?" walks in with 30 extra minutes, two copies of one task, and a sub plan that teaches nothing. *Breaks:* Brian (minutes must add up), Yawp! values (ready to print), clear alignment. The "Add everything" button's behaviour is itself a problem: it should add one chosen option per slot, not every alternative.
4. **The history is never tied to the play's people (R8), and the arc never closes (R12).** *Where:* slide 5 names Betty Parris, Abigail Williams, Reverend Parris, Tituba and Giles Corey, all characters in *The Crucible*, without saying so. John and Elizabeth Proctor never appear. The opening question is never revisited. *Why it matters:* the point of a context day (R1) is to arrive at Act 1 already knowing who Abigail and Parris are. Here students have to make that link on their own. *Breaks:* R1, R4, R8, R12(4).
5. **Content slides with nothing to look at, terms undefined, and a reused image.** *Where:* slide 4 (empty panel), slide 13 (text only), slides 11 and 12 (same Miller photo). "Spectral evidence … inadmissible" (slide 5), "censured" and "contempt of Congress" (slide 10) are never defined. *Why it matters:* 11th graders, and English learners most of all, get the key legal ideas of the play as bare words. Dual coding is missing exactly where the ideas are most abstract. *Breaks:* R5, R6, R7, R10, R13.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **"Oct 1692 … Governor Phips dissolves the court … The trials end" (slide 5).** Phips dissolved the Court of Oyer and Terminer in late October 1692, but trials went on before the new Superior Court of Judicature from January to May 1693. Phips's general pardon/release came in May 1693. High confidence.
- **"1954 McCarthy censured … The hearings end" (slide 10).** The Senate censured McCarthy in December 1954, but HUAC was a separate House committee and kept holding hearings for years. It called Miller himself in 1956, as the next timeline point says, which contradicts "The hearings end". High confidence.
- **"1950 McCarthy's speech … The hearings begin" (slide 10).** This conflates Senator McCarthy with HUAC, a House committee that had been holding hearings since the late 1930s (the Hollywood hearings were in 1947). Slide 9 sets them side by side without making that distinction. High confidence.
- **"His passport is revoked" after the 1956 testimony (slide 10 and plan).** The State Department refused to renew Miller's passport in 1954, when he wanted to attend the Brussels production of *The Crucible*. That was before his HUAC appearance, not a result of it. Fairly high confidence.
- **"Church law and civil law are the same — no separation" (slide 4, map note, plan).** This is an overstatement. Puritan Massachusetts kept clergy out of civil office, and under the 1691 charter (in force in 1692) the vote rested on property, not church membership. Witchcraft was a capital crime under a biblically based civil code. Moderate confidence that a historian would flag it. Also, "Massachusetts Bay Colony, 1692" should be the Province of Massachusetts Bay under the new 1691 charter (minor).
- **"Jun–Sep 1692 … 19 people are hanged on Gallows Hill" (slide 5).** The count and months are right. The execution site is now identified as Proctor's Ledge (confirmed 2016), so "Gallows Hill" is dated (minor). "Over 150 jailed" is placed under Mar–May, but that is the total for the whole crisis (minor).
- **"The Crucible was an act of desperation" (slide 11).** No source is given, and I could not confirm this exact wording. It may be a paraphrase of Miller's 1996 *New Yorker* essay "Why I Wrote 'The Crucible'". Low confidence either way, so treat it as unverified.
- **Allegory definition (slide 8):** "A story set in one time and place that is really about a different time and place." That is not what allegory means (a narrative whose characters and events stand for other ideas or events). It is a usable gloss for this play, but it will mislead students in later units. Moderate.
- "260 years apart": 1953 − 1692 = 261 (trivial). The image credits for the Miller (Anefo, CC0) and McCarthy photos are present. The map is credited (Natural Earth/GeoNames).

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Historical maps:** never shade a modern country to stand for a historical polity. Draw the historical extent or no shading, and generate the world → region → place sequence that the speaker notes already promise, with each label checked against its pin. (R2, R11)
2. **A fact-check pass for context timelines,** with a known-traps list for frequently taught units: when the Salem trials ended (1693), HUAC versus McCarthy, the Miller passport and contempt dates. Every quotation must carry its source on the slide, or be marked "paraphrase". (R11)
3. **Faithful stack and print:** "Add everything" should add the plan's steps once, with the alternatives stored as switchable options, not as extra rows. It should never duplicate a Short Response, and it should warn when the stack total exceeds the requested period. The printed plan must carry the objective, the key points and slide numbers computed from the final deck. (Brian: minutes add up; Yawp!: ready to print; alignment)
4. **Context-day template:** each historical figure who is also a character gets an on-screen "In the play:" tag. Every domain term gets a definition at or before first use (an auto-generated "words to know" slide). The last step returns to the class-starter question and sets the play's treatment beside the students' answers. (R1, R4, R5, R8, R12)
5. **Image rules:** every content slide gets a picture of the thing itself, or a labeled "[image needed: …]" slot, never an empty panel. Reusing an image within a deck should be blocked. (R6, R7, R10, R13)
