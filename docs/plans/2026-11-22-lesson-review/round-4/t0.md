# t0 — Macbeth 1.3: The Prophecy and the Aside (12th grade)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Teach a 50-minute lesson for 12th grade on Macbeth Act 1, Scene 3: the witches' prophecy and Macbeth's first aside. Include the slide deck."

**What came back:** A plan titled "The Prophecy and the Aside" in eight numbered steps. Step 1 is a header ("\*Macbeth\*, Act 1, Scene 3 — English 12 · 50 min", with the markdown asterisks showing) that carries the objective. Then: Opening question "What does a prediction do to you?" (5) with three class-starter options; Context (5); Read aloud lines 48–84 (10); Annotation and close read of the aside, lines 127–142 (12); Discussion "Fate vs. choice" (10); Short Response (8); Closing and exit ticket with three options (5). The real steps add up to **55 minutes**, and the plan's own footer says "This plan runs 105 minutes; the period is 50" because it counts the header as a 50-minute step. The deck has **13 slides at 52 minutes** (1+1+4+1+2+2+1+10+3+9+10+7+1), flagged "52 min for a 50-minute period". The handout PDF has 5 pages: starter, the aside (16 lines, numbered), a word list, the three "All hail" lines with a paraphrase of Banquo's prophecy, two annotation tasks, the Short Response (twice), and a paper exit ticket. The log says "no handout suggestion offered", but the handout was produced inside the first reply. After "Add everything" the stack reads **105 of 50 minutes, 55 min over**, with **17% writing (18 min)**. The phantom row "\*Macbeth\*, Act 1, Scene 3 — English 12 ·" sits at START as a 50-minute Activity, and the handout sits there as a 0-minute Activity.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** The arc is real. It opens on the students' own experience ("If someone you half-believed told you that you were going to be famous... what would that do to you?"), teaches briefly (Context, step 3), reads, then ties back: step 6 tells the teacher to "anchor to their class starter writing" before asking "whose fault is it". But the plan has two competing openings. The Opening question in step 2 and the class starter "I am the captain of my destiny" are different prompts, and only the starter survives into the deck (slide 3) and the stack.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** The aside is printed accurately on the handout (p.2, 16 lines) and the modeled think-aloud on "soliciting" (step 5) is specific and good. But the 10-minute read-aloud of lines 48–84 has no text anywhere. The handout gives only three "All hail" lines and a paraphrase, although step 4 says "students follow along on the handout". Lines 85–126, where Ross tells Macbeth he is Thane of Cawdor (the "truth" behind "Two truths are told"), are never read. Slide 9 cites 1.3.127–142 but stops mid-sentence at line 136 ("And make my seated heart knock at my ribs,").
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 3.** The Context step is short and correct: where we are in the play, what an aside is, the three titles in order. Words are glossed before reading (plan table, slide 6, handout p.2). There are errors, though: "corporal (line 53)" (it is around line 81), "the 2010 RSC production", and "Banquo hears the same prophecy" (slide 11 notes).
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R2–R4, R6, R7, R13): 2.** Only 2 of 13 slides have pictures: slide 5 has the de Witt/van Buchel Swan Theatre sketch, and slide 7 has an uncaptioned modern photo of Cawdor Castle, a building from the late 14th or 15th century with no link to the question on the slide. Nothing shows the witches, Macbeth, Banquo or the heath. Slides 4 and 11 left only "Picture to add" notes, and the one for slide 11 is Fuseli's *Lady Macbeth Seizing the Daggers*, an Act 2 image (off-scene, and it gives the plot away). Slides 2, 4, 5, 6, 8 and 10 are text only.
- **E. Writing: 3.** There is a lot of writing and it is aligned. The starter, the Short Response ("What does Macbeth's aside reveal that the witches did not put there? Point to at least one specific moment...") and the "Understand a text" exit ticket all measure the objective directly. The starter "captain of my destiny" leans on fate vs. free will more than on "what a prediction does to you", but it serves step 6. The stack shows 17% only because the phantom 50 minutes inflates the denominator; on the real 55 minutes, writing is about 33%.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** There are several checks: the one-minute prediction on slide 7, circulating during the pair annotation with a push question ("What does Banquo see?"), the discussion talk moves, and a nudge in the Short Response notes for students who are only summarizing. Annotations are never collected and there is no quick check on the plot of lines 48–84.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The stack is broken: 105 of 50 minutes, a 50-minute "Activity" row at START, and an Apply suggestion that offers to "Trim '\*Macbeth\*, Act 1, Scene 3 — English 12' to 3 min". Even without that row the plan is 55 minutes and the deck is 52. The handout repeats the Short Response (Part 3 on p.3, then section 3 on p.4), leaves most of p.3 empty, and lacks the text for the read-aloud. The plan PDF (step 5) dumps the handout as a run-on paragraph ("Words to know fantastical — imagined, unreal corporal — bodily...").
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** It offers real options: three ways to run the read-aloud, three starters, three exit tickets, and "this can also run as homework". The speaker notes are useful and in a teacher's voice, for example the slide 12 nudge: "You've told me what the witches said. Now tell me what Macbeth does with it in his own mind." It ends with one closing question. Against that, the stack does not match the plan: the opening question is missing and the handout sits in the run of show as a 0-minute step.
- **I. Access: 2.** It glosses vocabulary, gives the "read to the punctuation" tip, and pairs strong readers with struggling ones. For Shakespearean verse it offers no modern-English paraphrase or side-by-side, no frame for the annotation or the Short Response (slide 11 has the only frame, for discussion), and no chunking of the 16-line aside for English learners.
- **Overall: 3 (usable with edits, at the low end).** The intellectual core is strong: the modeled think-aloud on "supernatural soliciting", mapping the emotional shifts in the aside, and the fate-vs.-choice question tied back to the starter. Before using it, a teacher must delete the phantom 50-minute row, cut 5 minutes, supply lines 48–155 of the text, and fix slide 9.

## 3. Strengths

1. **The close-reading task is well built.** "Map the aside: at least three distinct emotional states in sixteen lines", with a 2-minute teacher model on lines 127–130 first (step 5, slide 10 notes), is a true I do / you do on a short passage, and the follow-up asks what Macbeth is "trying *not* to consider".
2. **The writing measures the objective.** The Short Response and exit ticket ask for "a specific moment in the aside" and "what in the text made you read it that way". The exit-ticket rubric even names the mix-up to watch for: summarizing the prophecy instead of analyzing the aside.
3. **The discussion is tied to the opening and complicated well.** Step 6 starts from the students' starter writing, then pushes with Banquo's contrasting reaction and with James I's belief in witchcraft. This is R12(4) as intended.

## 4. Problems

1. **The stack and plan count a header as a 50-minute step.** Where: plan step 1, the plan footer ("runs 105 minutes"), the stack START row, and the "Trim" suggestion. Why it matters: the teacher who clicks "Add everything" sees a lesson 55 minutes over, and the fix the tool offers is nonsense, which erodes trust in every number it shows. It also makes the writing share wrong (17% instead of about 33%). Breaks Brian's "minutes must add up" and Feasibility.
2. **The text students are told to read is not supplied, and the key part of the scene is skipped.** Where: step 4 / slide 8 ("Read lines 48–84... follow along on your handout"), and the handout p.2, which has only three lines of 48–70, "paraphrased". Lines 85–126 (Ross: "He bade me... call thee Thane of Cawdor") are never read, yet the aside opens "Two truths are told". Slide 8 also asks students to "Star the moment the aside begins" in a passage that contains no aside. Why it matters: students cannot follow along, and they meet the aside without the event that triggers it. Breaks R9 and R12(3).
3. **The plan runs over even when counted correctly, and the plan, deck and stack disagree.** Where: plan steps total 55, the deck 52, the stack 105. The opening question in step 2 is in the plan but not in the deck or stack; the class starter replaces it at 4–5 minutes. Why it matters: the teacher must find and cut 5 minutes on their own, and does not know which opening to use. Breaks Brian's deck-minutes rule and the alignment principle.
4. **The visuals do not show the play.** Where: slides 4–11. Only two pictures, neither of the scene: a theatre sketch and an uncaptioned castle photo (slide 7) unrelated to "which prophecy surprises him". No image of the witches meeting Macbeth and Banquo, although Fuseli's *The Three Witches* and many stage photographs exist. The slide 11 placeholder suggests an Act 2 painting. Why it matters: 12th graders meeting the witches benefit from seeing a staging, and the scene's visual strangeness ("you should be women, / And yet your beards forbid me") is part of the reading. Breaks R6, R7 and R13.
5. **Slide 9 cuts off the passage it says it shows.** Where: slide 9, labeled 1.3.127–142 but ending at line 136 on a comma, without "Present fears / Are less than horrible imaginings" or "My thought, whose murder yet is but fantastical", the lines where "the word that shows he is already imagining violence" actually is. Why it matters: the projected annotation task points students to an answer that is not on screen. Breaks R9 and R11.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- "*corporal* (line 53)": "what seem'd corporal melted / As breath into the wind" is around line 81. Line 53 is "Are ye fantastical...". High confidence.
- "the 2010 RSC production is excellent": the well-known 2010 filmed *Macbeth* (Rupert Goold, Patrick Stewart) was a Chichester Festival Theatre production, not the RSC's. The RSC films are 1979 (Nunn), 2001 (Doran) and 2018 (Eccleston). Fairly confident.
- "Banquo hears the same prophecy. Why does he react differently?" (step 6, slide 11 notes): Banquo hears Macbeth's prophecies plus his own different one ("Lesser than Macbeth, and greater"; "Thou shalt get kings, though thou be none"). The question is still usable if reworded. High confidence.
- Slide 9: the citation 1.3.127–142 does not match the text shown, which ends at line 136. Certain.
- "The aside contains the line 'If chance will have me king...'": in the standard numbering those lines (around 143–144) come after Banquo's "Look how our partner's rapt", outside the 127–142 range printed on the handout. The handout calls it a paraphrase "near the end of the aside", but students cannot find it. Moderate confidence on the numbering, which varies by edition.
- Slide 7 photo of Cawdor Castle: the castle postdates the historical Macbeth by centuries and is not a setting in 1.3. There is no caption for students. A misleading picture rather than a false statement.
- The quotations that are printed (the aside lines 127–142, the three "All hail" lines) are accurate. High confidence.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Never turn a plan's title or header line into a step.** A heading with no step type ("Title · English 12 · 50 min") should become the lesson title and objective, not a timed Activity. Total the plan only from real steps, and block "Trim" suggestions on non-steps. (Brian: minutes add up; Feasibility.)
2. **Before showing a plan, check its total against the period and make the plan, deck and stack agree.** Drop or merge the second opening when a class starter is chosen, and keep the deck's minutes equal to the plan's. (Brian; R12; alignment.)
3. **If a step says "follow along on the handout", the handout must contain those lines.** A public-domain text (Shakespeare) has no excuse: print the full read-aloud range. Also check that the range covers the plot event the focal passage depends on (here, Ross's news). (R9, R11.)
4. **Validate excerpt slides against their citation.** If the body is truncated to fit, change the cited range or split it across two slides; never cut off mid-sentence under the full range. (R9, R11.)
5. **For a scene lesson, look for images of that scene (stagings, period illustrations such as Fuseli's *Three Witches*) before any setting photo, and never suggest an image from a later act.** Give every photo a student-facing caption that says why it is there. (R6, R7, R13.)
