# d1 — Language and Power in 1984 (unit plan, 12th grade)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Build a unit plan for 1984 for 12th grade: four weeks, 50-minute periods, with a focus on language and power, ending in a research-backed essay."

**What came back:** A 20-day unit map ("Language and Power in 1984", 20 x 50 min = 1,000 min), returned in 108 s. Each day has a title, objective, reading, in-class activity and a check. The arc: Day 1 theme opener, Day 2 context ("Oceania on the map"), Days 3-14 close reading by theme (telescreen/diary, Newspeak/Syme, doublethink, Ministry of Truth, a Part One synthesis fishbowl, Julia, the room, Goldstein's book, O'Brien, Room 101, the Appendix), with research days on 11 and 15, then thesis (16), pre-writing (17), drafting (18-19) and submit/reflect (20). It ends in the school's Thesis-Driven Essay assignment. The chat ends with three check-in bullets (arc, research days, Day 13 care note). No deck, no handout (none expected at unit level). The printable plan PDF is 5 pages. The stack is empty ("0 of 50 minutes planned"). The script found no "Add everything" button, which is right for a unit map: days are built one at a time. Planner checks: factsFlagged 1, repaired 1, no quotations checked.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** Day 1 does what R12 asks: it opens on the students' own experience ("think of a feeling you have never been able to name"), then asks what they know about totalitarianism and propaganda, then gives a short context lecture. Day 20 returns to Day 1 ("what do you now believe... that you did not believe on Day 1?"). Inside the unit the order within days slips: on Day 10 and Day 20 the "class starter" comes after the group work and discussion.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** The scene choices are expert: Syme's "The Revolution will be complete when the language is perfect" (P1 ch5), Winston correcting the Times (P1 ch4), the coral paperweight, the oath, 2+2=5, the Appendix's past tense. But the reading schedule, which is the spine of a novel unit, is broken. Part One is read in the order 1, 5, 3+7, 4. Day 10 assigns Part Two ch9-10 (Goldstein's book and the arrest) before Day 12 assigns ch7-8 (the Brotherhood meeting). Part Two ch6 and Part Three ch5-6 (Room 101 itself, and "He loved Big Brother") are never assigned.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 3.** Day 2 front-loads the key terms (doublethink, Newspeak, thoughtcrime, telescreen), and the mini-lectures sit in the right places (Newspeak, doublethink, Goldstein's book, source credibility, thesis). What each mini-lecture covers is only named, which is normal at unit level. Orwell's "Politics and the English Language" (1946) is missing. It is the obvious companion text and research anchor for a "language and power" unit.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding: null.** Unit map only, no deck. Day 2 promises a "context deck" with the superstates, which is the right instinct (R1, R2).
- **E. Writing: 3.** Writing runs daily: a class starter or Short Response almost every day, a fishbowl followed by a written exit ticket, and a sensible essay pipeline (thesis, pre-write, two drafting days, revision). The essay is built from students' own accumulated writing ("By Day 18, students have a stack of their own thinking to mine for a thesis"), which is excellent. The research half of a "research-backed essay" is thin: two credible sources found and annotated in one period (Day 11), and one integration day.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** Every day names a check: whiteboards on Day 2, a one-sentence explanation of Syme on Day 4, a citation-plus-claim exit ticket on Day 11. Several "checks" (Days 15, 16) are just the practice activity restated.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The minutes add up (20 x 50). But the homework load is unrealistic: Part Three ch1-4 is one homework night before Day 13, and Part Two ch4-5 is one night. Day 2 packs a context deck, four vocabulary terms, a class starter and an 8-page read-aloud into 50 minutes. The printed plan PDF cuts every "In class" line off with an ellipsis ("Short context lecture: Orwell, 1940s...", "Class starter: if you were designing..."), so the printout cannot be taught from. Page numbers ("pages 1-8", "8-16") name no edition.
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** The plan offers options: "If you'd rather assign a fixed prompt, I can adjust", the research days "could move or combine", and the Day 13 care note includes an opt-out. Each day has a "Build this day" button. It ends with three questions, not one closing question (Brian), and Day 7 is labeled "Week 1 synthesis" though it falls in week 2.
- **I. Access: 2.** Apart from the Day 13 option to write rather than speak, there are no scaffolds anywhere: no audio, reading guide, chunked passages, sentence frames or vocabulary support after Day 2. Yet the unit assigns heavy independent reading every night and a research essay.
- **Overall: 2.** The thematic design and the essay pipeline are strong, and a teacher would want this skeleton. But the reading schedule has to be rebuilt before it can be taught: Brotherhood after the arrest, the novel's ending never read, Room 101 not in the assigned chapters. A department chair would send it back for that.

## 3. Strengths

1. The essay grows out of students' own writing. Daily starters on language, power, truth and resistance become the thesis source on Day 16 ("Students reread their own writing from the unit and choose the idea that has pulled at them most"). This is Yawp!'s writing value at unit scale.
2. The scenes are chosen with real literary judgment: Syme on Newspeak, the Ministry rewrite, the paperweight-as-room, O'Brien using Winston's language against him, and especially Day 14 on the Appendix's past tense as evidence that the Party falls. That is a 12th-grade-worthy reading.
3. Day 1 meets R12 (experience, then what they know, then short context), and Day 20 closes the loop on it. Day 13 carries a well-judged care note with an opt-out.

## 4. Problems

1. **The reading order is scrambled and the ending is never assigned** (Days 4-14). Part One goes 1, 5, 3+7, 4. Day 10 (P2 ch9-10, Goldstein's book plus the arrest) comes before Day 12 (P2 ch7-8, the Brotherhood oath). P2 ch6 and P3 ch5-6 (Room 101 and "He loved Big Brother") are never read, yet Day 13 is titled "Room 101" and Day 20 asks what Orwell "got right." Students reading at home would meet the arrest before the meeting that causes it and would never finish the book. This breaks R11 (accuracy), spacing and connection to the unit, and R1 (equip students to read *this* book).
2. **The printed plan truncates every in-class activity** (plan PDF, all 5 pages: "Short context lecture: Orwell, 1940s...", "Small groups: identify three Newspeak principles from the..."). The teacher's paper copy is missing the instructions. It also has a stray capital in the overview ("It ends with A research-backed..."). This breaks Yawp!'s "materials ready to print and use."
3. **The research essay is under-built for a research essay.** Source hunting gets one period, Day 11, mid-novel, before students know their thesis. There is no list of suggested sources, no "Politics and the English Language", and no citation format or works-cited support. 12th graders asked for a "research-backed essay" need anchor texts and a citation model. This breaks clear objective and alignment, and scaffolding.
4. **No access scaffolds for a reading-heavy unit** (all days). One night for P3 ch1-4, one night for P2 ch4-5, and no audio, reading guides, chunked passages or frames. Struggling readers and English learners fall behind by Day 5 and stay behind. This breaks scaffolding and access.
5. **The order within days and the labels are careless.** The class starter comes after the main activity on Day 10 ("Groups report back. Class starter: who do you trust...") and Day 20. Day 7 is called "Week 1 synthesis" in week 2. Page numbers are given with no edition. Small things, but a teacher building from this inherits them. This breaks R12 (the starter opens the lesson) and R11.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- Syme's line, "The Revolution will be complete when the language is perfect", is paraphrased correctly (P1 ch5). The chapter assignments for Syme (P1 ch5), Ministry work (P1 ch4), the note (P2 ch1), the room and paperweight (P2 ch4), the Brotherhood meeting (P2 ch8) and Goldstein's book (P2 ch9) are correct. Publication 1949 is correct.
- Error: Day 13 is titled "Room 101" but assigns Part Three ch1-4. Room 101 is Part Three ch5.
- Error (sequence): P2 ch9-10 is assigned before P2 ch7-8.
- Imprecise: "pages 1-8" and "8-16" for Part One ch1 match no named edition. In common editions ch1 runs about 20-25 pages.
- Confidence: high on chapter placements, moderate on page counts.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Validate the reading schedule of a novel unit:** chapters assigned in order, every chapter covered once (or explicitly marked skipped), and each "title" scene inside its assigned range. Reject or repair maps that go out of order. (R11; spacing and connection)
2. **Render the full "In class" text in the printable unit plan PDF** (wrap, don't ellipsize), and fix sentence-case joins in the overview template. (Yawp! materials ready to print)
3. **When a unit "ends in a research essay," require research scaffolds:** two or three suggested anchor sources (for language and power, "Politics and the English Language"), research placed after the thesis is formed or iteratively, and a citation mini-lesson. (Objective alignment; scaffolding)
4. **Add a per-unit access line and per-day reading supports:** audio and chunked-passage options, a reading guide for heavy homework nights, and a cap on homework pages per night. (Access; feasibility)
5. **Keep the class starter first in every day's activity text, and end the chat with one closing question.** (R12; Brian)
