# b1 — Of Mice and Men unit plan (10th grade, 3 weeks)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Build a unit plan for Of Mice and Men for 10th grade: three weeks, 50-minute periods, ending in an argumentative essay on whether George was right."

**What came back** (102 s, one turn): a 15-day unit, "Of Mice and Men: What Do We Owe Each Other?", English 10 · Period 3, every day listed at 50 minutes (750 minutes), ending in a Thesis-Driven Essay, "Was George right to shoot Lennie?". The chat reply opens with ten recurring class-starter prompts (friendship/obligation, loneliness, dreams, impossible choices), then a unit card with 15 days, each with objective, reading, in-class activity, a "why", and a check; then three questions for the teacher and a "Check before class" note. A 4-page printable unit-plan PDF mirrors the card. No slide deck, no handout (none expected for a unit plan; slides.pdf 404, handouts.pdf 409). The test script found no "Add everything to the lesson" button, so the stack is empty: "Untitled lesson", 0 of 50 minutes planned, 0% of class writing. That is the expected behavior for a unit plan (days are built one at a time with "Build this day"), so the stack is not judged as a lesson here.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** Day 1 opens on the theme in students' lives ("someone you've felt responsible for"), then asks what they know about the Great Depression, then direct teaching, then a closing question; across the unit the starters return to the essay's moral question and the Day 11 fishbowl sets the text beside students' positions. Weakened by the out-of-order reading (see Problems) and by asking for a thesis on George's choice (Day 5) before students have read Chapters 5–6.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** The right passages are named (rabbits speech, Candy joining the dream, Crooks's "a guy needs somebody" speech, Candy's dog, the final scene), but Chapter 5 (Lennie kills Curley's wife, the event that forces George's choice) is never assigned or read, and the dog scene from Chapter 3 is read on Day 8, after Chapter 4. No page references or quoted lines anywhere.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 2.** Day 1 is "short direct teaching on 1930s California, migrant labor, and the ranch world" with no content: no Dust Bowl, Salinas Valley/Soledad, bindlestiffs, the 1937 publication, or vocabulary (bunkhouse, swamper, skinner, "stable buck", "bucking barley"). Writing mini-lessons are named by Yawp! title only.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding: n/a** (unit plan, no deck). Not scored.
- **E. Writing: 3.** Strong: a class starter every day, Short Responses on Days 2, 6, 10, a skills sequence (thesis, evidence, analysis, topic sentences, hooks/conclusions/transitions) that maps onto the final essay, a full drafting day and a revision day with specific targets. Weak alignment: Days 5, 7 and 9 build thesis and evidence for an essay about an ending students have not yet read.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 2.** Every day has a "Check", but most are oral ("each student reads their best quote aloud", "each group names the one detail"); there is no reading check on any homework chapter even though Chapters 1, 2 and 4 are homework, and no point where the teacher finds out whether students actually understand the plot before the argument.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** 15 × 50 = 750 minutes and three weeks match, but the reading pace omits a whole chapter, has students jigsaw Crooks's exclusion on Day 3 before reading Chapter 4, and Day 15 starts with "final essay submitted at the start of class" one day after a revision lesson, leaving no time to finish revisions.
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** Good options at the end ("If you'd rather front-load more reading days...", alternative framings "mercy vs. betrayal"); each day has a clear "why". But the "Check before class" box tells the teacher "Not an error; the Chapter 3 placement is correct", an internal checker note that reassures the teacher about the one thing that is wrong. It ends on three questions, not one.
- **I. Access: 1.** No sentence frames, no audio or read-aloud plan for the homework chapters, no vocabulary support for 1930s ranch dialect, no chunking, nothing for English learners, and no guidance on handling the racial slurs in Chapters 4 and 6 (Crooks) — something a chair would expect in any 10th-grade plan for this book.
- **Overall: 2.** A strong teacher would keep the writing spine and the starter arc, but would have to rebuild the reading schedule (missing Chapter 5, Chapter 3 split across Days 4 and 8) and add a real context day before using it.

## 3. Strengths

1. The writing spine is well-built for the stated essay: thesis (Day 5) → evidence (7) → analysis (9) → fishbowl with counterargument (11) → topic-sentence outline (12) → full drafting (13) → targeted revision of hook, conclusion and transitions (14). Each skill is tied to a Yawp! Writing Fundamental and to the George prompt.
2. The ten class starters are genuinely good personal-experience prompts that seed the essay ("When does protecting someone become controlling them?", "Is mercy the same as weakness?") and follow the R12 opening move every day.
3. The Day 11 fishbowl ("four argue George was right, four argue he was wrong... everyone writes their position and names the best argument against them") is an excellent pivot that forces students to meet the counterargument before drafting.

## 4. Problems

1. **Chapter 5 is never read.** Where: the whole unit card; reading lines cover Ch. 1 (Day 2), Ch. 2 (Day 3), Ch. 3 (Days 3–4, 8), Ch. 4 (Day 6), Ch. 6 (Day 10). Why it matters: Lennie killing Curley's wife in the barn is the cause of George's decision; students cannot argue "was George right" without the event that left him with only Curley's lynch mob or the gun. Principle: R1, R9, alignment of reading to the final task.
2. **The reading order is broken.** Where: Day 4 reads "Chapter 3 (the dream scene with Candy)"; Day 6 reads Chapter 4; Day 8 then goes back to "Chapter 3 (Carlson and the dog)". The dog is shot before Candy joins the dream in the same chapter. Day 3 also has groups find Crooks's "moment of exclusion" before Chapter 4 is read. Why it matters: students meet Candy's offer to buy in without having seen him lose his dog, which is the reason he says "I ought to of shot that dog myself"; the parallel the unit calls "the structural key to the essay" is taught out of sequence. Principle: R11 (facts must be exactly right), sequencing/coherence.
3. **Thesis and evidence before the ending.** Where: Day 5 (thesis about George), Day 7 (evidence for "their own thesis"), Day 9 (analysis of "a quote about George"), all before Day 10's final scene. Why it matters: students write an arguable claim about a shooting they have not read, which either spoils the ending or produces empty theses that must be rewritten. Principle: objective/task alignment; Kevin's R12 step 4 (the text's treatment set beside students' views needs the text).
4. **Day 1 context is a placeholder, and there is no access support at all.** Where: Day 1 "Short direct teaching on 1930s California, migrant labor, and the ranch world"; no scaffold anywhere. Why it matters: 10th graders need the specific world (Salinas Valley near Soledad, itinerant "bindlestiffs", barley harvest, the ranch hierarchy, the dialect) and struggling readers and English learners are expected to read four chapters at home with no audio, frames or vocabulary. No guidance on the novel's racial slurs. Principle: R1, R5, scaffolding and access.
5. **Misleading "Check before class" note and thin checks.** Where: bottom of the reply: "'Chapter 3 (the dream scene with Candy) read closely in class': Not an error; the Chapter 3 placement is correct." Why it matters: the accuracy check examined chapter placement in isolation and told the teacher it was fine, while the sequence problem went through; meanwhile no day checks that homework chapters were read. Principle: R11; checking for understanding.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- No quotations are given, so none to check.
- Sequence error: the killing of Candy's dog (Chapter 3) is placed on Day 8 after Chapter 4, and precedes Candy joining the dream within Chapter 3 (high confidence).
- Chapter 5 omitted from a whole-novel unit (high confidence; verified by searching the reply text).
- Day 10 check, "What does George's silence after the shot tell us", is loose: George is not silent after the shot; he lies to the men about how he got Carlson's Luger and answers Slim ("Yeah. I got it away from him") and Slim says "Never you mind... You hadda, George. I swear you hadda." His speech is sparse, not absent (medium-high confidence on the wording of these lines).
- Day 3: Crooks's moment of exclusion comes in Chapter 4; on Day 3 students have only Candy's Chapter 2 account of him (high confidence).

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Validate unit reading coverage and order** against the work's chapter list: every chapter assigned exactly once in order, any re-reading flagged as "return to", and the event the final essay turns on read before any thesis work on it. (R11, R1, alignment)
2. **Make the accuracy check test sequence, not just location.** A placement check that says "the Chapter 3 placement is correct" should also check that Day N's chapter is not earlier than Day N−1's, and never surface an internal "Not an error" line to the teacher. (R11)
3. **Require a content stub for any context day in a unit plan**: three to five named facts (place, years, who, three words students need) so "short direct teaching" is not a blank. (R1, R2, R3, R5)
4. **Add an access line to every unit and every day**: audio/read-aloud plan for homework chapters, sentence frames for the starter and exit, a glossary for period dialect, and a content-sensitivity note when the text contains slurs or violence. (Scaffolding and access)
5. **Put a reading check on every homework day** (a two-question Short Response or a Yawp! quick write on the chapter), and offer three exit-ticket options per Brian. (Checking for understanding; Brian's exit-ticket options)
