# b2 — Things Fall Apart, Day 1 (Igbo society; who Achebe wrote back to)

## 3. Strengths

1. The close look at the opening paragraph (step 4, slides 12–13, handout p. 2) is real teaching: the teacher thinks aloud on one sentence ("He doesn't tell me what Okonkwo looks like — he tells me what other people think of him... reputation = identity in this world"), then students annotate with two concrete tasks and discuss "Why does that matter as an opening move?" That ties the text directly to the counter-Conrad argument.
2. The "what do you already know about Africa" list, kept on the board and revisited at the close ("which of these does the book complicate?"), is a smart, low-stakes way to surface stereotypes and let the text answer them.
3. The timeline (slide 6) is a real timeline, not a list, and its notes ask pointed questions ("What do you think it means that no African was in that room?"; the gap between 1899 and 1958).

## 4. Problems

1. **The map does not show the place.** Where: slide 5. The legend lists "1 Nigeria — not on the map, 2 Ogidi — not on the map, 3 Lagos, 4 London"; only Lagos and London are pinned, while the notes tell the teacher to "start with Nigeria... then move to Ogidi". Lagos is a Yoruba city in the far west, not Igboland. Why it matters: students leave day 1 unable to place Umuofia, and the one pin in Nigeria points to the wrong region. Principle: R2, R11.
2. **No pictures of pre-colonial Igbo life; one modern photo used twice.** Where: slides 7 and 13 (same "Igbo family in traditional attire" photo); slide 8 (egwugwu, chi, ozo with no image); slide 1 notes refer to an absent Van Gogh. Why it matters: the lesson's subject is "Igbo society before the colonizers", yet students see only a contemporary family portrait; egwugwu masks, a compound, a yam barn or a wrestling match would teach the content. Principle: R6, R7, R13.
3. **"Who Achebe was writing back to" is incomplete and anachronistic.** Where: plan step 3 ("the dominant image of Africa... came largely from one novel: Heart of Darkness... Achebe called Conrad 'a thoroughgoing racist' in his 1975 lecture... Things Fall Apart was his answer"), slide 9. Joyce Cary's *Mister Johnson* (1939), which Achebe repeatedly named as a spur to write, is absent, as is the colonial-officer genre the novel's last page satirizes (the District Commissioner's planned book). Presenting a 1975 lecture as the reason for a 1958 novel misleads students about cause and sequence. Principle: R11, R1.
4. **Quotation and citation errors in student-facing material.** Where: handout p. 2 reads "one of the greatest since the founder of their town"; Achebe wrote "one of the fiercest". Plan step 3 cites the proverb "When the moon is shining the cripple becomes hungry for a walk" as Chapter 1; it is in Chapter 2. Why it matters: students annotate a misquoted paragraph, and a teacher pointing to Chapter 1 will not find the proverb. Principle: R11, Brian (quotations exactly right).
5. **45 minutes for a 50-minute request, with internal timing mismatches.** Where: plan total, deck header "15 slides · 45 min", stack "45 minutes planned", blank "—minute period" chip; context section 15 min vs slides 17 min; close look 15 vs 12. Why it matters: the teacher is left with five unplanned minutes and a deck whose sections do not match the plan. Principle: Brian (deck minutes must add up to the lesson); feasibility.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- Handout p. 2: "one of the greatest since the founder of their town" should be "one of the fiercest since the founder of their town" (fairly confident, about 85%).
- Plan step 3: the "moon is shining" proverb is attributed to Chapter 1; it appears in Chapter 2 (the night-time passage before the town crier's ogene) (fairly confident, about 80%).
- Achebe's 1975 Chancellor's Lecture at UMass Amherst used "a bloody racist"; "a thoroughgoing racist" is the wording of the revised 1977 published version. Attributing the latter phrase to "his 1975 lecture" is slightly off (medium confidence, about 70%).
- Slide 5: Lagos is pinned as the novel's Nigerian location marker; the novel is set in Igboland, east of the Niger, and Ogidi (Achebe's home) is not pinned despite the notes (high confidence; the legend says so).
- Not an error but an omission: Joyce Cary's *Mister Johnson* as a target of Achebe's "writing back" (high confidence).
- Timeline facts (Berlin Conference 1884–85, Heart of Darkness 1899, amalgamation of Nigeria 1914, Things Fall Apart 1958) are correct. Achebe born in Ogidi: correct.
- The planner's checks report factsCorrected 2, factsFlagged 4, repaired 4, but quotationsVerified 0 and excerptsFromSource 0: the paragraph was not checked against a source.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Map builder must place every listed pin or refuse the slide.** A legend that says "not on the map" should never ship; geocode the novel's region (Igboland, Ogidi, the Niger) and fall back to a labeled slot (R10) rather than a pin on an unrelated city. (R2, R10, R11)
2. **Picture selection: no repeats, and match the period.** For a "before the colonizers" context day, search for period artifacts (egwugwu masks, Mbari houses, compounds, yam barns, early 20th-century photographs) and reject contemporary portraits; block reuse of the same image in one deck; never write speaker notes that describe an image not placed on the slide. (R6, R7, R13, R4)
3. **Verify quoted passages against a source text word for word**, and verify chapter citations for proverbs and quotes; if the source is unavailable, mark the passage "check against your copy" (R10) instead of reporting zero quotation checks silently. (R11, R9)
4. **Hold the lesson to the requested period length.** If the plan totals less than the period, add a named buffer or extend a step and say so; reconcile each plan step's minutes with the minutes of the slides that serve it. (Brian: deck minutes add up; feasibility)
5. **For "writing back" requests, require the full set of targets with dates in order** (Cary's *Mister Johnson*, Conrad, the colonial-administrator writing the ending satirizes) and keep later commentary (the 1975 lecture) labeled as later. Return to the opening thematic question at the close and offer three exit-ticket options. (R11, R12, Brian)
