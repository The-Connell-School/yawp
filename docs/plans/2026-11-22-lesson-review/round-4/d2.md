# d2 — "To be, or not to be" close reading (Hamlet 3.1, 12th grade)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Teach a 50-minute lesson for 12th grade on Hamlet's 'To be, or not to be' soliloquy (Act 3, Scene 1). Include the slide deck."

**What came back** (123 s): A 50-minute plan with these steps:
- Class starter, 5 min, three options
- Context: what Hamlet is carrying, 5 min
- Read aloud twice, 8 min
- Guided close reading in three sections, 15 min (model 2, pairs 10, whole class 3)
- Short Response, 12 min ("Pick one image... carrying more than it first appears")
- Closing and exit ticket, 5 min, three options (conclusion / "conscience" / ask a question)

The real steps sum to 50. But the plan's subheading "12th Grade English · 50 minutes" was parsed as step 1, a 50-minute "Activity". The chat therefore says "This plan runs 100 minutes; the period is 50", and the stack shows **100 of 50 minutes planned, "50 min over"**. The stack's own fix is "Trim '12th Grade English' to 3 min".

The deck has 14 slides and is labeled "100 min". Slide 7 ("Read along") is timed at 58 min. Two slides carry pictures.

The handout has 6 pages:
- p1: starter with lines
- p2: the full soliloquy with line numbers and a glossary
- p3: blank
- p4: the three-section close-reading questions
- p5: Short Response with lines
- p6: exit ticket with lines

The stack's writing share is 22% (22 of a bogus 100 min). The true share is 44% of the 50-minute lesson. The plan PDF is 2 pages.

Planner checks: work "hamlet", 11 quotations verified, 4 corrected, 1 unverified, 4 facts flagged and repaired.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** It opens on the theme in students' lives: "The thing you're most afraid of is probably the thing you most need to do", with the note that "a student who says 'I overthink everything and never do anything' has already found Hamlet". It then gives short context, reads the text, and leaves the thematic question on the board ("What is the difference between thinking about something and doing it?", slide 6). Nothing explicitly sets Hamlet's answer beside what students wrote at the start. The tie-back is only implied in a note.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 3.** Handout p2 prints the whole soliloquy accurately, numbered, through "And lose the name of action". The close reading is specific: "'conscience' here means consciousness... not guilt", the catalogue's accumulation, and "undiscover'd country" as the unknown. The slides show only three excerpts (3.1.56-60, 70-74, 83-85). The "undiscover'd country" passage that slide 12's notes push toward is on no slide. Slide 11, "The conclusion", stops before "lose the name of action", the line that is the conclusion it asks students to judge.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 2.** The context is admirably short (5 min). The handout glossary defines contumely, dispriz'd, quietus, bodkin, fardels, bourn, conscience and sicklied o'er before students need them. But the one "big idea" slide teaches a false premise: "By Act 3, Hamlet knows who killed his father... though the Mousetrap has not yet been staged" (slide 4; also the plan's first context bullet). Hamlet has only the Ghost's word, and he stages the Mousetrap precisely because "The spirit that I have seen / May be a devil" (2.2).
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R4, R6, R7, R13): 2.** Two pictures in 14 slides.
  - Slide 5 has a credited photo of Kronborg. It is the right place, but nothing on the slide says that Kronborg is Elsinore (R8).
  - Slide 8 has a skull vanitas still life as a mood image. It evokes Yorick (5.1), not this scene, and its credit is boilerplate ("Originally from en.wikipedia; description page is/was here").
  - Slides 4 and 6 are content slides with nothing to look at. The "Picture to add" note is only in the speaker notes; there is no labeled slot on the slide (R10).
  - There is no image of Hamlet himself (a production still of the soliloquy, R4).
- **E. Writing: 4.** The writing is generous and aligned: a 4-minute starter, a 12-minute graded Short Response asking for an image and "the specific words that make it carry that", and an exit ticket on what the argument concludes. Each has a ruled page on the handout. That is 22 of 50 minutes, 44%.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** Pairs write answers per section while the teacher circulates. There is "one observation per section" in the whole-class debrief, with stated targets (the catalogue builds; conscience = consciousness). The exit ticket's teacher key lists "mix-ups to watch for" (reading it as a suicide note, or thinking Hamlet reaches a decision). Nothing checks the context before the reading.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The real lesson sums to 50, but every artifact around it says 100:
  - the stack (100/50, "50 min over")
  - the deck's "100 min" label, with slide 7 at "58 min"
  - the slide 2 agenda's first item, "12th Grade English · 50 minutes"
  - the plan PDF's step 1 ("50 min")

  A teacher who clicks "Add everything" gets a run of show that is double the period. Handout p3 is blank.
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** It offers real options: three starters, three exit tickets with keys, and for the Short Response "Run this in class or assign it for homework — your call". The cold-call advice is kind ("give a student a heads-up before class"). The speaker notes give the actual model think-aloud. It ends on one closing question ("Want to refine or edit any part of this?"). The phantom 50-minute step makes the stack incoherent. The plan PDF drops the context bullets and the pairs/whole-class parts of step 6.
- **I. Access: 3.** It has a glossary, the text chunked into three sections, two read-alouds, 60 seconds of silence to add marks, and a sentence frame on slide 12 ("I chose '___' because it seems to mean more than just ___"). It has no paraphrase or side-by-side modern gloss and no audio or film option for English learners. The handout's section line ranges are inconsistent (Section 2 is "lines 60-76", but its quote starts at line 70; lines 77-82 belong to no section).
- **Overall: 3.** The pedagogy is strong and the text is right. A strong teacher would use it after fixing the context claim, deleting the phantom 50-minute step, retiming the deck, and replacing the skull.

## 3. Strengths

1. **Accurate full text, well chunked.** The whole 33-line soliloquy is verbatim on handout p2, with a glossary of the hard words. The three-section structure (question, catalogue, conclusion) gives pairs a manageable job.
2. **Real modeling.** The speaker notes give the teacher's actual think-aloud: "He doesn't say 'to act or not to act.' He says 'to be.' That's a bigger claim." There are precise push-points too ("'conscience' here means consciousness... thinking too hard is what stops action"). This is an "I do" before "you do".
3. **Writing is the backbone.** The starter connects to the speech, and the 12-minute Short Response demands textual evidence ("point to the specific words"). There are three exit-ticket options with answer keys and named misconceptions, exactly as Brian asks.

## 4. Problems

1. **A phantom 50-minute step doubles the lesson** (stack, deck slide 2 and slide 7, plan PDF step 1). The heading "12th Grade English · 50 minutes" became an activity. The stack reads "100 of 50 minutes planned", the deck says "100 min", and "Read along" is timed at 58 min. A teacher pacing from the deck or stack is misled, and the planner's own fix suggestion ("Trim '12th Grade English' to 3 min") is nonsense. This breaks Brian's rule that a deck's minutes must add up to the lesson, and feasibility.
2. **The context teaches a false premise** (slide 4, plan step 3): "By Act 3, Hamlet knows who killed his father." He suspects it on a ghost's word, and he stages the Mousetrap because the ghost "may be a devil". Students primed with "he knows and does nothing" miss the epistemological doubt that makes "the dread of something after death... puzzles the will" resonate. The error is on the one slide labeled "Big idea". This breaks R11.
3. **The slides cut the text where it matters** (slides 9-11). The excerpts skip 61-69 and 75-82 ("perchance to dream", "undiscover'd country"), even though the notes and handout Q4 target that passage. Slide 11 asks "does Hamlet reach a conclusion?" but omits the final three lines ("And lose the name of action"). Students looking up at the board cannot see the evidence the task requires. This breaks R9 and close reading.
4. **The images are decorative or missing** (slides 4, 5, 6, 8). The skull still life is a mood picture from another scene, with an unusable credit. Kronborg is not linked to "Elsinore" on screen. Two content slides are blank, and their missing-picture notice is hidden in the notes rather than shown as a labeled slot. There is no face for Hamlet. This breaks R4, R6, R7, R8, R10, R11 (credit) and R13.
5. **The handout has layout and consistency defects.** Page 3 is blank. The passage citation says "lines 56-89" but ends at 88. Section 2 is labeled "lines 60-76" but quotes from 70. The slide citations read "Hamlet, p. 3.1.56-60" (a page marker on an act.scene.line reference). These are small, but this goes to students. This breaks Yawp! "ready to print" and R11.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **Fact:** "By Act 3, Scene 1, Hamlet knows his uncle murdered his father" (plan step 3, slide 4). This is wrong. Hamlet doubts the Ghost (2.2: "The spirit that I have seen / May be a devil... I'll have grounds / More relative than this"), and that doubt is the purpose of the Mousetrap. High confidence.
- **Quotations:** the soliloquy on handout p2 and slides 9-11 matches standard modern texts (Folio "dispriz'd", "pith"). I found no misquotations. Line numbers follow Arden-style numbering (56 to 88). The handout's "lines 56-89" is off by one; the text it prints ends at line 88. High confidence.
- **Citation format:** "p. 3.1.56-60" on the slides is malformed. **Image credit:** the slide 8 credit is boilerplate, not a real attribution (R11).

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Never turn the plan's title or subheading into a timed step.** Ignore headings like "Grade · N minutes" when parsing steps. Assert that stack minutes, deck minutes and plan minutes all equal the period before rendering, and refuse any single slide timed longer than its step. (Brian: deck minutes add up; feasibility)
2. **Add a fact check for "what the character knows" claims in context steps.** For plays and novels, check plot-state claims ("knows", "has learned", "has not yet") against a scene-level summary of the work. (R11)
3. **Put every quoted passage the lesson asks about on a slide.** If the notes, handout questions or exit ticket name a line ("undiscover'd country", the conclusion), the section slides must include it. A "conclusion" excerpt must run to the end of the sentence or speech. (R9, close reading)
4. **Tighten image rules:**
   - Reject vanitas, mood or symbol images on content slides (R6, R13).
   - Caption place photos with the book's name for the place ("Kronborg = Shakespeare's Elsinore") (R8).
   - Render a visible labeled slot on the slide when no image is found (R10).
   - Reject boilerplate credits (R11).
   - Prefer a production still of the scene for R4.
5. **Handout QA pass:** no blank pages, line ranges consistent with the printed numbering, and section ranges that cover the passage without gaps or overlaps. (Yawp! ready to print; R11)
