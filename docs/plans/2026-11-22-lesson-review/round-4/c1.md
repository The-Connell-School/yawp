# c1: The Crucible, day 1 (Salem 1692 and Miller in 1953)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Build day 1 of a unit on The Crucible for 11th grade, 50 minutes: Salem in 1692 and why Arthur Miller wrote about it in 1953. Include the slide deck."

**What came back:** The chat plan has six steps that add up to 50 minutes: a class starter (5 min, three Yawp! prompt options: "We believe what we want to believe...", "Laws vs. morality", "If no one ever found out"), "Salem, 1692" direct teaching (15), "Arthur Miller and 1953" direct teaching with a pair talk (10), a read-aloud of "Miller's preface" (10), a Short Response (7), and a closing with a Yawp! exit ticket (3). There is no student handout request (HANDOUT=0). The 15-slide deck has a title, an agenda, a vocabulary slide (theocracy, spectral evidence, hysteria), a map, a 1692 timeline, a big idea, a discussion slide, a divider, a 1950–54 timeline, a compare slide, an "allegory" definition, a discussion slide, the read slide, the write slide and the exit slide. The auto-generated handouts PDF is 3 pages, one per writing prompt. The stack totals 50 minutes in six steps and reports "30% of class is writing" (15 min: starter, Short Response, exit ticket). The planner's checks were `work: none`, no quotations, 3 facts flagged, 2 repaired, 1 left for the teacher.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 2.** The starter ("We believe what we want to believe, and call it the truth") is a good thematic opening. Nothing finds out what students already know about Salem, witch hunts or McCarthy (R12.2, required on day 1). The starter is never brought back: neither the Short Response nor the closing asks students to set Miller's argument beside what they wrote at the start (R12.4).
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** The only reading is "the first two pages of Miller's prose preface" (step 4, slide 13). Slide 13 says only "Read along. Mark two things" and has no passage, no act or page reference, and no labeled slot. The close-reading prompt ("where did you feel Miller stepping out of history") never points to the actual sentences where that happens. "Preface" is also the wrong name for this material (see section 5).
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 3.** It comes in short, punchy pieces and is mostly accurate. Slide 3 defines theocracy, spectral evidence and hysteria with examples, and slide 11 defines allegory. However, "Puritan" is listed as key vocabulary in the plan but never defined on screen, and HUAC, informer, censured and contempt of Congress are used undefined. The plan attributes the Hollywood investigations to McCarthy's Senate committee, and it leaves out Elia Kazan's 1952 testimony, which is the actual answer to "why Miller wrote about it."
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R2–R4, R6, R7, R13): 2.** There is one regional map (slide 4: the Massachusetts coast only, with no world-to-country-to-place zoom), and its pin sits on Salem Town, not Salem Village. Slides 5 and 9 are lists of dates, not timelines (R3). The one photograph (McCarthy) appears twice (slides 9 and 12, R6). Arthur Miller's face is never shown (R4), and no character from the play is named on screen. Slides 5, 6 and 7 are content slides that carry only "picture to add" notes and are bare on screen (R7, R13), although they are honestly labeled (R10).
- **E. Writing: 3.** Thirty percent of the lesson is writing, and the starter, Short Response and exit ticket are all Yawp! assignments aligned to the objective. However, the Short Response (slide 14) and the exit ticket ask essentially the same question ("why 1692 instead of writing directly about the 1950s"), so the exit ticket measures nothing new.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** These are genuine and well placed: a cold call on "What made spectral evidence so dangerous?" with listen-fors and a reteach contingency, a pair talk, the compare slide with listen-fors, and circulating during the Short Response.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The deck's minutes reach 50 only because slide 13 (the read) is set to 16 minutes against the plan's 10, and the Salem and Miller sections run 11 and 12 minutes against the plan's 15 and 10. The plan says Salem is "slides 3–9" and Miller is "slides 10–13", but they are actually 3–7 and 8–12. The printable plan PDF drops the content outlines (step 3 ends at "What to cover: The political climate."; step 2 has no list; step 4 has no marking tasks). The handouts spend three pages on three short prompts and do not include the passage students are asked to annotate.
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** There are three starter options, and the speaker notes are specific and usable (listen-fors, what to say, when to pause). The stack matches the plan. Two things damage trust. The deck note claims "The deck uses *The Great Wave*... Since you've used it for *The Crucible* before," but no such image is in the deck, and it would be a decorative metaphor anyway. The "preface / else use the opening stage directions" fallback is also confused.
- **I. Access: 2.** There are sentence stems on the discussion slides ("The problem with spectral evidence was that...", "Setting it in the past let Miller...") and a vocabulary slide with examples. There is no printed copy of the passage being read aloud, no chunking or glossary for Miller's dense prose, and no frame for the two-part Short Response.
- **Overall: 3** (at the low end). The structure and content are sound and the notes are good, so a strong teacher could run this after an hour of edits: find three pictures, add Miller and Kazan, put the passage on screen and on paper, fix the minutes, and add a prior-knowledge check and an introduction to the characters before Act 1 homework.

## 3. Strengths

1. **The compare slide (slide 10) is excellent.** "Salem, 1692" and "Washington, 1953" sit in parallel columns, line for line (accuse, the court accepts testimony, deny and look guilty, confess or cooperate but name others). It makes the allegory structural rather than vague, and its speaker notes ask for the mechanism, not surface details.
2. **Spectral evidence is the right hinge, and it is defined.** Slides 3, 6 and 7 build to the question "How do you prove you are not a witch?", followed by a cold-call check with listen-fors and a reteach plan.
3. **The speaker notes are concrete and kind.** They give exact phrasing, wait times and listen-fors, and say what to do if students are blank. Gaps are labeled honestly ("Picture to add... Yawp! could not find one it could use") instead of being filled quietly (R10).

## 4. Problems

1. **The reading slide has no reading (slide 13, plan step 4).** Students are told to "Read along" and to underline and circle, but the slide has no text, no page or act reference, and no labeled slot, and the handout has no copy either. The planner also calls it "Miller's preface." *The Crucible* has no preface: the historical prose is embedded in Act One's opening stage directions, so the fallback "substitute the opening stage directions" points to the same pages. Students without the book open cannot annotate, and the planner never names the sentences where Miller "steps out of history" (for example, the passage describing Salem as a theocracy that "developed from a paradox"). This breaks R9, R10, R11, and close reading of short passages.
2. **Day 1 never introduces the play's people, even though Act 1 is homework tonight (closing, slide 15).** There is no character list, no faces for real people (no Miller, Parris, Abigail Williams, Tituba or Proctor), and no "who's who" before students meet them in Act 1. The only characters named are "Goody Proctor" in a definition example and "Reverend Samuel Parris" in small map text. Students will hit a crowded Act 1 cold. This breaks R1, R4 and R8.
3. **The visuals are thin, repeated or missing (slides 4–12).** The map shows the coast only, with no world-to-country zoom. Both "timelines" are lists of dates. The McCarthy photo is used twice, and Miller is never shown. Three content slides (5, 6, 7) have nothing on screen. The chat note describes a *Great Wave* image that is not in the deck. Without dual coding, the context will not stick, and a false note sends the teacher looking for an image that isn't there. This breaks R2, R3, R4, R6, R7, R13 and R11.
4. **The minutes and references don't line up (slide 13, plan steps 2–4, the plan PDF).** The read slide is set to 16 minutes against 10 in the plan. The slide ranges cited in the plan are wrong. The printable plan omits the "what to cover" lists that are the substance of the direct teaching. A teacher who prints the plan, or follows the deck timer, gets a different lesson from the one in chat. This breaks Brian's rule that a deck's minutes must add up to the lesson, and G.
5. **The arc is missing the day-1 prior-knowledge check and the return to the opening theme (starter, Short Response, closing).** Students write about belief and truth for 5 minutes and never hear about it again. The Short Response and exit ticket ask the same question, so the final writing doesn't set Miller's view beside the students' own. The opening question loses its payoff, and the lesson has no read on what students already knew. This breaks R12.2, R12.4, and alignment.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **The plan says "Senator Joseph McCarthy's Senate investigations into alleged Communist infiltration of American institutions — Hollywood, the military, the State Department."** The Hollywood investigations were HUAC's (a House committee), not McCarthy's Senate subcommittee. This is a common but real conflation, and the planner repeats it as fact. (High confidence.)
- **Miller's 1952 trigger is missing.** The plan omits that Elia Kazan named names before HUAC in April 1952, the same week Miller drove to Salem to research the play. This is the central fact for "why Miller wrote about it." The "check before class" note even questions the 1952 HUAC date, which is in fact correct (the 1951–52 hearings). (High confidence.)
- **The map note says the accusations began "in the home of the Reverend Samuel Parris" at a pin on Salem.** Parris lived in Salem Village (now Danvers), not Salem Town, where the pin is placed. (High confidence.)
- **"Miller's preface":** there is no preface. The historical commentary is in Act One's opening stage directions, and the only separate front matter is a short "A Note on the Historical Accuracy of This Play." (High confidence.)
- **The slide 5 timeline entry "Sep 1692 · 19 hanged, 1 pressed to death"** reads as if all of it happened in September. The hangings ran from June to September 22, and Giles Corey was pressed on September 19. Also, "Feb 1692 · Girls begin having fits": the fits began in January 1692. (Medium to high confidence.)
- **"The governor's own wife was accused"** is stated as fact. It is a traditional account (Lady Mary Phips) and is not documented in the court records. (Medium confidence.)
- **The deck note says "The deck uses *The Great Wave*"**, but no such image appears in the deck. (Certain, from the slide sheet.)
- No quotations from the play were used, so there are none to check.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Read slides need text.** When a step is a read-aloud or annotation, the planner must put the passage on the slide (and in the handout) when it is in the public domain or the teacher has it. For a copyrighted play, it must put a labeled slot naming the act, the opening and closing words of the passage, and the page in a common edition. It must also name in the notes the two or three sentences the close reading should land on. (R9, R10, B)
2. **Context days require the book's people and the real people's faces.** On day 1 of a unit with reading homework, the planner should add a "Who you'll meet tonight" slide naming the main characters, and it should always fetch portraits of the author and the central historical figures (Miller, McCarthy, Kazan). It should also refuse to show the same image twice. (R1, R4, R6, R8)
3. **Run a deck/plan reconciliation pass.** Each slide's minutes must sum to its plan step, slide ranges in the plan must be computed from the deck, the printable plan must carry the full "what to cover" lists, and any chat note that names a deck image must match an image actually in the deck. (Brian: minutes add up; R11; G)
4. **Arc check for unit day 1.** Require a 2–3 minute "what do you already know" check, and require the final writing (Short Response or exit ticket) to call back the starter's question so students compare their view with the text's. The exit ticket must not repeat the Short Response. (R12.2, R12.4, alignment)
5. **Fact guard on author/context lessons.** Add a check for the HUAC vs. McCarthy (House vs. Senate) distinction, Salem Village vs. Salem Town, and the author's immediate occasion for writing (Kazan, 1952). Make the map show world, then country, then place, and render dates as a visual timeline rather than a list. (R11, R2, R3)
