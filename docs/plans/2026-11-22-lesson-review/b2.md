# b2: Things Fall Apart, Day 1 (context day, 10th grade)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Build day 1 of a unit on Things Fall Apart for 10th grade, 50 minutes: Igbo society before the colonizers, and who Chinua Achebe was writing back to. Include the slide deck."

**What came back** (turn 1, 120 s): a plan titled "Day 1: The World Before the Missionaries" with an objective and six timed parts: class starter (5 min, three prompt options), "What do you already know?" (3), context from the deck (15, with three optional "frames": historical, who Achebe was writing back to, literary/postcolonial), read-aloud of Chapter 1 "through the end of the wrestling scene (roughly the first three pages)" with two tracking tasks (15), Short Response (8), closing that returns to the board list plus one exit ticket (4) = 50 min. Ends with "Want to refine or edit any part of this?". Deck: 15 slides, labelled 53 min (title, agenda, KWL talk, two maps, timeline, "no single king" big idea with photo, three-word vocab slide, Achebe portrait, Heart of Darkness big idea, Achebe quotation, "Writing back" pair talk, read slide, write slide, exit). Two photographs, two maps, one timeline. Handouts PDF: 8 pages, one prompt per page with lines, no reading or vocabulary. Printable plan: 3 pages, 15 steps. Stack after "Add everything": 141 minutes planned, 33% writing (47 min), including a phantom 50-minute "*Things Fall Apart* · English 10 ·" activity at the start.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** The arc is right: a thematic starter in students' own terms ("History is just whichever version of the past won"), a brief what-do-you-know (slide 3), short direct teaching (slides 4–11), the "Writing back" pair talk (slide 12) as the hinge into the text, then writing and a return to the opening list. The close returns to the KWL list rather than to the starter's thematic question, and the third starter ("Your life is mostly determined by where... you were born") is only loosely on theme.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** The only reading is "Chapter 1, pp. 1–3" (slide 13) of an unnamed edition; no passage is on a slide or the handout, the tracking tasks are generic ("one detail that tells you what this society values"), and "the wrestling scene" is in fact a one-paragraph summary in the novel's opening, not a scene with "real energy" (slide 13 notes). The Achebe quotation (slide 11) is unsourced and silently trimmed.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 3.** Short, mostly accurate frames (no single king, egwugwu, 1914 amalgamation, 1958 vs. 1960, Yeats title). But the vocabulary slide (slide 8, "Words for reading Chapter 1") defines egwugwu, obi and chi, words from later chapters, while the words students actually meet on pp. 1–3 (Umuofia, Amalinze the Cat, ilo, kola, harmattan, cowries, title/ozo) go undefined; and "who Achebe was writing back to" is reduced to Conrad, omitting Joyce Cary's *Mister Johnson*, the novel Achebe himself named.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R2–R4, R6, R7, R13): 3.** The best part of the deck: world-to-Nigeria map (slide 4) then the Igbo region with Lagos and Calabar (slide 5) follow R2; slide 6 is a real timeline (R3); slide 9 gives Achebe's face with a credit (R4). Against that: slide 7 illustrates pre-colonial village governance with a modern family portrait in festive dress (R6, R13); slide 10 (Heart of Darkness) has no image and no labelled slot (R7, R10); the Berlin Conference image in the timeline data never renders; and Okonkwo, Unoka and Umuofia are never introduced on screen before students read about them (R4, R8).
- **E. Writing: 3.** Students write at the start (starter), after the reading (8-minute Short Response that asks for a claim plus a detail, well aligned), and at the exit (explain why Achebe wrote the novel, with useful mustMention/watchFor). Only one exit-ticket type is offered, not Brian's three, and the 33% writing share is inflated by duplicates in the stack.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** Several real checks: one or two observations after each map and timeline, a 30-second turn-and-tell after the writing-back slide, pair share after the reading, circulating during the Short Response for plot summary vs. claim (slide 14 notes), and an exit ticket with a stated misconception to watch for.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The plan's parts add to 50, but the deck says 53 minutes, its own agenda (slide 2) adds to 46, and the stack totals 141 minutes after "Add everything" (a phantom 50-minute title step, all three starters, the 15-minute context step plus all three frames, the Short Response twice, the exit ticket twice). The handout packet is 8 pages of lines with page 1 offering three starter prompts that match none of the plan's three; plan.pdf leaves Goals blank ("Add this lesson's goals") and the Closing step begins mid-sentence ('" moment. Ask:...').
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** Genuinely teacher-friendly language and options: "pick one, or stack the first two", "If you like to cold-call readers, ask ahead", "assign it as homework if the reading ran long"; speaker notes are specific and useful (slide 6: "Pause on 1885 — ask what it means that Africans had no seat at that table"). Ends on one question. The stack and printouts, however, would confuse a teacher.
- **I. Access: 2.** A sentence stem on slide 12 ("I think it changes because..."), a teacher read-aloud option, and a three-word glossary; but the glossary misses the passage's words, there is no pronunciation help for Igbo names, no printed passage to annotate, and no frame for the Short Response.
- **Overall: 3.** A strong teacher would use most of this deck tomorrow after fixing the vocabulary slide, adding a character slide and the Cary context, and ignoring the stack and the handout packet.

## 3. Strengths

1. The maps and timeline (slides 4–6) do exactly what R2 and R3 ask: world, then Nigeria, then the Igbo region with Lagos and Calabar, and a timeline that sets the 1890s setting beside 1885, 1914, 1958 and 1960, with speaker notes that turn each into a question.
2. The framing of "writing back" is conceptually strong for 10th graders: the "Writing back" talk question (slide 12, "What changes about a story when the person telling it changes?"), the Achebe "one long night of savagery" quotation (slide 11), and a Short Response that asks what Achebe shows "before you hear anything a colonizer says" tie context directly to reading purpose (R8, R12 (4)).
3. The speaker notes and plan prose are expert and kind: realistic advice on cold-calling reluctant readers, think time, what to listen for, and a misconception to watch for in the exit ticket (Brian: "expert, kind, on the teacher's side").

## 4. Problems

1. **The reading is not prepared or supplied.** Where: slide 8 ("Words for reading Chapter 1") defines egwugwu (first appears around Ch. 10), obi and chi, with a chi example from later in the novel; slide 13 gives only "Chapter 1, pp. 1–3" with no passage; no slide introduces Okonkwo, Unoka or Umuofia. Why: students meet Umuofia, Mbaino, Amalinze the Cat, the ilo, kola and the harmattan on page 1 with no support and no names on screen, while being taught words they will not need for weeks. Principle: R4, R5, R9; close reading of short passages.
2. **"Who Achebe was writing back to" is answered incompletely and partly inaccurately.** Where: Frame 2, slides 9–10. The plan names only Conrad, says Achebe read *Heart of Darkness* in "colonial schools", and calls the novel "a direct answer" to it. Achebe repeatedly named Joyce Cary's *Mister Johnson* (read at University College, Ibadan) as the book that made him want to tell the story from the inside; his famous Conrad critique ("An Image of Africa") came in 1975, long after 1958. Why: this is the heart of the request, and students will carry an oversimplified origin story into the unit. Principle: R11; R1.
3. **Minutes do not add up anywhere except the chat plan.** Where: deck 53 min; agenda slide 2 totals 46; stack 141 min with a phantom 50-minute step titled "*Things Fall Apart* · English 10 ·" (the plan's subtitle parsed as an activity) and every alternative added. Why: a teacher who trusts the stack or deck is misled about pacing; "Add everything" turns options into a 141-minute lesson, which defeats its purpose. Principle: Brian "a deck's minutes must add up to the lesson"; feasibility.
4. **Visuals fall back to mood or nothing at key moments.** Where: slide 7 (a modern family in festive dress to illustrate pre-colonial councils and titled elders); slide 10 (Heart of Darkness, words only, no labelled slot); slide 11 (quotation with an empty "Annotate" panel); Berlin Conference image missing from slide 6. Why: the "no single king" idea is the lesson's key concept and gets no picture of the thing itself (an obi, a village assembly, an egwugwu mask, a period photograph). Principle: R6, R7, R10, R13.
5. **Printables are not ready to use.** Where: handouts p. 1 lists three starter prompts ("Think of a time someone had power over you...") that are not the plan's three; pp. 2–4 repeat the plan's starters; pp. 5–6 and 7–8 duplicate the Short Response and exit ticket; nothing for the read-aloud tracking tasks or vocabulary. plan.pdf: blank Goals, a 50-minute title "step", and a Closing step cut mid-sentence. Why: a teacher cannot print and go; students get 8 pages for one lesson. Principle: Yawp! "materials ready to print and use".

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **Achebe quotation (slide 11 and Frame 2):** from "The Novelist as Teacher" (1965). The original reads "...if my novels (especially the ones I set in the past) did no more than teach my readers..."; the slide drops the parenthetical without an ellipsis (the chat frame has "..."), and no source is given. High confidence on the wording and source.
- **Conrad as the "direct" target, read "in colonial schools":** oversimplified; Achebe credited Joyce Cary's *Mister Johnson*, read at university, as his trigger; his Conrad critique dates from 1975. Moderately high confidence.
- **"Words for reading Chapter 1" (slide 8):** egwugwu do not appear in Chapter 1 (first major appearance is the trial in Ch. 10); the chi example ("When Okonkwo says his chi is not made for great things...") paraphrases a narrated thought from Part Two (Ch. 14), not something he says. High confidence.
- **"The wrestling scene" (plan step 5, slide 13 notes):** in Chapter 1 the bout with Amalinze the Cat is a brief summary in the opening paragraphs, not a dramatized scene. High confidence.
- **Map (slide 5):** "Achebe grew up in Ogidi, near here" pinned at Enugu; Ogidi is near Onitsha in Anambra, about 80 km west. Minor. High confidence.
- **Berlin Conference "1885":** it ran November 1884 to February 1885; "divide Africa among themselves" is a common simplification (it set rules for claims). Minor.
- Correct: Achebe 1930–2013, born in Ogidi; 1914 amalgamation; 1958 publication; 1960 independence; the Yeats line "Things fall apart; the centre cannot hold" from "The Second Coming"; Achebe portrait credited (Carlo Bavagnoli, 1966).

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Derive a reading day's vocabulary and character slides from the assigned passage itself.** When a read step names a chapter or pages, the define slide should list words and names that occur in that span, and a "who's who" slide should introduce named characters and places first. (R4, R5, R8.)
2. **For "who was the author responding to" requests, require the author's own named sources with dates and a citation.** A fact-check pass should flag claims of direct response and keep quotations verbatim (ellipsis for cuts) with source on the slide. (R11.)
3. **Enforce one time budget.** Do not parse the plan's subtitle line as a step; make the deck's minutes and agenda equal the plan's minutes; and change "Add everything" so that it adds the chosen option of each alternative (or asks), not every alternative. (Brian "minutes add up"; feasibility.)
4. **When an image search does not return a picture of the thing itself, leave a labelled slot** ("Photo: an Igbo obi or village assembly, c. 1900 — add one") instead of a mood or modern stand-in, and never drop a timeline image silently. (R6, R10, R13.)
5. **Build handouts from the plan, not alongside it:** one page per lesson, starters as a single choice block matching the plan, a space for the read-aloud tracking tasks and vocabulary, no duplicate Short Response or exit-ticket pages; fill plan.pdf Goals from the stated objective. (Yawp! "ready to print and use".)
