# a3: The Odyssey, Book 9, the Cyclops: hero or reckless show-off? (9th grade, 50 min)

## 1. The request and what came back

> "Teach a 50-minute lesson for 9th grade on Book 9 of The Odyssey, the Cyclops: is Odysseus a hero here or a reckless show-off? Include the slide deck."

**What came back:** a six-step plan totalling 50 minutes: Class starter (5, three options), Context on the heroic code, *kleos*, *mētis* and Poseidon (7), Close reading of three moments in the cave (18; lines "~216–230", "~408–412", "~500–521", each with a one-minute pair question), Discussion with a Hero/Reckless evidence T-chart (10), Short Response "Hero or show-off?" (8), and Closing (2, with three exit-ticket options). The deck has 16 slides and **adds to exactly 50 minutes**. It has two distinct pictures in five placements: Hokusai's *Great Wave* on slides 1, 4, 6 and 16, and the Eleusis amphora (blinding of Polyphemus) on slide 5. The handout turn worked. "Hero or Show-Off? — Book 9 Close Reading" gives definitions of kleos and mētis, a summary and annotation tasks for each passage, an evidence chart and success criteria for the Short Response. But it appears only as a separate chat card. "Add everything to the lesson" (under the first reply) does not include it, so it is **not in the stack or the printed handouts PDF**. The handouts PDF (7 pages) is starter, Short Response and exit-ticket pages. The stack shows **80 of 50 minutes (30 over)**, "54% of class is writing · 43 min".

## 3. Strengths

1. **A genuinely good lesson design for an argument question.** Three well-chosen moments (into the cave, "Nobody", the name) each get a read, a pair question and shares. They feed a whole-class Hero/Reckless T-chart that insists on "which line puts it there", and then an individual claim. The crew's-eye question ("If you were one of the men...") is a smart reframe.
2. **The right concepts, taught briefly and before use.** *Kleos* and *mētis* (slide 4, handout) give students the vocabulary to say why Odysseus shouts his name, and exit ticket 2 checks exactly that. The Eleusis amphora (slide 5) is an excellent, era-appropriate image of the episode.
3. **Timing discipline in the deck:** 16 slides adding to exactly 50 minutes, aligned slide by slide to the plan's six steps. This is the only one of my three lessons where the deck's minutes add up (Brian).

## 4. Problems

1. **No text to close-read.** The reading slides (7, 9, 11) give line numbers only, with no translation named, and the numbers don't match any single edition. The handout replaces each passage with a summary and still asks students to "underline" and "circle" in it. This is a close-reading lesson in which students never read Homer's words. It breaks R9, R10 (the substitution is silent) and close reading of short passages.
2. **Factual slips on screen and in the plan.** "If you were one of the twelve men on that raft" (slide 14) is wrong: six of the twelve were eaten, and they escape by ship, not raft (the raft is Book 5). "Shouted his real name — twice" (plan T-chart) is wrong: he taunts twice but names himself once. The handout's sequence (wine "until he falls asleep drunk. When the Cyclops asks his name...") reverses the order. Students will repeat these in their Short Responses. This breaks R11.
3. **A decorative picture used four times.** Hokusai's *Great Wave* (slides 1, 4, 6, 16) is a Japanese print from about 1830, unrelated to Homer, and dominates the deck. Meanwhile the passage slides, where an image of the ram escape or the boulder would anchor comprehension, are bare. This breaks R6 and R13.
4. **The requested handout does not reach the classroom.** It arrives as a separate chat card that "Add everything to the lesson" ignores, so the stack and the printed packet omit it. The packet instead begins with three generic starter prompts the plan never offered, and a generic Short Response prompt. A teacher who prints the packet gets the wrong materials. This breaks "materials ready to print and use".
5. **The plan's closing and the stack do not fit the period.** Two minutes for a share, a restatement and an exit ticket is not feasible. "Add everything" turns the three starter options into 20 minutes of starters, so the stack is 80 of 50. The garbled speaker notes (slides 4 and 9) would confuse a substitute. This breaks Brian's rule that minutes must add up, and feasibility.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **"Twelve men on that raft"** (slide 14, plan step 4): six of the twelve survive the cave, and they escape to their ship, not a raft. High confidence.
- **"Escaping on the raft"** (plan step 3, slide 11, handout passage 3: "escape on a raft under the bellies of Polyphemus's sheep"): they escape the cave under the rams, then row away in their ship. High confidence.
- **"Shouted his real name — twice"** (plan, Hero/Reckless table): the first taunt is nameless. After his men beg him, the second taunt gives "Odysseus, raider of cities... Laertes' son... Ithaca." The slides' "His men beg him to stop. He shouts again" is correct. High confidence.
- **Handout passage 2 sequence:** Polyphemus asks his name *after* the wine and *before* he falls asleep (he promises to eat "Nobody" last). The handout puts sleep first. High confidence.
- **Line numbers:** "216–230" and "500–521" match the Greek line numbering (entering the cave; the name-taunt around 9.502–505). But in the Greek, 9.366–367 is where Odysseus says his name is "Nobody", while 9.408–412 is Polyphemus's "Nobody is killing me" scene. The handout quote "Nobody — that's my name" is Fagles's wording, and in Fagles the other two references fall elsewhere (the men's plea around 9.250 and the name-shout around 9.560). So no single edition matches all three references. Medium-high confidence.
- **Image credit** (slide 5): "Zde, Greek vase painting, archaic or geometric". This is the Proto-Attic Eleusis amphora (about 670–650 BCE, Eleusis Archaeological Museum), not Geometric, and "Zde" is the photographer. The credit should name the object. Medium-high confidence.
- **Mojibake:** the notes on slides 4 and 9 render *mētis* as "m7F—2—2F†R6ÆPverness" and "m7F—0". This is a rendering bug, not a content error, but it is printed.
- Correct: Poseidon as Polyphemus's father, the definitions of *kleos* and *mētis*, twelve men taken into the cave, the men begging him to take the cheese and go, and "Next time: Book 10: Circe".

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Put the actual passage on the reading slide and the handout.** Use a public-domain translation (Butler, Butcher and Lang) when the class's edition is unknown, naming the translation and the line or page reference system. When it must use a copyrighted edition, leave a labeled "paste your translation's lines here" slot. Never paraphrase and then ask students to underline the paraphrase. (R9, R10)
2. **Run a fact-check pass on narrative details before output** (who survives, which vessel, how many times something happens, the order of events), and check that line references come from one stated edition. (R11)
3. **Drop the "series art" mechanism for literature decks, or limit it to the title slide,** and spend the image budget on the subject: passage slides should carry an image of the moment itself, with a full credit naming the object and its date. (R6, R11, R13)
4. **Include handouts generated in later turns in "Add everything to the lesson", or give the handout reply its own "add to lesson" that places it in the stack.** Build the print packet only from prompts that are in the plan, never invented fillers. (Materials ready to print)
5. **Enforce a minimum closing time when an exit ticket is in the plan (about 4–5 minutes), and have "Add everything" add one option per "pick one" group.** Then the plan, deck and stack all say 50. (Brian: minutes must add up; feasibility)
