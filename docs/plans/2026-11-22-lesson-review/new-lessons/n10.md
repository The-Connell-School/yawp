# n10: Lord of the Flies, Chapter 1: First Impressions (Grade 9, 50 min)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Teach a 50-minute lesson for 9th grade on chapter 1 of Lord of the Flies: first impressions of Ralph, Piggy and Jack, and what the island promises. Include the slide deck."

**What came back:** No clarifying question. The reply took 144 s. The plan has six steps: (1) "The world they're about to enter", an oral opening on "stranded with no adults, who would take charge?" (5 min); (2) Class starter, with three Yawp! prompt options (5); (3) a read-aloud of the three character introductions, with a teacher think-aloud and a vocabulary gloss (15); (4) pair-then-whole-class discussion, "what detail did the most work?" (10); (5) a Short Response, "one detail that tells you the most about Ralph, Piggy or Jack", with three sentence starters (10); (6) Closing, an open question ("The island looks like paradise... What could go wrong?") followed by one of three exit tickets (5). Total: 50 min. The deck has 14 slides totalling 50 min, with one picture: a WWII evacuation poster on slide 4. The handout runs 4 pages: the starter; a close-reading sheet with three "[Paste ... here]" slots, since the novel is under copyright; the Short Response; and exit ticket 1. The stack shows 50 of 50 min, "Right on time", and 40% of class writing (20 min). The close-reading handout sits in the stack as a 0-minute step. `checks`: work "none", factsFlagged 2, repaired 2, no quotations verified.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** It opens on the students' own experience (Step 1 and slide 3, "No adults. Now what?"), then teaches, reads and writes. But the opening question about who takes charge never comes back to be set beside Golding's answer. The Jack vote and the conch are in Chapter 1, yet the discussion and the Short Response stay on "first impressions", and the island question (slide 13) is left open by design.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** No passage appears on any slide or handout, only paste slots (handout p.2). The slot boundaries are wrong: Passage 1 runs "from 'The boy with fair hair' through Ralph's first swim in the lagoon", which takes in Piggy's whole introduction, and the swim is in the bathing pool. Passage 3, "from 'Within the diamond haze' through the vote for chief", is several pages, not "approximately 200 words". The modelled think-aloud also misplaces a detail (see section 5).
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 2.** There is a vocabulary slide (slide 6), but its headline word "effulgence" is very likely a misremembering of Golding's "efflorescence". There is no direct teaching of the core concept the Short Response requires, indirect versus direct characterization, even though the prompt asks "what does the detail do that a direct description wouldn't?"
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R2, R3, R4, R6, R7, R13): 2.** Only 1 of 14 slides has a picture: the Ministry of Health evacuation poster on slide 4, which is apt and credited. There is no island image, though the lesson is half about "what the island promises". The slide 13 picture slot is labelled only in the notes ("Yawp! could not find one"). There is no map of where the boys might be, and nothing on the character slides (7 to 9) to look at. The slide 1 speaker notes describe Bruegel's *Children's Games* ("If a student notices, let them say what they see"), but the slide has no picture.
- **E. Writing: 3.** About 40% of class is writing. There are three starter options, a focused Short Response with sentence starters, and three exit-ticket options, one of which covers the island. But exit ticket 1 ("explain how Golding introduces one of the three boys... point to what in the text") repeats the Short Response written 5 minutes earlier. The handout's close-reading questions (p.2) have no writing space, because the blank lines collapsed in the PDF.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** There is a think-aloud at two points, a pause after the Jack reading ("tell me how he made you feel"), a pair share with one detail per character, and circulation during the Short Response. Nothing checks whether students understood the vocabulary or the island's promise before the exit.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The minutes add up (plan 50, deck 50, stack 50). But the teacher must find and paste three passages, and two of them are mis-bounded. The p.2 questions have no answer space. The close-reading handout is a 0-minute stack step with no time to complete it. Reading three introductions aloud and annotating them in 15 minutes is tight.
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** It offers options, not prescriptions (three starters, three exit tickets, "or assign it as homework"), and the notes are warm and specific ("He's playing. He's not scared."). Against that, the slide 1 notes refer to a painting that isn't there, the plan's "Precentor" is missing from the vocabulary slide, and the printable plan PDF truncates every step to its first sentence or two.
- **I. Access: 3.** It offers sentence starters, pairing with a stronger reader, an audio option, and a sensible entry point ("start with Jack"). There is no chunked or glossed text, because there is no text.
- **Overall: 2.** The structure is sound and the teacher voice is good. But a strong teacher would have to supply every passage, fix the vocabulary slide and the think-aloud detail, and build the island half of the lesson themselves.

## 3. Strengths

1. The opening builds a real bridge from the students' lives to the novel. Slide 3 ("If you and your classmates were stranded... who would take charge?") and the starter's "Hold that. Watch what Golding does with the same question" set up exactly the tension Chapter 1 dramatizes.
2. The discussion is about detail, not summary: "Each pair picks ONE detail per character (not a summary — a specific word, action, or line)". The watchFor line on the exit ticket names the right misconception ("summarizing what happens rather than saying what a detail reveals").
3. The WWII evacuation poster on slide 4 is a genuinely apt context image (R6). It shows students why British schoolboys are flying anywhere at all.

## 4. Problems

1. **Half the request is missing: "what the island promises" gets no text.**
   - Where: plan steps 3 to 5, slides 7 to 12, handout p.2.
   - All three passages and all the writing are about the boys. The island appears only as a 2-minute unresolved question (slide 13) and as exit ticket option 2, yet the objective says students will "explain what the island represents". Ralph's "This belongs to us" from the mountain top, the scar, the platform and the bathing pool are not read at all.
   - Why it matters: students cannot meet half the objective, and the exit ticket that measures it has no instruction behind it.
   - Breaks: objective alignment, R12(4).
2. **Wrong vocabulary and a misplaced detail on screen and in the think-aloud.**
   - Where: slide 6 and plan step 3.
   - Slide 6 teaches "Effulgence... Golding uses it for the lagoon". The Chapter 1 word, describing the bathing pool, is almost certainly "efflorescence" ("bright with the efflorescence of tropical weed and coral").
   - The modelled think-aloud (plan step 3, slide 7 notes) has Ralph "stood on his head in the shallow water". In the novel he stands on his head in the scar, right after realizing there are no grown-ups, before any swim.
   - Why it matters: the teacher models close reading with a detail that is not on the page, and students learn a word the book does not use.
   - Breaks: R11, R5.
3. **No text and wrong paste instructions.**
   - Where: handout p.2, slides 7 to 9.
   - The labelled slots honour R10, but the boundaries given would make Passage 1 overlap Passage 2, and Passage 3 would run several pages. The reading slides say only "Follow along" with no page or paragraph pointer.
   - Why it matters: a teacher on a busy morning pastes the wrong chunk or none at all, and students annotate nothing.
   - Breaks: R9, R10, close reading of short passages.
4. **The deck is nearly all words.**
   - Where: slides 1 to 14.
   - Only slide 4 has a picture. There is nothing for the island, though R13 asks for "a picture of the thing itself": a lagoon, coral reef, palm-fringed beach or pink granite. The slide 13 image request lives only in the notes, and the slide 1 notes describe a Bruegel painting that was quietly dropped.
   - Why it matters: ninth graders get no picture of the setting whose "promise" they are asked to judge.
   - Breaks: R6, R7, R13, R10.
5. **Duplicated writing tasks and no space to write.**
   - Where: Short Response (step 5), exit ticket 1, handout p.2.
   - Students write "one detail... what Golding wants you to understand" and then, 5 minutes later, "explain how Golding introduces one of the three boys... point to what in the text". That is the same task twice, while the island goes unwritten. The handout questions on p.2 have no lines.
   - Why it matters: time is spent re-measuring one skill, and the printed sheet isn't usable.
   - Breaks: Yawp! values (ready to print, room to write), alignment.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **"Effulgence" (slide 6, plan step 3).** Golding's Chapter 1 word for the pool is "efflorescence" ("bright with the efflorescence of tropical weed and coral"). It describes the bathing pool, not the lagoon. Confidence: fairly high.
- **"Ralph 'stood on his head' in the shallow water" (plan step 3, slide 7 notes).** In Chapter 1, Ralph "stood on his head and grinned at the reversed fat boy" in the scar, on land, after "No grown-ups!". Confidence: high.
- **Handout passage boundaries.** Passage 1 as defined contains Piggy's introduction. Passage 3 is far longer than 200 words. Confidence: high.
- **"Decorous" (slide 6).** It is presented as describing the choir's march. I cannot confirm the word appears in Chapter 1. Confidence: low; worth a check.
- Correct: 1954, Jack's red hair and black cap and cloak, "I ought to be chief", "Within the diamond haze of the beach", "The boy with fair hair".

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Check every planned passage against the request's stated focus.** When a request names two aims (characters and island), the planner should require at least one passage and one writing task per aim before finalizing. [objective alignment, R12(4)]
2. **Verify glossary words and modelled details against the source.** Vocabulary words and quoted actions used in think-alouds should be verified against the text, or, for a copyrighted text, flagged "verify in your edition" when they cannot be. The `checks` object shows no quotation verification ran here. [R11, R5]
3. **Generate correct, short paste slots for copyrighted passages.** Paste instructions should give a precise first line and last line, with a word count that matches. The planner should check that slots don't overlap, and put the same pointer (chapter and paragraph cue) on each reading slide. [R9, R10]
4. **Render `&nbsp;` answer space in handouts.** Answer space under handout questions is lost in the PDF; render it as real ruled lines. [Yawp! values: room to write]
5. **Keep speaker notes in sync with the pictures.** Speaker notes should never refer to a picture that isn't on the slide. When an image search fails, put a visible labelled slot on the slide itself, not only in the notes, and require a picture of the setting on any slide that teaches it. [R10, R13]
