# n07 — Night, Day 1 context day (10th grade, 50 min)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Build day 1 of a unit on Night by Elie Wiesel for 10th grade, 50 minutes: what students need to know before they read, handled with care. Include the slide deck."

**What came back:** No clarifying question; one turn (126 s). The plan opens with an objective and a strong teacher "care note" (alert the counselor, an opt-out script, no personal-trauma sharing without distance), then five steps: Opening "What do you know?" (5), Direct teaching "The world of Night" (20: where, when, who, words, the author), Reading "The first page" read aloud by the teacher (10), Class starter quick write with three prompt options (7), Closing on a Wiesel quotation plus homework and an "explain a concept" exit ticket (8) = 50 min. Deck: 13 slides (title, agenda, map, timeline, people, two vocabulary slides, "big idea", read slide, pair-talk, write, quotation, exit), slide minutes summing to 50. Handouts: 2 pages (the "What gets taken" starter prompt and the exit ticket, each with ruled lines). Stack: 50 minutes, 5 steps in plan order, "30% of class is writing · 15 min" (starter + closing). Printable plan: 2 pages.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 2.** The opening (step 1) does find out what students know (R12.2), but it never opens on the theme in students' own experience, which R12.1 says is required on day 1; the one personal, thematic write ("What gets taken") sits at minute 35 and is still labeled a "class starter". The closing quotation does tie the context back to a thematic question, so the arc's last move is present, just with the first move misplaced.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** The only contact with the book is the teacher reading "the opening paragraph" aloud (step 3, slide 9): the slide holds no text, no page reference, and no labeled slot for the passage (R9, R10), yet slide 10 asks pairs "What does Wiesel choose to tell us on the very first page?", which they have heard once and cannot see. The closing quotation is misattributed (see section 5).
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 3.** Slides 6–7 define ghetto, deportation, concentration camp, testimony and witness in plain student language with a Night-specific example each, and the "novel vs. testimony" question is the right conceptual frame. Accuracy has gaps: slide 3 places Sighet in "what is now Romania" while slide 4 says "Hungarian Jews" were deported, without ever explaining that Sighet was annexed by Hungary in 1940 (which the book itself depends on: Hungarian police, the 1944 German occupation); slide 6 says "the Nazis created a ghetto" in Sighet when Hungarian authorities did; "1941 The Final Solution" is loose.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R2–R4, R6, R7, R13): 2.** Genuine positives: a real map with pins for Sighet, Auschwitz and Berlin (slide 3) and a real horizontal timeline (slide 4). But the map starts at Europe, not the world (R2), and Buchenwald is "not on the map"; the people slide (5) shows a face only for Wiesel, and an elderly one at that, with Shlomo, Tzipora and "Moshe" as text only (R4); the same Wiesel portrait is reused on slide 8 (R6); slide 10 uses a modern colour photo of Sighet's town hall (EU flag visible) although the planner asked for the town "as it looked before 1944"; vocabulary slides 6–7 have no picture of a ghetto, a deportation train or a camp (R13).
- **E. Writing: 3.** The three starter prompts are thoughtful and distanced ("a place, a group, a way of life... taken from them"), and the exit ticket ("explain what kind of book Night is and why Wiesel wrote it") measures the objective's core idea, with good main points and "watch for" notes. Only one exit-ticket option is offered (Brian asks for three), and the real writing time is about 5 + 3 minutes, not the 15 the stack claims.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** There are useful checks (opening show of hands, "What surprised you?" after the deck, the "leaving vs. being deported" question on slide 6, exit tickets sorted into "testimony" and "historical fiction" piles for tomorrow). But the 20-minute direct-teaching block has no student response on the map or timeline content itself.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The plan adds to 50 and so does the deck, but the deck's minutes don't match the plan's sections: slide 1 (a title slide) is given 12 minutes against a 5-minute opening, direct teaching gets 18 slide-minutes against 20, and reading gets 6 against 10. The printable plan drops the care note, all the direct-teaching content after "Where", the closing quotation and the homework, and calls the minute-35 class starter something that "gets every student writing in the first minutes". The handouts are ready to print.
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** The care note and speaker notes are expert and humane ("These are real people. Not characters — people."; hold 30 seconds of silence), and there are real options (three starters, a student reader "but ask ahead, never cold"). The stack mislabels step 5 with the exit ticket's topic ("what kind of book Night is and why...") and leaks markdown into a step title ("The world of *Night*").
- **I. Access: 2.** There is one sentence stem (slide 10) and five defined terms. There are no guided notes for a 20-minute lecture, no printed text for students to follow during the read-aloud (a serious barrier for English learners), and no visual glossary.
- **Overall: 2.** A strong teacher would keep the care note, the vocabulary slides, the map, the timeline and the exit ticket, but would have to reorder the arc, supply the first-page text, rebuild the people slide, and correct a quotation they would otherwise teach to 10th graders with the wrong source.

## 3. Strengths

1. The care note in the plan and the slide 2 notes ("This book is hard. It is supposed to be... that is never a sign of weakness") is exactly the right way to handle a Holocaust text, and the starter prompts keep the "distance" the note promises.
2. Testimony vs. novel (slide 7 and the exit ticket's "watch for: students calling it a novel or historical fiction") is the single most important framing for reading Night, and the exit-ticket sorting routine sets up Day 2.
3. A real map and a real timeline, with notes that use them ("It is not close to Germany. The war came to him.").

## 4. Problems

1. **The closing quotation is misattributed.** *Where:* plan step 5 and slide 12 (notes: "This is a paraphrase of Wiesel's Nobel acceptance speech (1986) — tell students that"). *Why it matters:* "To forget the dead would be akin to killing them a second time" is Wiesel's own wording from his preface to the 2006 Marion Wiesel translation of Night, not a paraphrase of the Nobel speech. The teacher is told to tell students the wrong source, in a unit whose subject is bearing accurate witness. *Principle:* R11, Brian (quotations exactly right).
2. **There is a reading step with no reading.** *Where:* step 3, slides 9–10. *Why it matters:* students hear the opening paragraph once and are then asked to discuss word choice and sentence length ("Notice how he writes... the length of his sentences") without the text in front of them. There is no labeled slot for the copyrighted paragraph and no page reference, so close reading isn't possible and English learners are shut out. *Principle:* R9, R10, close reading, access.
3. **The arc is out of order.** *Where:* steps 1–4. *Why it matters:* the lesson opens with a show-of-hands knowledge check, and the thematic personal write ("What gets taken") comes at minute 35, labeled "class starter"; the printable plan even claims it "gets every student writing in the first minutes". On day 1 the theme should come first, so that the context lands on something students already feel. *Principle:* R12.1, Yawp! value (the starter starts the class).
4. **Faces and pictures are thin and repeated.** *Where:* slide 5 (only Wiesel has a photo, an elderly portrait; father, sister and Moishe are text only, and mother Sarah and sisters Hilda and Bea, named in the plan, are missing), slide 8 (the same portrait again), slide 10 (a modern town-hall photo standing in for 1944 Sighet), slides 6–7 (no images). *Why it matters:* students meet the people of Night as a column of text, and the vocabulary they most need (ghetto, deportation, camp) isn't shown. Archival photos exist and would be appropriate here. *Principle:* R4, R6, R13.
5. **The deck's timing and the printable plan disagree with the plan.** *Where:* slide 1 (12 min), slides 9–10 (6 min against a 10-minute reading step), printable plan pages 1–2. *Why it matters:* a teacher running from the deck or a substitute running from the printout gets a different lesson: no care note, no closing discussion, no homework. *Principle:* Brian (deck minutes add up to the lesson), Feasibility.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- "To forget the dead would be akin to killing them a second time" is attributed on slide 12 as a paraphrase of the 1986 Nobel acceptance speech. It is a direct quotation from Wiesel's preface to the 2006 new translation of Night (high confidence). The plan's own label "(paraphrased...)" sits beside quotation marks, which contradicts itself.
- Slide 6, ghetto example: "In Sighet, the Nazis created a ghetto." The Sighet ghettos (April 1944) were set up by Hungarian authorities after the German occupation of Hungary (fairly confident).
- Slides 3–4: nothing explains that Sighet was part of Hungary in 1944 (annexed 1940), so "Sighet... now Romania" and "Hungarian Jews... deported" read as a contradiction. This is an omission rather than an error, but it will confuse students (high confidence).
- Slide 8 and the plan: "he said silence felt like a second crime" is presented as something Wiesel said; I know of no such statement in those words. It looks like a gloss on the "killing them a second time" line (moderate confidence it's unsourced).
- Slide 5: "Moshe the Beadle". The 2006 translation has "Moishe the Beadle" and the 1960 Rodway translation has "Moché"; "Moshe" matches neither edition students will hold (high confidence).
- Slide 4: "1941 The Final Solution — Nazi leadership plans the genocide" is defensible but imprecise; the Wannsee Conference was in January 1942 (moderate).
- Correct: Shlomo, Sarah, Hilda, Bea, Tzipora; Wiesel aged 15 at Auschwitz and 16 at liberation (Buchenwald, April 1945); roughly 10,000 Jews in Sighet.
- The planner's `checks` report 3 facts flagged and 3 repaired, but none of the errors above were caught.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Verify a quotation's source along with its wording.** When a quotation carries an attribution ("Nobel speech", "interview"), check the source as well as the words, and never let "paraphrased" sit beside quotation marks. *(R11, Brian)*
2. **A context day still opens on the theme.** On day 1 of a unit, generate a theme-in-experience opening (or put the personal class starter first) before the know-what-you-know check, and never place a step labeled "class starter" after minute 10. *(R12.1, Yawp! values)*
3. **A "read" slide needs the reading or a labeled slot.** For a copyrighted text, a read slide must carry a labeled "paste the opening paragraph here (page __ in your class edition)" slot, and the plan should say so. Questions about word choice or sentence length shouldn't be generated without visible text. *(R9, R10)*
4. **Fill the people slide and stop reusing images.** Every named person on a "people" slide gets a face (archival photo, or a labeled empty slot); one image may not appear on two slides; an image search should reject results that contradict the "shows" request (modern vs. 1944). *(R4, R6, R10)*
5. **Make the deck's minutes and the printable plan follow the plan.** Slide minutes should be derived from plan step minutes (no 12-minute title slide), and the printable plan should carry the care note, the full direct-teaching content, the closing and the homework. *(Brian: deck minutes add up; Feasibility)*
