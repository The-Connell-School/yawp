# n08 — Animal Farm unit plan (9th grade, 3 weeks, 50-minute periods)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Build a unit plan for Animal Farm for 9th grade: three weeks, 50-minute periods, ending in an argumentative essay on who is to blame for what happens on the farm."

**What came back:** No clarifying question; one turn (93 s). The reply opens with a three-sentence overview (Week 1 reading and discussion, Week 2 writing moves, Week 3 drafting and revision) and the six class-starter prompts written for the unit (Days 1, 4, 7, 9, 11, 14). Then comes a 15-day unit card, "Animal Farm: Who Is to Blame?". Each day has an objective, the reading, the in-class work, a "why" and a check, and each is 50 minutes. Reading runs Days 1–7 (Chapter 1 aloud in class, then Chapters 2–9 mostly as homework, then Chapter 10 aloud on Day 7). Days 8–11 teach thesis, topic sentences, evidence and analysis through Yawp! lessons; Day 12 is drafting, Day 13 hooks and conclusions, Day 14 peer review, Day 15 submission and debrief. The reply ends with three questions for the teacher (arc, prompt, reading at home) and a "Check before class" note. There is no deck and no handout (expected for a unit plan); the stack is empty ("0 of 50 minutes", no "Add everything" button), since a unit plan is built day by day with "Build this day". The printable unit plan runs 4 pages.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** At the unit level the arc is sound: Day 1 opens on the theme in students' own lives ("Think of a leader you've trusted...") and a brief "what do you already know" share, then introduces Orwell and the allegory. The central question is carried through the Day 3 one-sentence claim, which is "revisited on Day 14", and the Day 15 debrief asks "did anyone change their mind?" Day 1 is overloaded, though (starter, share, context, read Chapter 1 aloud, discussion, check, all in 50 minutes), and the summary's "Week 1 reads" is inaccurate: reading runs into Week 2 (Day 7).
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** No passage is named anywhere; it's always "key Squealer speeches", "Boxer's key scenes", "the Commandments passages", with no chapter-and-moment list a teacher could pull from. Worse, the reading sequence is wrong in two places: Day 5 debates Boxer as victim, complicit or hero before students have read his betrayal and death (Chapter 9, assigned on Day 6), and Day 6 asks students to write "the final, single Commandment from memory" before they have read Chapter 10, where it appears.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 3.** The writing instruction is explicit and well sequenced, with modeling: Day 8 "teacher works through the rule and four examples together", Day 10 "teacher models selecting and integrating one quote from Chapter 5", Day 11 "models the full claim–evidence–analysis sequence on one paragraph about Boxer". Literary instruction is thinner: allegory and the Russian Revolution get one clause on Day 1, and no key terms (propaganda, allegory, satire, totalitarianism) are listed for definition.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding: n/a.** This is a unit plan with no deck.
- **E. Writing: 3.** There is a lot of genuine writing: six well-made thematic starters (Day 7's "If someone lies to you and you believe them, who is responsible... the liar or you?" is exactly on the essay question), short responses on Days 4 and 7, a staged thesis → topic sentences → evidence → analysis build, an in-class draft and revision. But the "argumentative essay" became a "Thesis-Driven Essay" with no day on counterclaim and rebuttal, which is the defining move of argument. Only 6 of 15 days have a class starter, and there is no running evidence log for blame during the reading week.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** Every day ends with a concrete check, most of them written (Day 2 "one thing the animals gained and one thing they gave up without knowing it"; Day 5 "where does Boxer sit on the blame spectrum"). The Day 6 check is broken (see B), and the Day 12–13 checks are just "submitted".
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** As written, Days 5 and 6 can't be taught, because the discussion and the check depend on chapters students haven't read. The homework load is uneven (Chapters 7–8 and then 8–9 on consecutive nights, with Chapter 8 assigned twice). Day 1 is too full. The printable plan and the chat card cut in-class descriptions off mid-sentence with "…" (e.g., Day 1 "students meet Old Major and discuss his…"; Day 8 "apply the 'someone could argue…"), so the printout is missing the very instructions it is for.
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** The teacher is consulted rather than dictated to (three explicit choices: pacing, prompt breadth, reading at home vs. in class), and every day has "Build this day". But the reply leaks an internal checker note to the teacher ("Check before class: 'Day 6 — ...students write the final, single Commandment…': Not an error; ignore."), which is both confusing and wrong, and it closes on four questions rather than one.
- **I. Access: 2.** Nothing addresses struggling readers or English learners: no chapter reading guides, no audio option, no vocabulary per chapter, no sentence frames for the thesis or claim, no chunked versions of the key speeches. The only nod is "if your students reliably don't do the reading at home, let me know."
- **Overall: 3.** The unit's spine (thematic starters, a blame question carried from Day 3 to Day 15, a modeled writing sequence) is what a good department would want. A strong teacher would still have to fix the chapter sequencing on Days 5–6, add a counterclaim day and an evidence log, and name the passages before using it.

## 3. Strengths

1. The thematic class starters are excellent and tied to the essay question: trust in leaders (Day 1), going along (Day 4), the liar or the believer (Day 7), rules "always" having been that way (Day 9), silence as agreement (Day 11), whom we blame first (Day 14). Students arrive at the essay with their own thinking on paper (R12.1 and R12.4 at unit scale).
2. Spacing and retrieval: the Day 3 one-sentence claim ("who is more to blame, Napoleon or Snowball? Save it") is revisited on Day 14, and the Day 5 "blame spectrum" asks students to place the followers, not just the leaders.
3. The writing week follows I do / we do / you do with models: the Day 8 rule plus four examples, the Day 10 modeled quote integration, the Day 11 modeled full paragraph on Boxer, and the "claim vs. announcement vs. fact" sort on Day 9.

## 4. Problems

1. **The reading sequence contradicts the lessons.** *Where:* Day 5 ("Boxer and the Cost of Loyalty", reading Chapters 7–8) and Day 6 (check: "write the final, single Commandment from memory"; reading through Chapter 9). *Why it matters:* Boxer's collapse and sale to the knacker are in Chapter 9, so the Day 5 "victim, complicit or hero" debate happens without the scene that decides it. "All animals are equal, but some animals are more equal than others" is in Chapter 10, which is read on Day 7, so the Day 6 check asks students to remember something they haven't read. *Principle:* R11, alignment (the check measures what was taught).
2. **The planner's own checker caught the Day 6 error, dismissed it, and showed the dismissal to the teacher.** *Where:* end of the reply, "Check before class: ... Not an error; ignore." (`checks`: factsFlagged 1, repaired 0, leftForTeacher 1). *Why it matters:* the teacher is told to ignore the one real error that was found, and the internal note reads like a bug. *Principle:* R11; Brian (expert, on the teacher's side).
3. **The argumentative essay has no counterargument.** *Where:* Days 8–15. *Why it matters:* the request was an argumentative essay on blame, a question with several defensible answers that the reply itself lists (Napoleon, Squealer, the animals' passivity, Old Major). Ninth graders need a taught counterclaim-and-rebuttal move to argue "primary responsibility". The unit silently switches to a "Thesis-Driven Essay" and never teaches it. *Principle:* clear objective and alignment.
4. **No passages are named, and no evidence is gathered during reading.** *Where:* Days 2–7 ("key passages", "key Squealer speeches", "Boxer's key scenes"). *Why it matters:* the essay depends on evidence, but students keep no blame log or quotation tracker while reading, and on Day 10 they "reread their assigned chapters" to hunt for quotes after the fact. The teacher gets no list of the scenes the unit depends on (the Cowshed, Snowball's expulsion, the confessions and executions in Chapter 7, the milk and apples, the knacker's van). Chapter 7's executions, central to any blame argument, are never mentioned. *Principle:* R9, close reading, spacing and connection to the unit.
5. **There are no access supports and the printouts are truncated.** *Where:* the whole unit, and printable plan pages 1–4 (descriptions cut off with "…"). *Why it matters:* a novel read largely as homework, with no reading guides, audio, chapter vocabulary or sentence frames, will leave struggling readers and English learners behind by Day 3. A printed plan with half-sentences can't be handed to a co-teacher or substitute. *Principle:* scaffolding and access; Yawp! (materials ready to print and use).

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- Day 5: Boxer's "key scenes" assigned through Chapter 8. His collapse, his removal in the knacker's van ("Alfred Simmonds, Horse Slaughterer and Glue Boiler") and Squealer's deathbed lie are in Chapter 9 (high confidence).
- Day 6: the single remaining Commandment ("ALL ANIMALS ARE EQUAL / BUT SOME ANIMALS ARE MORE EQUAL THAN OTHERS") appears in Chapter 10, not by the end of Chapter 9 (high confidence). The checker flagged it and labeled it "Not an error".
- Day 6 reading lists "Chapter 8 (Commandments scenes)", but Chapter 8 was already assigned for Day 5. The Commandment changes are spread across Chapter 6 (beds), Chapter 8 (killing "without cause", drinking "to excess") and Chapter 10, so "Chapter 8 and 9" is an incomplete locator (high confidence).
- Day 7 paraphrase of the last image ("looking from pig to man and back again") is accurate in substance; the text reads "from pig to man, and from man to pig, and from pig to man again" (high confidence).
- Day 3: "Chapter 5 through the expulsion of Snowball, reread in class", but Chapter 5 was never assigned before it is "reread" (minor; high confidence).
- No other errors found in the names (Old Major, Napoleon, Snowball, Squealer, Boxer, Jones) or the chapter content.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Check every day against the chapters assigned so far.** Before a unit plan is shown, check each day's discussion and check against a chapter-to-event index of the text, and reject any day that refers to an event in a chapter not yet assigned. *(R11, alignment)*
2. **Resolve flags or show them correctly; never show "Not an error; ignore".** When the accuracy checker flags something, either repair it from the source or show it as an open question. Internal verdicts should never reach the teacher, and a flag shouldn't be dismissed without checking the source text. *(R11, Brian)*
3. **Argumentative-essay units must teach counterclaim and rebuttal.** When the request says "argumentative", require a day (or half-day) on counterclaim and rebuttal, plus a running evidence log (here, a "blame log") started on Day 1 and used on evidence day. *(Alignment, spacing, retrieval)*
4. **Name the passages.** For each reading day, a unit plan should list the two or three specific scenes or speeches (chapter and moment) the in-class close read uses, so "key passages" becomes something a teacher can act on. *(R9, close reading)*
5. **Build access in by default, and never truncate the printable plan.** Give each reading day a short reading guide or audio note, chapter vocabulary and a sentence frame for the day's claim; the printable unit plan must print full text, without "…" cut-offs. *(Access; Yawp! ready-to-print)*
