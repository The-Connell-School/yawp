# n04: "The Tell-Tale Heart": the unreliable narrator (English 9)

**What came back:** The planner answered in one turn (198 s, no clarifying question). The plan has six steps: Class starter (5 min, three prompt options), "What is an unreliable narrator?" mini-lesson (7), Close reading: three passages, three signals (18, I do / we do / you do), Short response "The case against the narrator" (12), Share and land the idea (5), and Exit ticket (3, three options: understand text, explain concept, ask a question). ## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** The opening is in students' own experience ("Have you ever doubted someone's story?"), then a short direct-teaching piece, then modeled-to-independent close reading, writing, and a landing question ("If the narrator is unreliable, who is telling us the truth?"). R12(4) is only gestured at: the starter is "held onto", but students never return to their own doubted-story moment and set it beside Poe's narrator.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 3.** Passages 1 and 2 are real, accurate, and well chosen (the opening's "why will you say that I am mad?" and the vulture-eye "motive"), and the Passage 1 think-aloud is specific and good. But the "you do" passage, the climax, is not supplied. It is a paste slot even though Poe is public domain. The plan also mislocates it as "the final paragraph, beginning 'I felt that I must scream or die!'"; that sentence sits inside the penultimate paragraph.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 3.** The definition of an unreliable narrator is accurate and short, and the three lens questions ("What is this person trying to convince me of? Why so hard? What do they leave out?") are excellent. None of Poe's hard vocabulary for 9th graders is defined anywhere (dissemble, hearken, sagacity, vex, suavity, acute), and the passage-1 quote itself contains "acute" just after the excerpt ends.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R2–R4, R6, R7, R13): 1.** There is no deck, though one was requested. There is not a single image (no Poe portrait, no 1843 *Pioneer* first-printing page, nothing).
- **E. Writing: 4.** 40% of class is writing. The class starter, short response, and exit ticket are all Yawp! assignments. The short response ("what does this moment reveal about the narrator that he doesn't intend to reveal?") is tightly aligned to the objective, and the exit ticket "understand text" option measures exactly the objective.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 2.** The plan's main check is "put the two-sentence quiz question on the deck (slide 5) and ask students to vote with fingers (1 or 2)", but the deck doesn't exist and the quiz question is never written anywhere. Circulating during Passage 3 with a named look-for is good, but there is no planned check after Passage 2's pair discussion.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The minutes add to 50 and the stack matches, but the materials are not ready to print. The handout puts the Passage 1 quote above the three questions and leaves nothing under the "Passage 1" header (page 2). It prints a literal "**This moment reveals…". The "[____]" blank expands into three full pages of ruled lines (pages 3–5). There is no writing room under Passages 1–2. Passage 3 is a paste slot. The plan PDF says "Goals: Add this lesson's goals and any standards it meets" even though the plan states an objective, and step 3 is cut off at "Read aloud: 'True!".
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** The starter and exit ticket come with real options, the "watchFor" notes are expert ("'he's unreliable because he's crazy'… that's the conclusion, not the evidence"), and the 90-second reteach contingency is good. But the plan tells the teacher to "project the deck from this point" and refers to "slide 5" and "the exit slide", none of which exist. The dangling "Here's the deck to go with it:" will confuse a teacher.
- **I. Access: 2.** There is one sentence frame ("This moment reveals that the narrator ___"), plus pair work. There is no glossary, no chunked or glossed version of the climax, no audio or read-aloud plan for the full story, and no ELL support for 1843 diction.
- **Overall: 2.** The plan text is a strong skeleton that a good teacher would happily build from. It is not usable tomorrow because the requested deck is missing, the handout is broken in print, and the key passage is absent.

## 3. Strengths

1. The Passage 1 think-aloud is genuine modeling: "He opens by denying something nobody asked… A person who isn't worried about being believed doesn't start there." It names a transferable move and has students mark it ("answers unasked question").
2. The three lens questions plus the I do / we do / you do progression across three passages build cleanly toward the written claim. The short response comes after the evidence-gathering, and the plan says why.
3. The exit-ticket trio follows Brian's pattern exactly (check understanding, explain a term, ask a question), each with main points and sharp misconception notes ("Confusing 'unreliable' with 'lying on purpose'").

## 4. Problems

1. **The requested slide deck was not produced** (reply after step 6, stack "No deck yet", slides.pdf 404). The teacher asked for it by name and the reply claims to supply it ("Here's the deck to go with it:"). Students get no projected passage, no definition on screen, and no quiz slide. This breaks R7, R13 and the request itself, and it silently fails R10 (never substitute or omit quietly).
2. **The climax passage is missing from the student handout** (handout p. 2, Passage 3: "[Paste the final paragraph of your edition here…]"). The "you do" step, which is the most important evidence for the objective, can't happen as printed, and the text is public domain so there was no reason to omit it. The anchor is also mislocated: "I felt that I must scream or die!" is mid-way through the penultimate paragraph, not the start of the final one. This breaks R9 and R11.
3. **The handout PDF is not print-ready** (pages 2–5). The quote is printed above the questions and the "Passage 1" header is empty, there is a literal "**" markdown, three pages of runaway ruled lines come from the underscore blank, and there is no room to write under Passages 1 and 2 (the `&nbsp;` spacers collapsed). A teacher copying 30 of these wastes 90+ sheets and has to explain the layout. This breaks Yawp!'s ready-to-print value.
4. **The check for understanding points at material that doesn't exist** (step 2: "the two-sentence quiz question on the deck (slide 5)"). No quiz question is written anywhere, so the one planned whole-class check before close reading can't run. This breaks checking for understanding and Brian's "a deck's minutes must add up" (the plan is built around a deck it doesn't have).
5. **There is no plan for whether students have read the story, and no vocabulary support.** The plan never says whether "The Tell-Tale Heart" was read for homework or how students meet the whole text. Three excerpts in 18 minutes presume a prior reading, and Passage 3 is a spoiler otherwise. Poe's diction (dissemble, hearken, sagacity, vex) is never glossed for 9th graders or ELLs. This breaks R5 and scaffolding/access.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- Passage 1 ("True! — nervous — very, very dreadfully nervous I had been and am; but why will you say that I am mad? The disease had sharpened my senses — not destroyed — not dulled them.") is accurate.
- Passage 2 ("He had the eye of a vulture — a pale blue eye, with a film over it. Whenever it fell upon me, my blood ran cold; and so by degrees — very gradually — I made up my mind to take the life of the old man") is accurate. It truncates mid-sentence (the original continues "and thus rid myself of the eye forever") without an ellipsis on the handout.
- **Misplacement:** "the final paragraph, beginning 'I felt that I must scream or die!'" is wrong. That sentence is near the end of the penultimate paragraph; the final paragraph begins "'Villains!' I shrieked."
- "I heard all things in the heaven and in the earth" is accurate, but it comes from the opening paragraph, not the confession. The plan's phrasing ("the contradiction between…") is fine, though students looking for it in Passage 3 won't find it.
- Confidence: high.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. When the request says "include the slide deck", the reply must not finish without the deck. Gate the "done" on the deck being built, or at minimum replace "Here's the deck to go with it:" with an explicit, labeled notice that the deck still has to be built and a working button. (R7, R10, request fidelity)
2. Supply public-domain passages in full from the verified source (the checker already matched "poe-tales"). Reserve paste slots for copyrighted text, and verify passage boundaries, such as "begins with…" and paragraph location, against the source. (R9, R10, R11)
3. Fix handout rendering: collapse runs of underscores into a fixed number of writing lines, honor `&nbsp;` spacing as answer space, render markdown bold inside fill-in lines, and keep blockquotes under their own header. Add a page-count sanity check, so that a "4 pages" handout that renders to 7 is flagged. (ready-to-print)
4. Never reference a slide number or quiz in the plan unless that slide or quiz text exists in the output. When a check depends on the deck, write the question into the plan step itself. (checking for understanding, Brian's deck-minutes rule)
5. For any older or difficult literary text at grades 9–10, auto-generate a short glossary of the excerpt's hard words (on the handout and, when there is one, on the slide before the passage), and state in step 1 how students meet the whole text (homework, read-aloud, audio). (R5, access)
