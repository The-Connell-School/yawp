# n11: Letter from Birmingham Jail: How King Answers the Clergymen (Grade 11, rhetorical analysis)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Teach a 50-minute rhetorical analysis lesson for 11th grade on Martin Luther King Jr.'s "Letter from Birmingham Jail": how he answers the clergymen. Include the slide deck."

**What came back:** No clarifying question. The reply took 213 s. The plan has six steps: (1) an opening and class starter, "someone criticized a choice you held", with three options (7 min); (2) context taught directly, with five points and two key terms: rhetorical situation, and concession and rebuttal (8); (3) close reading of three charges (outsider, extreme, breaking the law), with the teacher modelling the first and pairs doing the other two (20); (4) a mini-lesson on analysis versus summary, "which means that..." (7); (5) a Short Response (8); (6) Closing and one of three exit tickets (5). **Total: 55 min, for a 50-minute request.** The deck has 14 slides, labelled "55 min", with three pictures: a fire-hose photo, an icon of the Apostle Paul, and a portrait of Lincoln. The handout runs 6 pages: the starter; the three-excerpt sheet; the Short Response; exit ticket 1. Page 3 is almost blank because of a page break. The stack shows "55 minutes planned", the period length unset ("—minute period"), and 36% writing (20 min). It also lists the handouts as "4 pages", but the PDF has 6. `checks`: work "none", factsFlagged 2, repaired 2, no quotations verified.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** The arc is clear. It opens on the students' experience of being publicly criticized (slide 3), teaches context and terms directly (slides 4 and 5), and moves to close reading with I do / we do (slides 6 to 10), then analysis versus summary, writing, and the exit. The opening question ("was it the right move?") is never brought back beside King's choices in the closing.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** Real excerpts are printed on the handout (p.2), which is the right call. But two of the three are altered without ellipses, and one contains sentences King never wrote (section 5). Excerpt 1 also leaves out the sentence where King actually redefines "outsider", which is the very move the I-do model (slide 6) and its question 1 ask students to find.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 3.** The context is accurate and compact (slide 4: eight white clergymen, "unwise and untimely", moderates not segregationists). The two key terms are defined on slide 5, and the "Analysis vs. summary" slide (11) with its "which means that..." test is strong. The slide 5 example misdescribes the extremist list.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R2, R3, R4, R6, R7, R13): 2.** There are 3 pictures in 14 slides. There is no photo of King himself (R4), no image of the clergymen's statement or of the letter or newspaper (the documents at the heart of the lesson), no map of Birmingham and Alabama, and no timeline. The fire-hose photo on slide 4, captioned "Birmingham, April 1963", almost certainly shows the May 1963 hosings, which came after the letter.
- **E. Writing: 3.** About 36% of class is writing. The starter, the annotation questions, the Short Response (name the move, quote it, explain the audience effect) and the exit tickets all align with the objective. But the handout's "answer in the space provided" has no space under any question, and exit ticket 1 largely repeats the Short Response.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** The teacher models one charge, then circulates through pair rounds with listen-fors (slides 7 and 9 notes). The analysis-versus-summary check ("What does Version B do that Version A doesn't?") is good. There is no quick whole-class check that students can name the moves before writing.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The lesson is planned at 55 minutes for a 50-minute period, and the stack, plan and deck all agree on the overrun. The mini-lesson says "a quick two-minute version" but is timed at 7. On the handout, Charge 3's second question is orphaned onto a nearly empty page 3, and no question has answer space.
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** There are options throughout (three starters, three exit tickets), and the speaker notes are specific and useful ("He doesn't say he's not an outsider. He changes what outsider means."). Against that, the stack's period length is unset, the handout page count is wrong, and the printable plan PDF truncates each step.
- **I. Access: 2.** The excerpts are short and chunked by charge, and slides 7 and 9 carry frames ("For this audience... Paul matters because..."). But there is no vocabulary support for 1963 diction (Tarsus, Greco-Roman, categorized, conversely, "squares with the moral law"), and no frame for the Short Response's three-part analysis sentence.
- **Overall: 2.** It is well structured and the teacher voice is expert. But a rhetorical analysis lesson whose handout misquotes King, whose model excerpt lacks the line being modelled, and which runs 5 minutes over needs real work before a strong teacher would use it.

## 3. Strengths

1. The three-charges structure (outsider, extreme, breaking the law) is exactly how to teach "how he answers the clergymen". Slide 4's note, "They are not segregationists. They are moderates. That is the whole problem", frames the audience precisely.
2. The analysis-versus-summary mini-lesson (slide 11 and plan step 4) is high-value. It sets Version A, "King uses the Apostle Paul...", against Version B, which explains the effect on the audience, and gives the transferable "which means that..." test (slide 12). This is a worked example done well.
3. The context is accurate and short (R11): the eight clergymen, Protestant, Catholic and Jewish; "unwise and untimely"; the letter written in the margins and smuggled out. The key terms are on screen at first use (R5).

## 4. Problems

1. **Fabricated and silently trimmed quotations.**
   - Where: handout p.2, Charges 1 and 2.
   - The extremist excerpt ends: "Was not Abraham Lincoln an extremist? Was not Thomas Jefferson an extremist?" King wrote no such sentences. His list runs Jesus, Amos, Paul, Martin Luther, John Bunyan, Lincoln and Jefferson, each with a quotation ("And Abraham Lincoln: 'This nation cannot survive half slave and half free.'"). The Jesus quotation is also cut mid-verse with no ellipsis.
   - The Paul excerpt drops "Just as the prophets of the eighth century B.C. left their villages..." without an ellipsis.
   - Why it matters: in a lesson about the exact words of a rhetorician, students annotate words he did not write. Slide 9 then asks why King puts "those three names in one sentence", a list that doesn't exist.
   - Breaks: R11, Brian ("names and quotations exactly right").
2. **The model excerpt does not contain the move being modelled.**
   - Where: handout Excerpt 1 and slide 6.
   - Slide 6 tells students to "Underline the sentence where King redefines the charge". The redefinition ("Injustice anywhere is a threat to justice everywhere... Anyone who lives inside the United States can never be considered an outsider anywhere within its bounds") is not in the excerpt.
   - Why it matters: the "I do" collapses, because students hunt for a sentence that isn't there.
   - Breaks: R9, worked examples and modelling.
3. **The lesson is 55 minutes for a 50-minute request.**
   - Where: the plan total, the deck header ("55 min"), and the stack ("55 minutes planned").
   - The mini-lesson is billed as two minutes but timed at 7.
   - Why it matters: the teacher must cut on the fly, and the Short Response or the exit ticket is what gets lost.
   - Breaks: Brian (deck minutes must add up to the lesson), G.
4. **King is never shown, and one picture is out of time.**
   - Where: slides 1 to 14 and slide 4.
   - There is no photo of King, no image of "A Call for Unity" or of the letter, and no map. Slide 4's fire-hose photo is from the May 1963 Children's Crusade, captioned under "Birmingham, April 1963". The Lincoln portrait illustrates a list that is misquoted.
   - Why it matters: students meet a real person without his face, and get a chronology error on the context slide.
   - Breaks: R4, R7, R11, R13.
5. **The handout is not ready to print.**
   - Where: handout pp.2 to 4.
   - The directions say "Answer the questions in the space provided", but there are no lines. Page 3 is blank except for "2.", so Charge 3's second question splits across pages. There are no glosses for hard words.
   - Why it matters: students cannot write on the sheet, and struggling readers get no support with 1963 diction.
   - Breaks: Yawp! values (ready to print, room to write), scaffolding and access.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **Extremist excerpt (handout Charge 2), fabricated.** "Was not Abraham Lincoln an extremist? Was not Thomas Jefferson an extremist?" does not appear. King writes "And Abraham Lincoln: '...' And Thomas Jefferson: '...'", after Amos, Paul, Luther and Bunyan, none of whom are marked as omitted. The Jesus quotation is truncated ("...do good to them that hate you" stops before "and pray for them which despitefully use you") without an ellipsis. Confidence: high.
- **Paul excerpt (Charge 1), trimmed.** It omits the prophets clause ("Just as the prophets of the eighth century B.C. left their villages and carried their 'thus saith the Lord'...") without an ellipsis, and drops the "and just as" construction. Confidence: high.
- **"Jesus, Lincoln, Jefferson ... in one sentence" (slide 9, slide 5, handout question 2).** The list has seven figures, and they are not in one sentence. Confidence: high.
- **Slide 4 photo.** "Demonstrators hosed" in Birmingham is from early May 1963, after the April 16 letter, but sits under "Birmingham, April 1963". Confidence: moderate to high, judging by the caption text.
- **Simplification.** "Arrested for leading a nonviolent protest march": he was arrested for parading without a permit, in violation of an injunction. Acceptable for class, but imprecise. Confidence: high.
- Correct: eight white clergymen; "unwise and untimely"; written in the margins and on scraps; Augustine's "an unjust law is no law at all"; the just and unjust law definitions in excerpt 3 (properly elided).

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Verify public-domain-style excerpts before they are printed.** Every quoted excerpt should be checked against a source text, and any omission should require an ellipsis. Block printing of composite "quotations" stitched from paraphrase. The `checks` object shows zero quotations verified for a heavily quoted lesson. [R11]
2. **Confirm each excerpt contains the move it is used to model.** When a slide says "underline the sentence where X", the planner should check that the sentence is in the excerpt it points to. [R9, worked examples]
3. **Enforce the requested period length.** If the step minutes exceed the requested length, the planner should rebalance them before replying, rather than shipping a 55-minute deck and stack, and should set the stack's period length from the request. [Brian: minutes add up]
4. **Show real people and check image dates.** Require a portrait slide for a real author or speaker, and check image dates against the slide's stated date and against the text's composition date. [R4, R11]
5. **Make handout layout print-ready.** Render answer space as ruled lines, keep each question together with its answer area, and add a short glossary box for archaic or advanced diction in excerpts. [Yawp! values, access]
