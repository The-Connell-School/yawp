# a2: To Kill a Mockingbird, day 1 context (9th grade, 50 min)

## 1. The request and what came back

> "Build day 1 of a unit on To Kill a Mockingbird for 9th grade, 50 minutes: Maycomb, the Great Depression and Jim Crow, the world of the novel before we read. Include the slide deck."

**What came back:** a six-step plan totalling 50 minutes: Class starter (5, three options: "Judging by the cover", "Right and wrong", "Kind and brave"), What do you already know? (5), Context mini-lesson "Maycomb, the Depression, and Jim Crow" (18), Talk "What kind of place is Maycomb?" (10), First lines read-aloud (7), and Share-back and close (5, three exit-ticket options), with homework of Chapter 1. The deck has 14 slides totalling **44 minutes**. It contains **no photographs or pictures at all**: one line-drawn map, one timeline, and the rest words. No handout was requested. The default handouts PDF (6 pages) is lined pages for the starters and exit tickets. After "Add everything", the stack shows **70 minutes planned** with "43% of class is writing · 30 min". Its period length is blank ("—-minute period"), so it reports "70 min planned. Set it and it's ready to teach" with **no over-time warning**.

## 3. Strengths

1. **A clean day-1 arc.** Starter on a theme, then prior knowledge, then where, when, who and what the law said, then talk, then the first voice of the text, then a return to the starter. This is the R12 sequence, and the notes explain *why* each move is there.
2. **A real timeline (slide 6)** that puts the crash, the deepening Depression, the novel's 1933–1935 span and the 1960 publication on one line. It is a timeline, not a list of dates (R3), and it connects history to the book (R8).
3. **Teacher-facing guidance that reads like an expert colleague.** Examples: "Listen for the word 'trapped'... If someone says 'safe' or 'boring,' ask: safe for whom?"; "Segregation was not custom or habit. It was law" (slide 10); and the explicit misconception to watch for (Jim Crow confused with slavery) in exit ticket 1.

## 4. Problems

1. **A context day with nothing to look at.** There are no photographs at all. Slide 11 asks students to study an image that was never supplied, and slide 8's Atticus portrait (a Gregory Peck still was requested) was dropped silently, with no labeled slot. There is no Harper Lee, no Monroeville courthouse, no Depression-era Alabama street, no "Colored" entrance or balcony, and no FSA photograph (for example Walker Evans's 1936 Hale County, Alabama work). Students meeting 1930s Alabama for the first time need to *see* it. This breaks R1, R4, R6, R7, R10 and R13.
2. **The map does not start from what students know.** Slide 5 opens zoomed into Alabama's borders with two dots, unshaded and with no US or world context, contradicting its own notes. A ninth grader cannot tell from it where Alabama is. This breaks R2.
3. **The most important real context is missing.** There is no Scottsboro Boys case (Alabama, 1931–37), which every TKAM context day should name beside Tom Robinson. There is no rural class structure (the Cunninghams and Ewells) and no 1960 civil-rights moment of publication. The Depression is reduced to national statistics. Students will not be equipped to read *this* book. This breaks R1 and R8.
4. **The reading step is wrong and has no text.** Slide 13 says "read the opening paragraph... stop after 'Maycomb was an old town...'". That sentence is several pages in, after the Jem's-arm opening and the Finch family history. A teacher following the notes either stops early or reads for far longer than 7 minutes. The slide does not show the opening paragraph or leave a labeled slot for the copyrighted passage. This breaks R9, R10 and R11.
5. **Timing and materials do not agree.** The plan is 50 minutes, the deck 44, and the stack 70 with no warning because the period length is blank. The handout's first page invents three prompts that are not in the plan. "Add everything" put all three starters in a row but only one of the three exit tickets (the reflection, not the content check that matches the objective). This breaks Brian's rule that minutes must add up, "materials ready to use", and objective/assessment alignment.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **"This is the novel's third sentence"** (slide 7 notes) and **"read the opening paragraph... stop after 'Maycomb was an old town...'"** (plan step 5, slide 13 notes): wrong. The novel opens "When he was nearly thirteen, my brother Jem got his arm badly broken at the elbow." The "tired old town" sentence comes several pages later. The quotation itself is verbatim.
- **"Unemployment reaches 25%", dated 1930–32** (slide 6): the peak (about 25%) was in 1933.
- **"Separate courtrooms"** (plan step 3; slide 9 example "separate schools, restaurants, and courtrooms were required by law"): courts segregated seating, as the novel's own balcony shows, not whole courtrooms. Slide 9's other example (the balcony) is correct.
- **"Every detail Harper Lee drew from Monroeville"**: overstated (Maycomb is a composite), though Monroeville as the model is right.
- Correct: Scout about six at the start ("almost six"), Jem four years older, the 1933–35 setting, published 1960 and Pulitzer 1961, Atticus a widower, Scout narrating as an adult, the coordinates of Monroeville, and Jim Crow running roughly from the 1870s to the 1960s.
- Confidence: high on the opening-sentence error and the Scottsboro omission; medium-high on the others.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Do not ship a content slide whose picture failed to resolve.** Show a visible labeled slot ("Picture needed: a 1930s Alabama main street, e.g. FSA photograph, Library of Congress") and list missing images in the chat reply. Never leave a "What do you notice?" slide with nothing to notice. (R7, R10, R13)
2. **Build maps as a sequence:** a world or US locator with the state shaded, then the state with the town. Make the map renderer honour the notes it is given ("shaded on the wider US map"). (R2)
3. **Give every real person in a context day a portrait (the author at minimum) and every main character a named card with a labeled image slot.** A "who" slide should never be text only. (R4)
4. **Keep a per-book "essential context" checklist that the context day must cover or explicitly decline,** for TKAM: Scottsboro, Jim Crow law in practice, rural class and sharecropping, the 1960 publication moment. Check any quoted "first lines" against the actual opening before telling the teacher where to stop. (R1, R8, R11)
5. **Make the stack inherit the period length from the request ("50 minutes")** so the over-time warning fires. Have "Add everything" add one starter and the objective-aligned exit ticket, not all alternatives. Generate the handout only from prompts that are in the plan. (Feasibility, Brian: minutes must add up)
