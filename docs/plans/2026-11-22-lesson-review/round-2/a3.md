# a3: The Odyssey, Book 9, the Cyclops: hero or show-off? (9th grade)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Teach a 50-minute lesson for 9th grade on Book 9 of The Odyssey, the Cyclops: is Odysseus a hero here or a reckless show-off? Include the slide deck."

**What came back:** A six-part plan totaling 50 minutes. The parts are a class starter (5 min, three library prompts on cleverness and courage), context (7 min: kleos, hubris, Polyphemus and xenia), close reading of "three moments" (18 min: entering the cave, the blinding, shouting his name, each 2-4 lines with an annotation task and a teacher-modeled first annotation), a pairs-then-whole-class discussion (12), a Short Response (6) and a closing (2, three exit-ticket options). The deck has 16 slides and 51 minutes. Two slides have pictures: a Homer bust (slide 4) and a Greek vase painting of the blinding (slide 6). The three reading slides (8, 10, 12) carry tasks but no text. The handout PDF is six pages, and it prints the same three excerpts twice (p.2 and p.6), the second time headed "trans. Robert Fagles". The handout came in the first reply (logged "no handout suggestion offered"). The stack is **50 of 50 minutes, "Right on time", 62% writing (31 min)**. The reply's own step list, however, shows the banner "This plan runs 100 minutes; the period is 50."

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** There is a good spine: the starter on courage and cleverness, a short context on the hero code, three escalating moments, discussion, writing, and a return to "What does a hero owe his men?" on the exit slide. But the starter's theme (cleverness) is never paid off, because the "Nobody" trick and the escape under the ram, the cleverness evidence, are not read. The exit slide's "You had an answer at the start" refers to slide 7, not to what students wrote.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R10, R11): 1.** The passages students annotate are labeled "(trans. Robert Fagles)" in quotation marks (handout p.6) with Greek-text line numbers. Yet the reply admits "The excerpts above are paraphrased closely from the Greek but are not Fagles's exact words". Excerpt 1 ("My spirit longed to see the cave man... no fine guest, I'd soon discern") is not Fagles. The key evidence is missing: the men begging to take the cheeses and go, the first taunt, and Polyphemus's prayer, the "cost" the whole lesson turns on.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 3.** Kleos and hubris are defined on slide 5 with text-linked examples, and xenia and Polyphemus's parentage are accurate (slide 6). It is short and punchy. There is no gloss for the passage words (shipwright, discern, Laertes, "raider of cities").
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R2, R4, R6, R7, R13): 2.** The Homer bust and the vase of the blinding are real, credited and from the subject's world. But 14 of 16 slides have no image. There is no map of the voyage, no image of the escape under the rams, and the reading slides show no text at all (slides 8, 10, 12 say "Follow along on the handout").
- **E. Writing: 3.** The Short Response is well aligned ("Pick the one choice that is hardest to defend as heroic... Use a specific detail from the passage"), and the handout's claim frame is useful. But the response gets only 6 minutes, and the stack's "62% writing" counts the whole 18-minute read-aloud as writing, which overstates it.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** There is a pause and annotation after each excerpt, a "Which of these three moments is hardest to defend?" poll, pair talk with stems, and exit tickets with sharp "watch for" notes ("Treating the blinding and the name-shouting as the same kind of act").
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The stack adds up exactly (50/50), but the materials are not ready to print. The teacher is told to "swap in your edition's wording before you print". The excerpts appear twice in the handout, and the reply shows a false "runs 100 minutes" warning.
- **H. Teacher usability: 2.** The discussion moves are expert ("What would a leader who put his men first have done differently?", "the hero code and the good leader are in conflict here. Which one wins?"). But the reply contradicts itself: it opens "The Odyssey is in the public domain, so the passage goes directly on the excerpt slide", then says the passages are not Fagles and must be replaced, and the deck has no excerpt slide. The plan's model quotes "my heart longed" while the handout says "My spirit longed".
- **I. Access: 2.** There is a modeled annotation, sentence stems on discussion slides, and a claim frame. There is no plot recap of Book 9 for students who have not read or understood it (the lesson never says whether they have), no glossary, and only 2-4 lines of text per moment, too thin for struggling readers to reconstruct the episode.
- **Overall: 2.** The question, the discussion design and the Short Response are strong. But the core reading material is misattributed paraphrase, and a teacher would have to rebuild the handout from their own edition before class.

## 3. Strengths

1. The discussion plan anticipates the best student defense of Odysseus ("he had to tell the Cyclops his name, that's how heroes work") and turns it into the lesson's real insight: the conflict between the hero code (kleos) and leadership.
2. The three-moment structure escalates well (curiosity, then violence, then pride), and the annotation tasks are specific and varied ("Box the words that make this feel planned and skilled").
3. The exit tickets are precise and diagnostic: "the blinding is survival, the shout is ego" names exactly the distinction a 9th grader needs.

## 4. Problems

1. **Paraphrased text presented as a named translation.** Where: handout p.6 ("trans. Robert Fagles", passages in quotation marks), handout p.2, and the speaker notes on slides 8, 10 and 12. The reply admits they are "not Fagles's exact words". Students are taught to quote "the text" and would be quoting invented lines attributed to a real translator. A teacher whose class uses Fagles will find the words do not match the books on the desks. Breaks R10 ("never substitute quietly"), R11 and Brian's "quotations exactly right".
2. **The key evidence is not in front of students.** Where: the three excerpts. Missing are the crew's plea to take the cheeses and lambs and sail (around 9.224-227, Greek), the first taunt and the men's pleading, the "Nobody" trick, and Polyphemus's prayer to Poseidon. The lesson asks "what does it cost his men?" but students never read the curse that answers it, and they cannot weigh "hero" because the clever escape is absent. Breaks close reading and discussion that requires evidence from the text.
3. **Reading slides with no reading.** Where: slides 8, 10 and 12 (titled "Moment 1/2/3") show only tasks. The reply promised the passage "goes directly on the excerpt slide", but there are no excerpt slides. A class without the handout, or a teacher projecting for a whole-class read, has nothing to look at. Breaks R9 and R7.
4. **Contradictory and false messages to the teacher.** Where: the reply's first line ("public domain... directly on the excerpt slide") versus its note ("Fagles... still in copyright... paraphrased"); the step-list banner "This plan runs 100 minutes" on a 50-minute plan; the duplicate excerpts on handout pp.2 and 6. The teacher cannot trust what is ready, and the 100-minute banner invites cuts to a plan that fits. Breaks Brian's "expert, on the teacher's side" and "ready to print".
5. **Thin visuals and no orientation.** Where: deck-wide, 2 of 16 slides with images. There is no map of Odysseus's route or the land of the Cyclopes, and no image of the ram escape or of the name-shout from the ship. Words-only content slides (5, 9) do not dual-code kleos or hubris. Breaks R2, R7 and R13.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **Excerpt 1 is not Fagles:** "My spirit longed to see the cave man, what he had to offer, / no fine guest, I'd soon discern, for all who'd come to visit." Fagles renders this moment differently (roughly "but I would not give way, / not till I saw him, saw what gifts he'd give"). The planner admits the excerpts are paraphrase, but the handout still credits Fagles. High confidence it is not Fagles; medium on the exact Fagles wording I give.
- **Excerpt 2 is a stitch:** "like a shipwright bores his beam with a shipwright's drill" and "So we seized our stake with its fiery tip / and bored it round and round in the giant's eye" are Fagles's phrasing, but the opening line and the ellipsis join non-adjacent material without saying so. Medium confidence.
- **Excerpt 3 matches Fagles closely** ("Cyclops, if any man on the face of the earth should ask you / who blinded you, shamed you so, say Odysseus, / raider of cities..."). Medium-high confidence.
- **Line numbers** (~224-230, ~382-394, ~502-505) are Greek-text line numbers, not Fagles's (his lines for the name-shout run in the 560s). Mixing a translation's words with Greek line numbers sends students to the wrong page. High confidence.
- **The plan's model** quotes "my heart longed", while the handout and slide notes say "My spirit longed". This is internally inconsistent.
- **Context facts are correct** (Polyphemus son of Poseidon, the Cyclopes' disregard for Zeus and xenia, Poseidon's wrath beginning here, two taunts with the men pleading in between). The planner's flag about the taunts is accurate.
- **"Next lesson: Book 12, Scylla and Charybdis"** skips Books 10-11. That is a choice, not an error, as the planner notes.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. Never print a passage in quotation marks under a translator's name unless it is verified against that translation. For Homer, default to a public-domain translation (Butler 1900; Butcher and Lang 1879) and say so on the handout, or leave a labeled slot ("paste Fagles 9.258-260 here") instead of a paraphrase. (R10, R11)
2. Tie line numbers to the edition actually quoted, and print the edition on the handout. (R9, R11)
3. When an excerpt layout is planned, the reading slide must carry the passage or an exact pointer (book, lines, edition). Reconcile the reply's prose claims ("on the excerpt slide") with what was actually built. (R9)
4. For an evaluative question ("hero or show-off?"), require excerpts that give evidence on both sides (here the Nobody trick and the ram escape, and the crew's plea and Polyphemus's curse), and a one-paragraph plot recap for students who need it. (Close reading; discussion that requires evidence; access)
5. Fix the step-list length check that announced "This plan runs 100 minutes" on a 50-minute plan, and dedupe handout sections so the same excerpts are not printed twice. Do not count a read-aloud as writing time in the writing share. (Brian: minutes add up; Yawp!: honest writing share)
