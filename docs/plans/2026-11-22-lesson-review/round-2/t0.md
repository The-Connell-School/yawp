# t0: Macbeth 1.3, the prophecy and the aside (12th grade)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Teach a 50-minute lesson for 12th grade on Macbeth Act 1, Scene 3: the witches' prophecy and Macbeth's first aside. Include the slide deck."

**What came back:** A six-part plan totaling 50 minutes: class starter (5, three free-will prompts), context (7), read aloud (12), close reading of the aside with a 3-minute teacher model, 5 minutes of pairs and 4 of share-out (12), a Short Response, "What ambition costs" (10), and closing plus exit ticket (4, three exit-ticket options). A 13-slide deck (the deck card says 51 min) with **no pictures on any slide** and one labeled "picture to add" slot (slide 5). A five-page handout: three short writing pages (starter, Short Response, exit ticket) and a two-page passage handout (5 glossed words, witches' prophecies "lines 48-65", the aside "lines 126-142", annotation and pairs questions with two sentence starters). The handout came in the first reply; the test logged "no handout suggestion offered", so there was no second reply. After "Add everything to the lesson" the stack reads **70 of 50 minutes (20 min over)** and **41% of class is writing (29 min)**.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** It opens on the theme in students' own terms (the free-will starter) and closes by sending them back to it ("Did the scene change your answer, or make it more complicated?"), with direct teaching and close reading in between. But the Short Response leaves the arc: it switches to "ambition costs something", not free will and not the aside, so the text never gets set squarely beside what students wrote at the start.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 3.** The aside is printed in full and accurately on handout p.4 and excerpted on slide 9. The teacher model ("he calls the prophecies 'prologues', that's a theatre word") is specific, real close reading. But the handout skips everything between the witches' exit and the aside (Ross's "He bade me, from him, call thee Thane of Cawdor" and Banquo's "instruments of darkness tell us truths"), which is the news the aside is reacting to. The line ranges also disagree (plan 38-78, handout and slide 8 48-65).
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 3.** The context is short and accurate (three prophecies in ascending order, dramatic irony from 1.2), and slide 6 defines soliciting, surmise and function before the reading. But "earnest" (pledge, down payment), a pivot word in "earnest of success", is defined nowhere, and neither are "start", "seated heart" or "single state of man".
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R4, R6, R7, R13): 1.** None of the 13 slides has a picture. Slides 4 and 5 teach content ("Where we are", "Three prophecies") with words only. There are no witches and no staging, and not even the public-domain Holinshed (1577) woodcut of Macbeth and Banquo meeting the three women, the obvious image for this scene. Slide 5 does leave an honest labeled slot (R10 met for that one).
- **E. Writing: 2.** The amount is good (41%, three Yawp! pieces). But the main written product is a generic library prompt ("from the text, from the news, or from your own life") that does not measure the stated objective ("what the aside tells us about him that the witches' words alone do not"). Only the spoken teacher note pulls it back to the aside. Exit ticket option 1 is well aligned.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** There are circulation notes with a specific misconception to listen for (summarizing the prophecy instead of reading the aside, slide 10 notes), a share-out, and an exit ticket with "watch for" notes. There is no quick hinge check after the context or the read-aloud.
- **G. Feasibility: 1.** The stack is 70/50 because the passage handout was added twice as timed 10-minute activity steps ("Passage handout" and "Act 1, Scene 3: The Prophecy and the Aside"). Two Short Response rows also appear. The deck runs 51 minutes, yet the reply claims "The deck's slide minutes total 50". The speaker notes say to pause "after line 65 (the witches vanish)", but the witches vanish later (around line 78).
- **H. Teacher usability: 2.** There are real options: three starters, reading-aloud choices phrased "If your class enjoys...", and three exit tickets. But internal text leaks to the teacher. The reply says "The painting `hokusai-great-wave` was used in your Odyssey unit, so I'm leaving it out" (this is a Macbeth lesson, and the teacher has no Odyssey unit here), and that sentence is printed as step 9 of the **printable lesson plan PDF**. A "Check before class" block quotes the plan's own sentences, cut off mid-word ("Thane of C…"). The printed plan has 11 steps with a duplicate "Class starter" and a duplicate "Short Response".
- **I. Access: 3.** There is a word bank on the handout and slide, a think-aloud model, two sentence starters, and read-aloud role options. There is no modern paraphrase or chunking of the 16-line aside for struggling readers or English learners, which is the standard scaffold for Shakespeare.
- **Overall: 2.** The teaching core (the aside, the model, the pairs questions) is strong and a good 12th-grade teacher would keep it. But the stack is 20 minutes over, the printed plan carries a stray internal note, the deck has no images, and the Short Response misses the objective. It is not usable tomorrow without real cleanup.

## 3. Strengths

1. The think-aloud model in step 4 and on slide 9 ("I noticed a word → I asked what it assumes → I said what that reveals about the speaker") is genuine I-do/you-do instruction in a reusable close-reading move.
2. The aside is printed accurately and in full, with sensible pairs questions ("Find the word or phrase that most clearly shows Macbeth has already thought about something he hasn't said aloud").
3. The arc returns to the starter at the close, and the slide 10 speaker notes anticipate the real misconception (summarizing the witches instead of reading Macbeth).

## 4. Problems

1. **The stack does not add up (70 of 50 min).** Where: stack rows "Passage handout" (10 min) and "Act 1, Scene 3: The Prophecy and the Aside" (10 min). The handout became two timed activities, and the Short Response appears twice. The teacher who clicked "Add everything" gets a lesson 20 minutes too long, and the planner's own fix suggestions (trim context, move Short Response) would cut real teaching instead of the phantom rows. Breaks Brian's "minutes must add up" and Feasibility.
2. **No pictures anywhere in the deck.** Where: slides 1-13, notably the content slides 4, 5 and 9. Students looking at a scene of three witches on a heath see only text. There is no visual anchor for who Macbeth and Banquo are, and nothing pairs with "Three prophecies". Breaks R4, R6, R7, R13.
3. **The Short Response does not measure the objective.** Where: step 5 and slide 12, "Ambition costs something... from the text, from the news, or from your own life." Students can write a whole response about a news story and never touch the aside, so the main writing of the day gives no evidence on the objective. Breaks objective/assessment alignment (Yawp!'s writing value, E).
4. **The handout cuts the hinge of the scene.** Where: handout p.4 jumps from "So all hail, Macbeth and Banquo!" to "Two truths are told". Ross's Cawdor announcement and Banquo's warning ("oftentimes, to win us to our harm, / The instruments of darkness tell us truths") are missing. Students annotate a reaction ("I am Thane of Cawdor") without the line that causes it, and they lose Banquo, the natural foil that shows what the aside reveals about Macbeth in particular. Line ranges also disagree across the plan (38-78), handout (48-65) and speaker notes ("pause after line 65 (the witches vanish)"). Breaks R9, R11, close reading.
5. **Internal notes leak into teacher-facing and printed materials.** Where: in the reply and in step 9 of the plan PDF ("The painting hokusai-great-wave was used in your Odyssey unit, so I'm leaving it out"), and the "Check before class" list of truncated self-quotations. A teacher printing the plan for a sub or an observer hands over nonsense about an unrelated unit. Breaks Brian's "expert, on the teacher's side" and "materials ready to print".

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- The aside text (handout p.4, slide 9) matches the standard text: "Two truths are told... And nothing is but what is not." Witches' hails and Banquo's prophecies are correct. High confidence.
- "Macbeth's first aside": strictly, Macbeth's first aside in 1.3 is "Glamis, and Thane of Cawdor! / The greatest is behind" (around 1.3.116-117, Arden; around 1.3.129-130, Folger). "Two truths are told" is his second. The plan never acknowledges this. It is a small point, but a 12th-grade teacher would expect it. Medium-high confidence.
- The handout's note "the other characters cannot hear him" sits over "I thank you, gentlemen", which Macbeth says aloud to Ross and Angus. The aside resumes after it. This is misleading.
- The speaker note on slide 8 says the witches vanish at line 65. They vanish after Macbeth's "Speak, I charge you" (around line 78, Arden). Line 65 ends Banquo's prophecy.
- Banquo's speech is cut mid-sentence after "so fair?" with no ellipsis (the planner itself flagged this, but left it in the handout).
- The reply's claim that the deck totals 50 minutes is wrong: the slide minutes sum to 51.
- Exit ticket option 2's answer key ("the irony is that fate and his own desire are already moving together") is not dramatic irony. It mixes up the concept the ticket tests.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. When "Add everything" runs, add a handout as a material attached to the step that uses it, never as its own timed step, and dedupe a writing prompt that already exists as a step. Block "added everything" when the stack exceeds the period. (Feasibility; Brian: minutes add up)
2. Never let tool chatter or image-picker reasoning (asset ids, "used in your X unit") into the reply body or the printed plan. Strip anything that is not addressed to the teacher before rendering the PDF. (Brian: expert, kind; Yawp!: ready to print)
3. Require at least one real image per content slide from the subject's world (for Shakespeare: public-domain woodcuts and engravings, production photos as labeled slots), and treat a deck with zero pictures as a failed build, not a finished one. (R6, R7, R13)
4. When a library prompt is chosen for the main writing, check it against the objective, and rewrite it so it names the text and the evidence required ("Using the aside, what does Macbeth reveal...") rather than allowing "the news or your own life". (Alignment, E)
5. For passages, require continuity checks: the printed excerpt must include the line that triggers the passage being analyzed, and line references must be identical across plan, slides, notes and handout. Label abridgements. (R9, R11)
