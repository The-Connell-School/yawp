# d1: 1984 unit plan, grade 12 (Language and Power)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Build a unit plan for 1984 for 12th grade: four weeks, 50-minute periods, with a focus on language and power, ending in a research-backed essay."

**What came back:** The reply took 126 s. It held a 20-day unit board, "Language and Power in 1984" (20 x 50 min = 1,000 min). Each day had an objective, reading, an in-class summary, a "why" line and a check. The board also had a "class starter arc" of five original prompts (Days 1, 5, 9, 13, 17), four notes (reading pace, writing lessons, essay, starter arc), a "Check before class" box with three flagged chapter errors, and two closing questions. Days 1–6 and 8–9 teach the novel, with Day 3 on the Newspeak Appendix. Days 11, 13 and 15 cover Goldstein, O'Brien and the ending. The writing days are 7 (thesis), 10 (evidence and analysis), 16 (hooks), 17 (outline) and 19 (transitions and conclusions), and the research days are 12 (find sources) and 14 (integrate sources). Days 18 and 20 are for drafting and for submission plus reflection. There is no deck or handout, which is expected for a unit. The printable plan PDF is 5 pages. The script found no "Add everything" button, so the stack is empty: "Untitled lesson, 0 of 50 minutes planned, 0% writing". For a unit this is reasonable, because each day is built later with "Build this day".

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** Day 1 opens on the theme in students' own experience ("a word that has been used to make something bad sound acceptable"), checks prior knowledge briefly, and teaches context. Day 20's reflection loops back to the Day 1 question, and the starter arc feeds the Day 17 outline. The arc toward the essay is coherent, but the context day is thin (see D and Problem 4).
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** Good anchor passages are named: the Appendix, the diary, "Who controls the past…", O'Brien's "Power is not a means; it is an end", and the final line. But the reading map has several errors and a gap. Day 9's "close read of Orwell's definition of doublethink" is assigned to Part Two, Ch. 4–7, where that definition is not. Day 13 is titled "Room 101" but reads Ch. 1–3, and Room 101 is in Ch. 5. Part Two, Ch. 8 is never assigned. Syme's Newspeak conversation (Part One, Ch. 5) is never used, though it is the novel's central language-and-power scene.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 2.** The Day 3 Sapir-Whorf mini-lesson and the Day 9 plain-language definition of doublethink are smart. Key terms are listed but never defined (Day 2: "telescreen, Thought Police, doublethink (introduced here, defined later)"). The research mini-lessons (Days 12 and 14) name no databases, citation format or works-cited instruction.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding: n/a (null).** No deck. Day 1 says only "teacher fills the map". No visual for Orwell, the Spanish Civil War, the BBC or Senate House (the model for the Ministry of Truth) is suggested.
- **E. Writing: 3.** Students write a lot: five themed starters, Short Responses on Days 4, 8 and 15, exit tickets, seminar reflection, and an essay process from thesis through revision. The flaw is that only 5 of 20 days have a class starter, though Yawp! expects one every day. Also, Day 12 finds two sources while the Day 17 outline requires "one source per paragraph" across three body paragraphs.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** Every day has a check, and many are well aimed (Day 9: "explain why it is not simply lying"; Day 5: "agree, disagree, or complicate"). Nothing checks the heavy homework reading, which is nearly the whole novel, so a teacher cannot tell who has read before a close read or seminar.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The structure is 20 x 50, but the content has known errors left in place: three were flagged in "Check before class" and not fixed, and the others are under B. Two research periods are thin for a "research-backed" essay. Day 10 fits two writing lessons in 20 minutes and still adds practice, which is plausible. The printable unit PDF cuts every "In class" description with "…" (e.g., Day 1: "Orwell's life, 1948 Britain, the…"), so the printed plan cannot be taught from. The overview reads "It ends with A research-backed…".
- **H. Teacher usability: 2.** The "Build this day" structure, the "why" lines and the starter arc are genuinely helpful. But the plan prescribes rather than offering options (one seminar, one jigsaw, no alternates). It leaves errors it detected on the board for the teacher to fix, exposes internal chatter ("the library returned nothing for these themes at grade 12"), and ends on two questions instead of one.
- **I. Access: 1.** There are no scaffolds: no reading guides, audiobook option, chunking, vocabulary lists, sentence frames for the thesis or analysis, or ELL supports. This is a 20-day unit with roughly the whole novel and Goldstein's book assigned for homework.
- **Overall: 2.** A strong teacher would find a usable skeleton and a good essay sequence, but would have to re-map the chapter assignments, add the missing core texts (Syme; "Politics and the English Language"), build reading accountability and scaffolds, and could not print the unit PDF. The plan needs major work before it is used.

## 3. Strengths

1. **A real writing spine.** The thesis lesson comes on Day 7, early, built from students' own starter entries. Then come evidence and analysis (Day 10), source integration with a teacher model (Day 14: "whose voice is louder?"), outline, draft, and revision, so the essay is taught and not just assigned.
2. **The starter arc is thematic, not comprehension.** "What's the difference between being persuaded and being manipulated?" (Day 9) and "official version vs. what you saw" (Day 13) are strong grade-12 prompts. Day 17 has students mine their own entries for a thesis, which is a smart retrieval and spacing move.
3. **Smart lesson ideas.** Day 3 pairs Sapir-Whorf with the Appendix and a "modern Newspeak dictionary". Day 9 has students write their own definition of doublethink and test it against two examples. Day 6 is a jigsaw comparing the audiences of Party language. Day 15 opens on the final line.

## 4. Problems (most serious first)

1. **The reading map is wrong in several places, and the tool knew about some of the errors and shipped them anyway.**
   - Day 6's jigsaw uses the Two Minutes Hate, which is in Ch. 1, against a Ch. 5–8 reading. This was flagged but not fixed.
   - Day 11 assigns "Chapters 9–10" for Goldstein's book, which is Ch. 9 only. This was flagged.
   - Day 9 close-reads "Orwell's definition of doublethink" from Part Two, Ch. 4–7. The definition is in Part One, Ch. 3 and in Goldstein's book (Part Two, Ch. 9). This was not flagged.
   - Day 13 is titled "Room 101" but assigns Part Three, Ch. 1–3, and Room 101 is Ch. 5. This was not flagged.
   - Part Two, Ch. 8 (O'Brien's apartment, the Brotherhood "oath") is never assigned.
   - Day 4 re-assigns the same Ch. 1–2 as Day 2.

   Students will be told to close-read passages that are not in that night's reading. (R11; alignment.)
2. **Core language-and-power texts are missing.** Syme's Newspeak scene (Part One, Ch. 5: "the whole aim of Newspeak is to narrow the range of thought") is the novel's central statement on the unit's theme and is never taught. Instead Day 3 jumps to the Appendix on Day 3, before Syme appears. Orwell's "Politics and the English Language" (1946), the obvious paired nonfiction and a natural research source, is absent. (R1; close reading; alignment to the focus.)
3. **The research is thin for a "research-backed" essay.** There are two periods, with no named databases or source sets, no citation format or works cited, and no annotated sources. Day 12 yields two sources, but Day 17 requires one source per body paragraph across three paragraphs. (Alignment; scaffolding; G.)
4. **The context day does not equip students for this book.** Day 1 says only "teacher fills the map — Orwell's life, 1948 Britain, the Cold War". It leaves out the Spanish Civil War (Orwell's experience of propaganda and of history being rewritten), his BBC and Ministry of Information years, any map of Airstrip One / Oceania / Eurasia / Eastasia, Orwell's face, and a timeline. The same period also carries a starter, a KWL share, a lecture and a close, so it is crowded. (R1, R2, R3, R4, R12(3).)
5. **There are no access supports and no reading accountability across 20 days.** Nearly the whole novel and Goldstein's book are homework, with no reading checks, guides, chunking, audio, vocabulary support or sentence frames for the thesis and analysis work. Students who fall behind on the reading cannot take part in Days 8–15. (Scaffolding and access; checking for understanding.)

Also noted: the printable unit PDF cuts every in-class description with "…". The overview capitalizes "A research-backed…" mid-sentence. The plan ends on two closing questions (Brian). Internal tool chatter is shown to the teacher.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- Day 9: the doublethink definition is not in Part Two, Ch. 4–7. It is in Part One, Ch. 3 ("To know and not to know…") and in Goldstein's book, Part Two, Ch. 9. High confidence.
- Day 13: the title "Room 101" does not match the reading (Part Three, Ch. 1–3). Room 101 is Part Three, Ch. 5. High confidence.
- Day 6: the Two Minutes Hate is in Part One, Ch. 1, not Ch. 5–8 (flagged by the planner, not fixed). High confidence.
- Day 11: Goldstein's book (*The Theory and Practice of Oligarchical Collectivism*, not named in the plan) is excerpted in Part Two, Ch. 9. Ch. 10 is the arrest (flagged, not fixed). High confidence.
- The planner's own flag that "Chapter 2 deals with the neighbors and Parsons" is incomplete. Ch. 2 ends with Winston writing in the diary again ("To the future or to the past…"; "Thoughtcrime does not entail death: thoughtcrime IS death"). So Ch. 1–2 is a fair reading for the diary scene. Medium-high confidence.
- The planner's flag that "Chapter 8 is the O'Brien visit that gives Winston the book" is slightly off. O'Brien promises the book in Ch. 8, and Winston receives it in a briefcase during Hate Week (Ch. 9). Medium confidence.
- These quotations are correct: "Who controls the past controls the future" (Part One, Ch. 3); "Power is not a means; it is an end" (Part Three, Ch. 3); the slogans; "The Principles of Newspeak".

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **When a check flags an error, fix the board instead of appending a warning.** Re-map the chapter and passage for each flagged day, or drop the activity. Also check each day's title and focus passage against that day's assigned chapters, not only against the activities. (R11; Brian: names and quotations exactly right.)
2. **Add a canonical passage map for frequently taught novels**, giving where each key scene and definition lives (Syme, the doublethink definition, Room 101, Goldstein). Check that a unit's reading sequence has no gaps or repeats. (R9, R11.)
3. **For "research-backed" units, require a research block.** It should include named source types and databases, one paired nonfiction anchor (here "Politics and the English Language"), a citation and works-cited mini-lesson, and a source count that matches the outline's demands. (Alignment; scaffolding.)
4. **Make every unit include a reading-accountability routine and an access line per day**: a quick reading check, reading guide or chunking, audio option, vocabulary and sentence frames for thesis and analysis. Put a class starter on every day, not only 5 of 20. (Access; checking for understanding; Yawp! values.)
5. **Print the full "In class" text in the unit PDF instead of truncating it with "…".** Strip internal notes such as "the library returned nothing" from teacher-facing text, and end on one closing question. (Materials ready to use; Brian: one closing question.)
