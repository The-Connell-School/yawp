## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** The lesson opens on the theme in students' lives ("stayed in a bad situation because the alternative felt too uncertain"). It ends direct teaching by tying back ("does that match what you said at the opening?") and closes with individual writing. The direct teaching comes after pair work rather than before it, which is a defensible inductive choice. But the opening is "two minutes of silent thinking", not a Yawp! writing starter.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 3.** The full speech is supplied, and it is accurate to a standard modern text ("despised love", "great pitch"). The modeled think-aloud is specific and good. But on the printed handout the verse is collapsed into one bold prose block with no line numbers. The plan says "lines 1–9" for what is line 5, and the notes cite "lines 79–82", a numbering students cannot find on their sheet.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 3.** The key move is accurate and well put: the "act" column slides from revenge to death, and "the undiscovered country… puzzles the will". The context beat says Hamlet "has done nothing" and is "still paralyzed", which ignores his feigned madness and the Mousetrap already planned at the end of 2.2. "Conscience" (consciousness, reflection), the hinge of the speech's conclusion, is never glossed or taught.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R4, R6, R7, R13): 1.** The deck failed, so there is nothing on screen. Even the proposed deck asked for one picture in 13 slides, a generic "stage production". Its theme art was Hokusai's *Great Wave*, a decorative metaphor unrelated to Elsinore (R6). There is no Hamlet, Ophelia or staging image beside the text.
- **E. Writing: 3.** The Short Response offers three good, specific prompts ("Identify where that shift happens…"), and the exit ticket is aligned to the objective with clear assessFor/watchFor. But the opening is not a written starter, writing is only 20% of the period, and there is a single exit-ticket option.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** The teacher models and then circulates "listen for…" cues during pair work. Pairs share, with a planned probe ("What about lines 79 to 82?"), and the exit ticket's watchFor sorts "he is afraid to die" from a real answer. There is no quick check after the read-aloud on literal comprehension.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The plan minutes add to 50, but the requested deck is missing. The proposed deck totals 51 min and its agenda does not match its slide minutes. The handout's central text is printed as an unreadable prose wall. The stack has duplicate 0-min items and puts the handout after the exit ticket. The handout PDF prints the writing pages before the text.
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** The plan prose is expert, warm and practical ("read to the punctuation, not the line end"; "When someone says 'fear,' hold it"), and the Short Response choices are real options. But the "Check before class" flags are mostly false alarms that cast doubt on a correct text (see section 5). There is no note on handling the speech's suicide content.
- **I. Access: 2.** There is a 7-word glossary and sentence stems on the (unbuilt) discussion slides. But the text has no line breaks or numbers and no margin for annotation. Key words are missing from the glossary (conscience, rub, mortal coil, respect, consummation). There is no modern paraphrase, chunking or audio option for struggling readers and ELLs.
- **Overall: 2.** The plan itself is close to usable with edits, and its arc and think-aloud are strong. But the teacher asked for a deck and got none, and the printed text students annotate is a bold prose block. A teacher would have to build both core materials before tomorrow.

## 3. Strengths

1. **A modeled think-aloud with a clear frame** (Model step): "Hamlet sets up two sides — 'suffer' or 'take arms'… but then he immediately asks whether *ending* the suffering is even possible." It sets up the Endure/Act table and then releases students to pairs (I do, we do).
2. **A sharp, accurate landing point** (Direct teaching): "The thing that stops Hamlet is not the pain of living — it is the uncertainty of dying". It is tied explicitly back to students' opening answers (R12(4)).
3. **Short Response options and an exit ticket built to diagnose.** The three prompts each demand specific words. The exit ticket's watchFor ("students who say only 'he is afraid to die'") tells the teacher exactly whom to reteach tomorrow.

## 4. Problems (most serious first)

1. **The slide deck the teacher asked for did not build.** The chat shows "This deck didn't build", the stack shows "No deck yet", and slides.pdf returns 404, even though valid deck JSON was produced. The deck block was also emitted after the closing question. The teacher must ask again or build slides by hand. And the proposed deck was weak anyway: 1 picture request across 13 slides, decorative Hokusai art, and 51 min. (Feasibility; R7, R13, R6; Brian: deck minutes must add up.)
2. **The handout prints the soliloquy as one bold prose paragraph** (handout p. 3). Verse lineation is lost, there are no line numbers and no annotation margin, and the "glosses alongside" sit below the text. The lesson's own references ("lines 1–9", "lines 79–82") cannot be located, and the activity is annotating verse by line. (R9; materials ready to use; cognitive load.)
3. **The speech's suicide content is never named, and there is no care note for the teacher.** "His quietus make / With a bare bodkin" is the speech's explicit image of self-slaughter. The opener asks students to recall staying "in a bad situation", but nothing prepares the teacher to handle disclosure or to frame the topic. A department chair would require this. (Expert, kind, on the teacher's side.)
4. **The direct teaching skips the speech's conclusion.** "Thus conscience does make cowards of us all… sicklied o'er with the pale cast of thought… lose the name of action" is the speech's claim that thinking itself undoes action, which is the key to Hamlet's delay. "Conscience" (consciousness, reflection) is not glossed. The speech's impersonal "we/us" (Hamlet never says "I") is also missed. (R5; close reading; C.)
5. **The context is slightly misleading, numbering is inconsistent, and the flags are false.** "He has done nothing… still paralyzed" ignores the antic disposition and the Mousetrap planned in 2.2. The Model step calls "lines 1–9" what ends at line 5. Two of the three "Check before class" flags are wrong (see section 5), which teaches the teacher to distrust a correct text. (R11; Brian: names and quotations exactly right.)

Also noted: the stack has duplicate 0-minute items and the handout appears after the exit ticket. There is one exit-ticket option, not three (Brian). Writing is only 20% of the period.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- The soliloquy text checks against standard modern editions (Folger-style): "despised love" (the Q2 reading), "great pitch" (the F reading), "No traveller returns". I found no errors in the text itself. High confidence.
- Planner flag, "Folger, Arden… lines 56–88": wrong. Folger numbers the speech about 3.1.64–96. Arden 3 and Riverside start at 3.1.55/56. Medium-high confidence.
- Planner flag, "standard texts read 'dispriz'd love'": misleading. "Dispriz'd" is the Folio reading, and "despised" (Q2) is what Folger and many school texts print. This is not an error. High confidence.
- Planner flag on the comma after "cowards of us all": trivial, and editions vary. Not an error.
- Model step: "lines 1–9 (through '…and by opposing, end them')". That phrase ends at line 5. High confidence.
- Context: "comes before the mousetrap play — he is still paralyzed / has done nothing". This is an overstatement. By 3.1 Hamlet has adopted the antic disposition and arranged *The Murder of Gonzago* (end of 2.2). Interpretive, but misleading as stated.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **When a deck fails to build, retry automatically or tell the teacher clearly why**, instead of a one-line "ask again". Emit the deck block before the closing question. Do not let a lesson with "Include the slide deck" finish without slides. (Feasibility; R7.)
2. **Render poetry and drama handouts as verse.** Preserve line breaks and print line numbers every 5 lines. Leave an annotation margin, and place glosses beside their lines. Use one line-numbering scheme across plan, notes and handout. (R9; materials ready to use.)
3. **Add a sensitive-content check.** When a text touches suicide, self-harm or abuse, add a short teacher note (framing, opt-outs, counselor referral) and choose an opener that is not a personal-disclosure prompt. (Expert, kind, on the teacher's side.)
4. **Improve the quotation and fact checker.** Recognize standard textual variants (Q2/F) and per-edition line numbering, and do not flag them as errors. Check the lesson's own internal line references and context claims (e.g., "has done nothing") against the play. (R11.)
5. **Always open with a Yawp! written class starter and offer three exit-ticket options.** De-duplicate stack items, and place handouts at the step where they are used. (Yawp! values; Brian: three exit-ticket options; stack coherence.)
