# b2: Things Fall Apart, Day 1 (Igbo society and who Achebe was writing back to)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Build day 1 of a unit on Things Fall Apart for 10th grade, 50 minutes: Igbo society before the colonizers, and who Chinua Achebe was writing back to. Include the slide deck."

**What came back:** In about 135 seconds, a 7-part plan totaling 50 minutes:
1. What do you know? (5 min)
2. Opening class starter, with three options (5 min)
3. Context in three beats: map and timeline, who the Igbo were, Achebe's argument against Conrad (18 min)
4. Read Chapter 1 aloud (12 min)
5. Pair discussion: "What does Achebe do that Conrad does not?" (6 min)
6. Short Response setup (4 min)
7. Closing and exit ticket (0 min)

The 15-slide deck adds up to 51 minutes. It has one map, one timeline, three photographs (one photo used twice, plus a Conrad portrait), a vocabulary slide, and an Achebe "person" slide with no picture (a slot is noted in the speaker notes). The 3-page handout is the class starter, the Short Response and the exit ticket, each with four ruled lines. The stack after "Add everything" shows 50 minutes and "18% of class is writing" (9 min), but it holds 10 rows, with duplicate class-starter, Short Response and closing rows at 0 minutes. The period label reads "—-minute period". The chat ends with a "Check before class" list that shows raw JSON fragments. Accuracy checks: `work: heart-of-darkness` (it matched the wrong source text), 1 fact corrected, 3 flagged.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** All four arc moves are present: a prior-knowledge probe, a thematic starter ("History is just whichever version of the past won"), direct teaching in short beats, and a text-based discussion. But the order is reversed: the knowledge probe comes before the theme. The opening question about history and winners is never explicitly brought back beside Achebe. Only a speaker note on slide 3 says "it will come back".
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** Reading Chapter 1 aloud with two focused tasks (slide 12: "Mark one detail that makes Okonkwo a specific person, not a type") is good, and the opening sentence is quoted correctly. But students are asked to compare Achebe with Conrad (slides 10, 11, 13) without seeing one line of Heart of Darkness. Achebe himself is misquoted (slide 10). The read slide gives no pages, though the plan says pp. 3–9.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 2.** The three vocabulary terms (chi, egwugwu, agbala, slide 8) are well chosen and correctly defined. The notes on slide 7 link the council of elders to Okonkwo's hunger for titles. But the timeline is out of order. The map places Umuofia and Achebe's childhood at Enugu. The planner itself flagged both problems and left them on screen. "Protectorate" and "colonizer" are never defined.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding: 2.**
  - Map (slide 4): a single scale with no zoom from world to country to Igbo region (R2). It pins Lagos, which is irrelevant to an Igbo novel, and does not shade Igboland.
  - Timeline (slide 5): out of order and not to scale (R3, R11).
  - Photo: one present-day "Igbo family in traditional attire" photo is used on both slides 6 and 7 (R6). It illustrates "long before any European arrived", so it is a modern picture standing in for the pre-colonial past (R13).
  - Faces: Conrad gets a portrait (slide 10) but Achebe does not (slide 9), though Achebe portraits are freely licensed (R4). Okonkwo and Unoka are never introduced by name on screen.
  - Slides 7 and 8 have no picture of an egwugwu mask, an obi, a yam barn or an elders' gathering.
- **E. Writing: 2.** Only 18% of class is writing. The starter gets 4 minutes. The Short Response says students write "10–12 minutes total" but gets 4 minutes. The exit ticket gets 0 minutes in the plan and stack and asks the same question as the Short Response. The handout gives four lines for a "one or two paragraph" response. The objective and both assessments do line up.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** This is a real strength in design. There is a mid-beat check with a re-teach contingency ("If most students are drawing a blank, slow down and re-teach Beat 2"). The exit ticket includes main points, a must-mention and a watch-for ("naming the literary tradition, not just colonialism"). It loses a point because the exit ticket has no time.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The plan is 50 minutes and the deck is 51, and the deck adds a discussion slide not in the plan (slide 11, 2 min). Eighteen minutes of context plus a 5-minute knowledge probe is 23 minutes before the book opens. Chapter 1 (about 6 pages) read aloud in 11–12 minutes is tight. The stack's duplicate 0-minute rows and five "Not in Yawp! yet" assignments (two of them "Quick write") would confuse a teacher. Handouts are one prompt per page with almost no room to write.
- **H. Teacher usability: 2.** There are genuine options: three starters, an audiobook backup, and Short Response in class or as homework. The speaker notes are specific and wise (slide 12: "Achebe gives us reputation before he gives us appearance. Ask why."). But the "Check before class" box shows the teacher raw JSON (`{ "date": "1900", "title": "British protectorate", ...`) and lists errors the planner then shipped anyway. The printable plan reduces the 18-minute context section to one truncated line. There is one exit-ticket option, not three (Brian).
- **I. Access: 2.** There is one sentence stem (slide 13), a three-word glossary and an audiobook suggestion. There is no pronunciation guide for Okonkwo, Umuofia, Unoka or egwugwu, which 10th graders will stumble over aloud. There is no glossary for the context terms, no chunked excerpt, and no frames for the Short Response.
- **Overall: 2.** The concept is exactly right and the speaker notes show real teacherly judgment. But a strong teacher could not project this tomorrow: it has a misordered timeline, a misplaced Umuofia, a misquoted Achebe, a reused modern photo standing in for the pre-colonial past, no Achebe portrait, and no Conrad text to compare against.

## 3. Strengths

1. **The framing is right.** It makes "writing back" the argument of the day and turns it into a reading task: "What does Achebe do in these pages that Conrad does not?", answered from Okonkwo's name, father, village, skill and fear. This is exactly how an expert would teach Day 1.
2. **The speaker notes do real pedagogical work.** Slide 7 ties the council of elders to Okonkwo's titles and chi to the novel's later question. Slide 12 has students pause on the first sentence. The plan's note on agbala and gender ("students will have feelings... worth being ready") is on the teacher's side.
3. **The exit ticket is built for diagnosis.** Its main points, must-mention and watch-for distinguish "tell the African side" from naming the literary tradition, which makes the exit ticket truly diagnostic.

## 4. Problems

1. **The planner flagged its own factual errors and shipped them.** Where: slide 5 and slide 4. On slide 5 the timeline runs 1800s → 1884–85 → 1900 → 1899 → 1958, with "British protectorate" placed before Heart of Darkness. On slide 4, Enugu is labeled "Near Umuofia" and its note says it is "where Achebe grew up". Umuofia is fictional and Achebe grew up in Ogidi, near Onitsha. The "Check before class" box names both errors in raw JSON instead of fixing them. Why it matters: students copy timelines and maps as fact, and a teacher who projects without reading the box teaches errors. Breaks: R3, R11, Brian ("names exactly right").
2. **The Achebe–Conrad argument is taught without Conrad's text, and Achebe is misquoted.** Where: slides 10, 11, 13 and plan Beat 3. Students must say what Achebe does "that Conrad does not" without seeing a single sentence of Heart of Darkness. Its 1899–1902 text is in the public domain, so there is no copyright reason to withhold a short passage. The "thoroughgoing racist book" quotation is not Achebe's wording (see section 5). Why it matters: the comparison becomes taking the teacher's word for it rather than evidence, and the one quotation offered is wrong. Breaks: R9, R11, discussion that requires evidence.
3. **The visuals do not show the subject.** Where: slides 4–9.
   - The same modern family photo appears on slides 6 and 7 as the image of a society "long before any European arrived".
   - Achebe has no face, while Conrad does.
   - Slides 7 and 8 show no egwugwu mask, obi, yam barn or village.
   - The map never zooms from world to Nigeria to Igboland.

   Why it matters: dual coding fails exactly where 10th graders need concrete images to replace the "generic Africa" the lesson is trying to dislodge. Breaks: R2, R4, R6, R7, R13.
4. **Writing gets too little time, and the writing tasks repeat each other.** Where: plan steps 6–7, stack, handout. Only 18% of class is writing. The Short Response is allotted 4 minutes for a 10–12 minute task. The exit ticket is 0 minutes and asks the same question. The handout gives four lines for "one or two paragraphs". Why it matters: the only extended writing slides to homework, and the exit ticket gives no new information. Breaks: Yawp! writing values, alignment, feasibility.
5. **The stack and printouts are messy.** Where: stack, plan PDF, handout.
   - The stack has duplicate class-starter, Short Response and closing rows at 0 minutes, and the period label is blank ("—-minute period").
   - The plan PDF truncates the 18-minute context section to one line.
   - The handouts print one short prompt per page, with chatty instructions on the exit ticket ("Keep it conversational... Don't worry too much about polish").

   Why it matters: a teacher clicking "Add everything" gets a run of show that does not match the plan they read. Breaks: feasibility, materials ready to print.

Smaller problems:
- The "what Achebe was writing back to" story leaves out Joyce Cary's *Mister Johnson*, which Achebe named as a spur. It also leaves out the novel's own reply in its last paragraph (the District Commissioner's *The Pacification of the Primitive Tribes of the Lower Niger*).
- The Yeats title source ("The Second Coming") is absent.
- "European powers drew the borders of Africa in a room in Germany" (slide 5 notes) overstates what the Berlin Conference did.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **Achebe quotation (slide 10, plan Beat 3):** "a thoroughgoing racist book". Achebe's revised essay "An Image of Africa" says "Joseph Conrad was a thoroughgoing racist". The 1975 lecture said "a bloody racist". The phrase describes Conrad the man, not "the book". Confidence: fairly high.
- **Timeline order (slide 5):** 1900 placed before 1899. Certain.
- **Map (slide 4):** Enugu labeled "Near Umuofia" and described as "where Achebe grew up". Achebe was born and raised in Ogidi (Anambra), and Umuofia is fictional. High confidence.
- **"British protectorate... beginning direct colonial rule" (slide 5):** this is misleading. Southern Nigeria was governed largely through indirect rule (warrant chiefs in Igboland). British protectorates in the Niger Delta region (Oil Rivers, 1884–85; Niger Coast, 1893) predate 1900. Moderate-high confidence.
- **"Penguin audiobook read by Peter Francis James" (plan step 4):** the Peter Francis James recording exists, but I believe it is from Recorded Books/Audible, not Penguin. Low-moderate confidence.
- **Correct:** the opening sentence ("Okonkwo was well known throughout the nine villages and even beyond."), the agbala gloss, chi and egwugwu, Achebe 1930–2013 born in Ogidi, 1958 publication, independence 1960, the Berlin Conference 1884–85 with no Africans present, Heart of Darkness 1899 (serial).

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Make fact-check flags blocking.** When the planner's own check flags a date-order, place or name error in deck JSON, it must correct it (sort timelines by date, drop or relabel fictional-place pins) before showing the deck. It must never print raw JSON to the teacher. (R3, R11)
2. **Show the "writing back" target.** When a lesson's argument is a comparison with another text, put a short excerpt of that text on screen, choosing public-domain passages first and using a labeled slot if the text is copyrighted. Verify any critic quotation against the source before using it. (R9, R10, R11)
3. **Enforce the image rules.**
   - No image may repeat within a deck.
   - Every "person" slide for the author must get a portrait or fail loudly.
   - Historical-claim slides must reject modern photos.
   - Context maps must use a two-step zoom and shade the actual region (e.g. Igboland), not only the modern country.

   (R2, R4, R6, R13)
4. **Give writing real time.** Never give a writing assessment 0 minutes. A Short Response gets the minutes its own instructions state. An exit ticket must not repeat the Short Response question; offer three exit-ticket options (check understanding, explain a term, ask a question). Size handout ruled space to the task. (Yawp! values, Brian)
5. **Build the stack from one source.** "Add everything" should produce exactly the plan's steps, with no 0-minute duplicate rows. The period length should carry through, and the printable plan should keep each step's substance. (feasibility, teacher usability)
