# d2: "To be, or not to be" close reading (Hamlet 3.1, 12th grade)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Teach a 50-minute lesson for 12th grade on Hamlet's 'To be, or not to be' soliloquy (Act 3, Scene 1). Include the slide deck."

**What came back:** A six-part plan totalling 50 minutes: Class starter (5; three prompt options), Context (5), Read aloud (8), Close reading: the argument (12; 2-minute teacher model, 8 pairs, 2 share), Short Response (12; two options plus a stray "Class Starter" card holding a third Hamlet prompt), Closing discussion and exit ticket (8; three exit-ticket options). A 15-slide deck ("51 min for a 50-minute period", flagged in the UI). A 5-page student handout (starter page, Short Response page, exit-ticket page, then the full soliloquy in three movements with questions and a vocabulary list). A 2-page printable plan. The stack after "Add everything": 50 of 50 minutes, 50% writing (25 min), eight rows, with the close-reading handout as the last row (0 min, after the exit ticket). The reply opens with leaked tool chatter ("Good - Short Response, Class Starter, and Exit Ticket are all available. Now I have everything I need.") and a "Check before class" note about line numbers. Checks: work "none" (no source text matched), quotationsVerified 0, factsCorrected 4, factsFlagged 1.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** Opens on the theme in students' own lives (starter "The thing you're most afraid of is probably the thing you most need to do"; slide 3 "Most regrets are about what we didn't do"), short direct teaching, close reading, then a whole-class question ("has Hamlet decided anything?"). But the tie-back to the starter is only a speaker-note promise ("you will bring it back when Hamlet says something similar"); no step sets students' starter answers beside Hamlet's reasoning.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 3.** The full soliloquy is printed accurately on handout p. 4-5 in three labelled movements, and slides 9 and 10 put the actual lines on screen. Weaknesses: the passage is the last section of the handout (after the exit ticket), lines are unnumbered though notes say "point them to lines 78-83", there is no margin room to annotate, and there is no Movement 2 slide.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 3.** Context is short and right (Ghost's word, no proof, Mousetrap still to come, Ophelia as bait; slide 5 timeline of Acts 1-3), and the vocabulary is well chosen (slide 6; handout p. 5: bodkin, quietus, fardels, conscience as consciousness, sicklied o'er). Slide 7 states as fact a contestable claim (see section 5).
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R2-R7, R13): 2.** Two pictures in 15 slides: Kronborg/Elsinore on slide 4 (good, credited) and a vanitas skull still life on slide 11 (a mood image, garbled credit "Originally from an unknown description page..."). Slides 5, 6, 7, 9, 10 teach content in words only; the three missing pictures (Hamlet in production, the Globe, Shakespeare) are noted as slots in speaker notes, honest per R10, but nothing is on the slide.
- **E. Writing: 3.** 50% of class is writing: starter, a graded 12-minute Short Response, exit ticket. The chosen Short Response ("Pick one image ... carrying more than it first appears to") is a good prompt but does not measure the stated objective (the argument and what it reveals about Hamlet); the stray option "Hamlet builds a logical case for not acting - and then undercuts it" would, and it is mislabelled as a Class Starter.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** Teacher model, circulating with clear listen-fors ("the undiscovered country", "conscience does make cowards of us all"), share-outs, a contested whole-class question and an aligned exit ticket ("explain what Hamlet actually decides"). No quick check after the first read (e.g., "what are the two choices?") before pairs start.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** Plan minutes add up to 50 but the deck runs 51 and its timings differ from the plan (starter 4 vs 5; close reading 14 vs 12). The deck asks the same question twice (slide 12 "By the end of the speech, has Hamlet decided anything?" before writing; slide 14 again after), the handout and plan PDF both print the passage after the exit ticket, and the plan's "lines 56-90 ... 35 lines" is inconsistent with the handout's movement labels and the checker's own note.
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** Options throughout (three starters, three exit tickets, "you can also assign this as homework"), warm and specific speaker notes with think-aloud scripts. Undercut by the leaked tool sentence, the mislabelled card in step 5, and a "Check before class" note that leaves the teacher to sort out line numbers.
- **I. Access: 2.** A vocabulary list (at the end of the handout, not beside the lines), pairing a weaker reader with a stronger one, sentence frames on slides 11, 12, 14 and in slide 13's notes. No line-by-line gloss or modern paraphrase, no chunked version, no frames on the handout itself.
- **Overall: 3.** A strong teacher would use this with edits: move the passage to the front of the handout, fix slide 7, cut slide 12, and swap in the argument-focused Short Response.

## 3. Strengths

1. The text is right and whole: the full soliloquy, accurately quoted (handout p. 4-5), divided into three sensible movements each with one focused question.
2. The close-reading step has a real "I do, we do, you do": a 2-minute think-aloud on lines 1-3 ("He's setting up a choice - endure, or fight back. That's not a feeling; that's a position"), then pairs, then share-out with named listen-fors.
3. The closing question "By the end of the speech, has Hamlet decided anything?" is a genuinely open, text-based question, and the exit ticket measures it directly.

## 4. Problems

1. **The passage is printed last.** Where: handout p. 4-5 and stack row 8 / plan step 7 ("To be, or not to be - Close Reading", 0 min, after the exit ticket). Why: students need the text from minute 10 (read-aloud); a teacher printing "everything in order" hands out a packet whose reading is behind the exit ticket. Breaks: R9; materials ready to use.
2. **A contestable interpretation is taught as fact.** Where: slide 7 "there is no performance for another character" and plan step 2 "The audience is the only witness". In 3.1 Claudius and Polonius have just hidden to spy on Hamlet; whether he knows or suspects it is a classic interpretive question (slide 6 itself says, more carefully, "while Hamlet believes he is alone"). Why: seniors are handed a closed answer to the scene's best open question. Breaks: R11; Brian "no rabbit holes" cuts both ways, but accuracy first.
3. **Slides teach in words only.** Where: slides 5, 6, 7, 9, 10; only slide 4 has a picture of the play's world; slide 11's skull painting is decorative and badly credited. Why: no dual coding of the staging (actor alone on stage, the hidden listeners) that the lesson's key idea depends on. Breaks: R6, R7, R13, R11 (credit).
4. **The graded writing does not measure the objective, and the right prompt is mislabelled.** Where: step 5; the default Short Response is "an image doing extra work", while the objective is the speech's argument; the argument prompt appears as a "Class Starter - opens the period" card inside step 5. Why: the graded work assesses a different skill than the lesson teaches. Breaks: alignment of task to objective.
5. **Deck and plan drift.** Where: deck 51 min vs plan 50; slide 12 duplicates slide 14; no Movement 2 slide; slide notes reference line numbers (78-83) not printed on the handout. Why: a teacher running from the deck runs over and repeats the discussion. Breaks: Brian "a deck's minutes must add up"; Feasibility.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- Soliloquy text on handout p. 4-5 and slides 9-10: checked line by line against the standard modern text; **no errors found** (variants such as "despised"/"despis'd", "pitch"/"pith" are legitimate editorial choices). High confidence.
- "The audience is the only witness ... there is no performance for another character" (plan step 2, slide 7): **misleading**; Claudius and Polonius are concealed and listening in 3.1. High confidence.
- "The soliloquy runs 35 lines (lines 56-90)": the speech is about 33 lines (roughly 56-88 in Arden/Riverside lineation; 64-98 in Folger); the handout's "Movement 3 (lines 74-90)" includes lines that are not in the speech. Minor; medium-high confidence.
- Slide 11 image credit "Originally from an unknown description page..." is not a credit (R11).
- Context facts (Ghost's accusation, Mousetrap in 3.2, Ophelia used as bait) are correct.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Order handouts by when students need them, not by stack order**: a reading passage used in any step goes first in the packet (and the stack should put it with the read-aloud step, not at the end with 0 minutes). (R9; ready to use)
2. **Flag interpretive claims**: when the planner states what a scene "means" (who is listening, what a character intends), require either a text citation or wording as a question/option; add "eavesdroppers in 3.1" type facts to the play's knowledge file. (R11; discussion with evidence)
3. **Default the graded writing prompt to the one that matches the objective**, and validate card labels (a prompt inside the Short Response step cannot render as a Class Starter). Strip tool-status sentences from the reply. (Alignment; teacher usability)
4. **Deck builder must mirror the plan**: same minutes per step, one slide per handout movement, no duplicate discussion slides, line numbers printed on the handout if notes cite them. (Brian: minutes add up; R9)
5. **Picture search for drama should look for production/staging images** (stage photos, the Globe, period playbills), and reject images with no real credit; decorative vanitas/still-life images should not count as content visuals. (R6, R7, R11, R13)
