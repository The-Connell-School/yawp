# b1 — Of Mice and Men unit plan (10th grade, three weeks)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Build a unit plan for Of Mice and Men for 10th grade: three weeks, 50-minute periods, ending in an argumentative essay on whether George was right."

**What came back (87 s, one turn):** A 15-day unit board (15 x 50 min = 750 min), ending in the Yawp! Thesis-Driven Essay "Was George right to shoot Lennie?". Days 1-7 are reading days (Day 1 context on the Great Depression / California farm labor with a prediction sentence; Days 2-7 one chapter a night with an in-class key passage: the rabbit-farm speech, the Curley introduction, Candy's dog, Crooks's speech, the barn scene, the ending). Days 8-11 are four consecutive Yawp! Writing Fundamentals workshops (thesis, topic sentences, evidence, analysis), Day 12 drafting, Day 13 revising hooks/conclusions, Day 14 essay due plus Socratic seminar, Day 15 reflection on the Day 1 prediction. Each day has an objective, reading, student activity, a "why" and a check. No deck and no handouts (unit plan; slides.pdf 404, handouts.pdf 409). A 5-page printable unit plan PDF. There is no "Add everything" button for a unit, so the stack the test opened is an empty "Untitled lesson" (0 of 50 minutes, 0% writing). The planner's accuracy checks ran with `work: none` and found nothing to check (no quotations were given).

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** The unit's arc is sound: Day 1 opens on the theme through the students' own lives ("Have you ever had to make a choice for someone else...?"), names the central question, and has students predict an answer. Day 15 returns to that prediction. Most reading days open on a personal starter that is set beside the chapter (Day 4 "Is it ever kind to end a life?" leads into Candy's dog). The weak point is the end: the Socratic seminar on the central question comes on Day 14, *after* the essays are handed in, so the discussion cannot shape the writing.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 3.** It picks the right passages (rabbit speech, Candy's dog as a rehearsal for the ending, Crooks, the barn, Chapter 6 annotated for and against George). But no passage is quoted, no page or paragraph range is given (only "the Curley introduction passage"), and Day 5's check refers to "Crooks's bunkhouse scene", which is wrong: Crooks is shut out of the bunkhouse and the scene is in his room off the barn.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 2.** Day 1's "Great Depression / California farm-labor context mini-lesson" has no content at all (no Dust Bowl, migrant workers, bindlestiffs, Salinas Valley, or Soledad), and the unit defines no vocabulary. The ranch slang and dialect (bindle, swamper, skinner, jerkline, "the can", "cat house") are a real barrier for 10th graders. The writing mini-lessons point to Yawp! Writing Fundamentals and do not teach anything specific to this book.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding: null** (unit plan, no deck).
- **E. Writing: 3.** There is a lot of writing (a starter or Short Response almost every day, timed writes on Days 4 and 6, a skills sequence that builds into the essay, Day 15 reflection) and it lines up with the essay. For an *argumentative* essay, though, counterargument is never taught: it shows up only as an extra for "students who finish" on Day 12. Revision on Day 13 covers only hooks and conclusions, not the argument.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** Every day has a named check (Day 6: "List George's three most realistic options. Next to each, write one consequence."), and the checks are good. But nothing checks the homework reading on Days 2-7, and the whole plan depends on students reading a chapter each night.
- **G. Feasibility: 3.** The days add up to exactly 15 x 50 min and a chapter a night is realistic. Writing the whole draft in one 50-minute period (Day 12) is tight. The printable PDF cuts off text mid-sentence with "…" (Day 1 "preview the…", Day 4 "a rehearsal for the…", Day 14 "Students…"), and page 4 is blank except for a stray "b.". The stack is an empty "Untitled lesson", so the teacher has nothing to schedule until each day is built.
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** The plan offers real options ("Chapter 6 is short enough to read entirely in class on Day 7 if you prefer"; "you could spread them out ... I can rebuild the map that way") and ends on a closing question. The "why" lines are useful. It gives no warning about sensitive content (see Problem 2), and there is a small inconsistency: Day 1 says the prediction "becomes a thesis draft on Day 14", but the thesis is drafted on Day 8.
- **I. Access: 2.** It offers nothing for struggling readers or English learners reading a chapter a night at home: no audiobook option, no chapter summaries or guiding questions, no dialect glossary. Day 12's "sentence starters and the Yawp! tutor" is the only scaffold in the unit.
- **Overall: 3.** It is a solid, teachable skeleton with the right key passages and a real writing arc. A strong teacher would use it after fixing the seminar timing, adding counterargument and a vocabulary/context lesson with real content, and planning how to handle the slur and the disability and violence content.

## 3. Strengths

1. **The Candy's-dog parallel is the hinge of the unit.** Day 4 asks "who decides, who acts, who is absent" and "Is this scene a rehearsal for the ending?" That is exactly the evidence a student needs for the essay, and it is planted ten days early.
2. **Day 6 is excellent essay preparation.** The class stops at the moment Lennie realizes what he has done, students write George's options, the teacher lists them, and the check is "three most realistic options + one consequence each". Students build the essay's argument space themselves, before they read the ending.
3. **The unit is bookended.** The Day 1 prediction comes back in a Day 15 Short Response ("Has your answer changed? What in the novel changed it?"), which shows students how far their thinking has moved, and Day 7's two-column annotation (for George / against George) feeds directly into the essay.

## 4. Problems (most serious first)

1. **The seminar comes after the essay is due.** On Day 14 essays are "submitted in Yawp!. Then: Socratic seminar". Discussion that requires evidence is the best rehearsal for an argument, and here it happens when the arguments can no longer change. Students lose the chance to test their claims and hear counterarguments before writing. This breaks evidence-based discussion and the R12 step where students weigh in and the text is set beside what they said.
2. **No plan for the novel's sensitive content.** Crooks's speech (Day 5, "read in class") and much of the novel repeat the n-word, and the unit centers on killing a man with an intellectual disability and on the death of Curley's wife. The plan says nothing about how to handle the slur in read-alouds, how to frame Lennie's disability, or what to tell families. Any department chair would require this before a read-aloud day. This breaks Brian's "expert, kind, on the teacher's side" and R1.
3. **Counterargument is never taught for an argumentative essay.** The skills days are thesis, topic sentence, evidence, analysis, then hooks and conclusions. Counterclaim and rebuttal (the defining move of the genre, and CCSS W.9-10.1b) appear only as an extra for fast finishers on Day 12. The prompt has two strong sides, and students who never answer the other side write one-sided essays. This breaks objective/assessment alignment.
4. **The context day and vocabulary are empty.** The Day 1 "context mini-lesson" has no facts, images, or terms, and no day defines the ranch vocabulary or 1930s dialect. Students meet "bindle", "swamper", "skinner", "jerkline" and the Weed backstory without support, and the world of the novel (migrant labor, Soledad, the Salinas Valley) is never shown. This breaks R1, R5 and cognitive load.
5. **Writing instruction is cut off from the text, and the printable plan is broken.** Days 8-11 are four back-to-back generic Writing Fundamentals lessons, so the novel disappears for a week, which the planner itself admits. Meanwhile the printed PDF truncates days mid-sentence with "…" and has a near-blank page 4. A teacher printing the plan loses the instructions for Days 1, 4, 8, 10, 11, 13 and 14. This breaks spacing/connection to the unit and Yawp!'s "materials ready to print and use".

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **Day 5 check: "What does Crooks's bunkhouse scene add..."** This is wrong. Crooks is barred from the bunkhouse because he is Black, and the Chapter 4 scene takes place in his room in the harness room off the barn. The error inverts the point of the scene. (High confidence.)
- **Day 1 "buildsTo": "the prediction becomes a thesis draft on Day 14"**, but the thesis is drafted on Day 8. This is an internal inconsistency, not a fact about the book.
- The rest checks out: the rabbit speech in Ch. 1, Curley's introduction in Ch. 2, Candy's dog shot by Carlson in Ch. 3, Crooks in Ch. 4, the barn and Curley's wife in Ch. 5, and Ch. 6 as the short final chapter. No quotations were given, so there are none to check. (Fairly confident overall.)

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **When a unit ends in an argument essay, place the evidence-based discussion before drafting.** For any argumentative or thesis-driven essay, schedule the seminar or debate between the thesis and drafting days, and include a counterclaim-and-rebuttal lesson. (Objective/assessment alignment; R12 step 4)
2. **Flag sensitive content for canonical texts.** When the text is known to contain slurs, sexual violence, or similar material (Of Mice and Men, Huck Finn, To Kill a Mockingbird, Things Fall Apart), the unit board should carry a short teacher note: what is there, which days it falls on, and options for handling it ("if you like, ..."). (Brian: on the teacher's side; R1)
3. **Give unit context days and vocabulary real content.** Every unit's Day 1 should carry a few concrete facts (where, when, who) and a short list of words students will meet in the first chapters, not just a label like "context mini-lesson". (R1, R5)
4. **Weave writing lessons into reading days by default.** Default to pairing each Writing Fundamentals skill with a reading day and a passage from the book, rather than stacking four generic workshops after the reading. (Spacing; connection to the text)
5. **Fix the unit-plan printing and the unit stack.** The unit PDF must print each day's "In class" text in full (no "…" truncation, no orphan page), and "Add everything" or the stack should say plainly that a unit is built day by day instead of opening an empty "Untitled lesson". (Materials ready to use)
