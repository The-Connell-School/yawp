# a2: To Kill a Mockingbird, day 1 context (9th grade)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Build day 1 of a unit on To Kill a Mockingbird for 9th grade, 50 minutes: Maycomb, the Great Depression and Jim Crow, the world of the novel before we read. Include the slide deck."

**What came back:** One turn (132 s). A six-part plan adding to 50 minutes: class starter (5, three new prompts: unfair rules / who gets the benefit of the doubt / what a place expects), "What do you already know?" call-out (3), direct teaching from the deck (18), pair discussion on the cost of speaking up (8), Short Response (12, three options), closing with exit ticket (4, three options: explain Jim Crow, Depression's effect, wondering), plus a thoughtful care note on the n-word and racial violence. Deck: 14 slides, 51 minutes by its own count; photographs on two slides (segregated water fountains, slide 9; the Scottsboro defendants, slide 12), a map (slide 5) and a timeline (slide 6); Plessy v. Ferguson picture slot left empty with a note (slide 8). Handout PDF: 3 pages, the Yawp! writing sheets only (starter, Short Response, exit ticket). Stack after "Add everything": 50 minutes planned, "42% of class is writing · 21 min"; the period-length chip reads "--minute period". Chat ends with "Check before class" flagging two facts (Harper Lee's birth year, Montgomery's direction) that the deck still contains.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 4.** Textbook day-1 arc: opens in students' experience (unfair rules), brief know-check written on the board and revisited at the close, direct teaching one idea per slide, then pairs and individual writing that return to the opening question ("Does something being legal make it right?"). The modelled bridge ("You said a minute ago that some people get trusted... In 1930s Alabama, that wasn't just a feeling — it was the law") is exactly R12(4).
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** No line of the novel and no primary document (a Jim Crow statute, a Depression-era photograph with source) is read; on a context day that is defensible, but the novel facts given are wrong: Scout is "8 years old in 1933" (slides 6, 11, closing script) and the Depression example "a can of beans" is not in the book.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 2.** Definitions are short and placed before use (slides 7, 10), but the content contains errors a 9th-grade teacher would repeat aloud: Scout's age, Harper Lee "born the same year" (1925), "separate courtrooms", and Jim Crow segregation laws dated from 1877. Only three terms are defined; none of the words students meet in chapters 1-2.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R2-R7, R13): 2.** Good choices where present (fountains photo; Scottsboro photo). But the map (slide 5) is a single continental view with pins and labels crowded together, no step from country to Alabama to Monroeville (R2); the two timeline images in the JSON do not appear in the deck PDF; slides 7, 8, 10 and 11 teach content with words only (no Depression photograph, no Harper Lee face, no Monroeville courthouse) (R4, R7, R13).
- **E. Writing: 3.** 42% writing; starter, a strong 12-minute Short Response with three real options, and an exit ticket on Jim Crow as a legal system that measures the objective. The exit-ticket sheet gives six lines; no pre-writing note-catcher for the 18-minute teaching block.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** Know-check, pair share-out with a listen-for ("courage"), exit ticket with a watch-for (confusing Jim Crow with slavery). Nothing checks understanding inside the 18-minute direct-teaching block.
- **G. Feasibility: 3.** Plan and stack add to 50, but the deck is 51 minutes; the discussion slide says 13 minutes against the plan's 8; deck direct-teaching slides total 13 minutes against the plan's 18; the plan points to "slides 3–14" when direct teaching is slides 5-11.
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** Options everywhere (three starters, three Short Responses, three exit tickets, each explained), an excellent care note, speaker notes that say what to say. Undercut by the note describing Bruegel's *Children's Games* "on the title slide, beside the big idea, and at the exit" when no such art is in the deck, and by "Check before class" flagging errors the deck still prints.
- **I. Access: 2.** Sentence stems on both discussion slides and in the plan; definitions with examples. No note-catcher or visual vocabulary for a dense 18-minute block, small body text on the map and people slides, nothing for ELs on the regional and legal vocabulary.
- **Overall: 3.** A strong teacher would use the arc and the writing tomorrow, after fixing Scout's age, Lee's birth year and the courtroom claim on three slides and adding real pictures to the teaching slides.

## 3. Strengths

1. The arc is right for a day 1 (R12): personal unfair-rules opener, a board list of what students know that is revisited at the close, and a Short Response ("Does something being legal make it right?") that ties the history back to the opening question.
2. The care note is expert and kind: decide in advance how the class will handle the n-word, say it will appear, check in quietly with students for whom this lands close to home. This is what a department chair would want a new teacher told.
3. The two photographs are the right kind of picture (R6): the segregated water fountains beside "Jim Crow in practice" (slide 9) and the Scottsboro defendants (slide 12), the real Alabama case behind Tom Robinson's.

## 4. Problems

1. **Wrong facts about the novel itself, stated three times.** Where: slide 6 ("Scout Finch is 8 years old in 1933", "1925 Scout is born"), slide 11 ("Our narrator, age 8"), closing script ("She's eight years old, it's 1933"). Scout is almost six when the novel opens in summer 1933; she is eight at the trial in 1935. Why: students will meet a six-year-old on page one; the teacher looks careless on day 1. Principle: R11.
2. **Errors the checker caught are not fixed in the deck.** Where: "Check before class" flags Lee's birth (1926, not 1925) and Montgomery "north", but slide 6 and slide 5 still print them. Why: the teacher must hand-edit a deck they did not write, and may not read the footnote. Principle: R11; Brian "names exactly right".
3. **Visuals thin where the teaching is.** Where: slide 5 map is a continent-wide view with overlapping labels (R2); timeline images absent from the deck PDF (slide 6); slides 7, 8, 10, 11 are text only: no Depression photograph, no Harper Lee photo, no Monroeville courthouse, no picture for Plessy (R4, R7, R13). Why: a context day lives or dies on students seeing the world; 9th graders get a wall of definitions instead.
4. **Deck minutes do not match the plan.** Where: deck 51 minutes; slide 12 discussion 13 minutes vs. plan 8; direct teaching slides 13 minutes vs. plan 18; plan says "slides 3–14". Why: a teacher pacing by the deck will run over or rush the Short Response. Principle: Brian (a deck's minutes must add up), G.
5. **Phantom art note and thin R1 coverage.** Where: the chat describes Bruegel's *Children's Games* on three slides; none exists. And the day equips students with three terms but none of the words or places of chapters 1-2 (the Radley Place, Finch's Landing, Cunningham, "entailment"). Why: a context day should prepare students to read this book (R1, R5); the false art note erodes trust.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- Scout "8 years old in 1933" / "born 1925" (slides 6, 11; closing): wrong; she is nearly six in 1933, eight in 1935. High confidence.
- "Harper Lee is born the same year" (1925) (slide 6): Lee was born 28 April 1926. High confidence (the planner's own check agreed but did not fix it).
- Montgomery "90 miles north" of Monroeville (slide 5): it is north-east, roughly 80-90 miles. Minor, high confidence.
- "Separate courtrooms" (slide 7 example; slide 9 facts): Jim Crow courts were segregated in seating (the novel's "colored balcony"), not separate courtrooms. High confidence.
- Atticus "paid in food — a can of beans, a bag of hickory nuts" (slide 10): in chapter 2 Mr. Cunningham pays with stovewood, hickory nuts, smilax and holly, and a crokersack of turnips; no can of beans. The plan's "turnips" is right. High confidence.
- "1877 Jim Crow begins ... Southern states pass laws enforcing racial segregation" (slide 6) and "1877–1965" (slide 7): a common simplification; most segregation statutes followed in the 1880s-1900s, after Plessy (1896). Medium confidence it should be corrected for accuracy.
- Bruegel *Children's Games* described as on three slides: not in the deck. High confidence.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. When the fact checker flags a claim, rewrite the slide and plan text, not just append a "Check before class" note; block the reply if a flagged claim still appears verbatim in the deck. (R11)
2. Add a novel-facts reference for commonly taught books (narrator age at opening, setting year, key names, chapter-level details) and check deck claims about characters against it. (R11, R1)
3. Map layout: require a stepped view (country, then state, then town) or at least zoom to the state when all pins are within one state; reject labels that overlap. (R2)
4. Picture coverage rule: every content slide on a context day must request a picture of the thing itself (a Depression photograph of rural Alabama, the author's face, the real town), and timeline images must render in the exported PDF. (R4, R7, R13)
5. Validate that the deck's slide minutes sum to the lesson length and that each plan step's minutes equal its slides' minutes; never describe art or slides that are not in the built deck. (Brian deck minutes, G, H)
