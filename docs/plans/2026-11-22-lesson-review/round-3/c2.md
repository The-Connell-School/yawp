# c2: Can We Trust Nick? The Great Gatsby, Chapter 1

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Teach a 50-minute lesson for 11th grade on chapter 1 of The Great Gatsby: can we trust Nick as a narrator? Close reading of the opening pages. Include the slide deck."

**What came back:** The planner replied in 111 s with a five-part plan totaling 50 minutes:
- class starter, 5 min (three options, all written by the planner)
- Context: Who Is Nick Carraway?, 5 min
- Close Reading: The Opening Pages, 20 min (read-aloud, teacher think-aloud, 3-min pairs, 5-min two-column board "What Nick Claims / What the Text Shows")
- Short Response, 12 min
- Closing, 8 min (3-min share of one sentence plus a 5-min Yawp! exit ticket on what an unreliable narrator is and why Nick might be one)

The plan already included a one-page "Close Reading Annotation Guide" handout. Because of that, `HANDOUT=1` produced no second turn (log: "no handout suggestion offered"), and there is no reply2. The deck has 13 slides labeled "51 min" and flagged "51 min for a 50-minute period." The handouts PDF is four pages: starter, Short Response, exit ticket, annotation guide. The stack runs 50 minutes and shows "50% of class is writing · 25 min." The handout sits at the end of the stack as a 0-minute "Read aloud" item at minute 50.

The planner's own checks (`quotationsCorrected 3, quotationsVerified 4, quotationsMisplaced 2, factsFlagged 2`) produced a "Check before class" note in the reply:
- "I am one of the few honest people that I have ever known" is in Chapter 3, not Chapter 1.
- "Her voice is full of money" is in Chapter 7, not Chapter 1.

Neither error was fixed in the lesson itself.

## 3. Strengths

1. **The two-column board is a real close-reading engine** (step 3, slide 10). "What Nick Claims / What the Text Shows" makes students hold a claim against evidence, and it feeds directly into the Short Response. That is exactly the move this question needs.
2. **Modeling before release** (step 3, slide 7 notes). The teacher thinks aloud on one moment before pairs try, which is a genuine I do / we do / you do sequence.
3. **The speaker notes and exit-ticket plan are expert and kind** (slides 11–13). Examples: "Don't interrupt writers who are going," and sorting exit tickets into "named a moment and made a claim / named a moment but stayed general / defined the term without the text." That is how a strong teacher plans tomorrow from today.

## 4. Problems (most serious first)

1. **The key quotation is from Chapter 3, taught as Chapter 1 (R11, R9; Brian: quotations exactly right).** The line is "I am one of the few honest people that I have ever known." It appears on slide 7, in the model think-aloud (step 3), in the Short Response prompt ("In the opening pages of Chapter 1, Nick Carraway tells us he is 'one of the few honest people'..."), and in starter option 2. Students reading Chapter 1 will search for a line that isn't there, and the graded prompt is false. The planner detected this (its "Check before class") but did not repair it. Chapter 1 has better evidence on its first page that the lesson never uses:
   - "Reserving judgments is a matter of infinite hope."
   - "after boasting this way of my tolerance, I come to the admission that it has a limit"
   - Gatsby "represented everything for which I have an unaffected scorn"
2. **The reading is never supplied, and its endpoint is wrong (R9, R10; close reading).** The reading slide (slide 6) has no passage, and the handout has none either. The passage is given as "the first 6–7 pages of most editions, ending around '…Her voice is full of money'," but that line is in Chapter 7. A close-reading lesson on a public-domain novel should put the paragraphs on the page, with line numbers.
3. **The father's advice is misquoted (R11).** Step 2 has "...haven't had the advantages that you've." The text reads "that you've had." The original also reads "criticizing any one," not "anyone." This is the novel's most-quoted opening, and students will check it against their books.
4. **Materials are out of order or unusable (feasibility; Yawp! ready-to-print).** The annotation guide promises writing space for two questions, but the space collapsed (handout p. 4). The handout is placed last in the stack and the packet (stack minute 50, 0 minutes, labeled "Read aloud"), though it is handed out before the reading. The deck runs 51 minutes.
5. **The visuals are decorative and repeated (R6, R7, R13).** The same book cover is on slides 3 and 4. Fitzgerald's portrait sits beside a claim about Tom (slide 8). Nothing shows the world of Chapter 1 (East and West Egg, the Buchanans' house) or the characters Nick introduces (Tom, Daisy, Jordan) before students meet them (R4).

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- "I am one of the few honest people that I have ever known" is presented as Chapter 1 (slide 7, step 3, Short Response, starter 2). **It is Chapter 3** (end of the chapter, after the Jordan Baker passage). High confidence.
- The reading endpoint "…Her voice is full of money" is given as Chapter 1. **It is Chapter 7** (Gatsby to Nick, in the Plaza/Tom's-house sequence). High confidence.
- "...haven't had the advantages that you've." **The text reads "...that you've had."** The original also has "criticizing any one." High confidence.
- "Within two pages, he has judged Tom as 'one of those men'" (slide 8). The "one of those men who reach such an acute limited excellence at twenty-one" description comes several pages after the father's advice, not within two. Medium-high confidence.
- Slide 2 says Nick "says he never judges anyone — then judges everyone." That is an oversimplification: Nick himself concedes on page 2 that his tolerance "has a limit." Interpretive, but students should be shown that line.
- Correct: Nick went to Yale ("New Haven in 1915"), is Daisy's cousin (second cousin once removed), and narrates in retrospect ("When I came back from the East last autumn"). The cover credit (Francis Cugat) is right.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Turn quotation-check findings into repairs, not footnotes.** When a quotation is "misplaced" (here it was flagged twice), rewrite every block that uses it (slides, model, prompts, starters) or swap in an in-chapter quotation. Never ship a lesson with a known-wrong chapter attribution in a graded prompt (R11; Brian).
2. **Supply the passage for public-domain texts.** If the work is public domain (Gatsby since 2021), put the close-reading paragraphs on the reading slide and on the handout, with line numbers and room to annotate. If it is not, state exact page and paragraph anchors, verified to sit in the assigned chapter (R9, R10).
3. **Choose evidence from the assigned chapter only.** Restrict excerpt and model quotations to the requested chapter. Prefer the text's own self-contradictions, such as Chapter 1's "my tolerance... has a limit" (close reading; R11).
4. **Fix materials rendering and ordering.** Preserve answer space where the handout says "in the space below." Order handouts and stack items by when they are used: the annotation guide comes before the read-aloud, at its real minute (Yawp! ready-to-print; G).
5. **Add images by purpose, not by subject tag.** One book cover at most. Character and setting introductions (Tom, Daisy, Jordan, East and West Egg) come before the reading. No author portrait beside a character claim (R4, R6, R13).
