# b2: Things Fall Apart, Day 1: Igbo Society and the Writer's Purpose

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Build day 1 of a unit on Things Fall Apart for 10th grade, 50 minutes: Igbo society before the colonizers, and who Chinua Achebe was writing back to. Include the slide deck." (HANDOUT=0; one turn, 210 s, no failures.)

**What came back:** A six-part chat plan totaling 50 minutes: class starter (5, three prompt options), "Who Achebe was writing back to" (8, four direct-teach points: Conrad, Achebe's counter-move, the Yeats title, a "writing back" question for the board), "Where and when" (12, map, timeline and five vocabulary items), close reading of Chapter 1 (15, circle/underline annotation on a teacher-pasted passage), share and connect (5), and exit ticket (5, three options). After that come a note about a van Gogh painting and a "Check before class" list of four facts the planner itself flagged. The deck has 14 slides, and its minutes sum to 50. It has three photographs: Conrad on slide 4, Yeats on slide 7, and a modern Igbo family on slide 11. Slide 8 is a map and slide 9 a timeline. Slides 5 and 6 have labeled empty slots for pictures. The planner produced a three-page handout anyway: the starter with lines, a Chapter 1 page with a paste slot, and the exit prompt with lines. The stack shows 50 minutes planned and "20% of class is writing · 10 min" (starter plus exit). It has seven rows, one of which is a 0-minute "Chapter 1 — Close Reading" row that duplicates the handout. The printable plan PDF has an empty Goals box ("Add this lesson's goals…") and cuts steps 2 and 3 off after their first sentence.

## 3. Strengths

1. **The opening and its callback.** The starter "What does someone have to do to earn real respect there? Is it the same everywhere?" leads straight into the circle task ("how Igbo society measures a man's worth") and the slide 13 question ("does any of this remind you of what you wrote?"). That is a real thematic through-line, built on what Okonkwo's world actually values.
2. **Honest handling of copyright.** The plan says outright that the novel is in copyright, labels the paste slot with the passage's first words, and labels the missing pictures on slides 5 and 6 ("Yawp! could not find one it could use; add your own"). This is R10 done right.
3. **Exit-ticket design.** There are three options matching Brian's set, each with mainPoints, mustMention and watchFor ("Students who say only 'he wanted to share his culture' without the idea of writing back"). The slide 14 note tells the teacher how to use the results to open Day 2.

## 4. Problems (most serious first)

1. **Achebe's name sits beside Conrad's face, and Achebe himself is never shown.** *Where:* slide 4 (Conrad portrait next to "In 1958, Chinua Achebe published a novel…"); no slide shows Achebe. *Why it matters:* On a day about who Achebe was, students will leave believing the bearded Edwardian in the photo is Achebe. The writer the lesson is about never gets a face. *Breaks:* R4, R6, R11, R13.
2. **The planner knew about errors and left them in.** *Where:* step 3 ("Okonkwo… means 'man of the sky'", "Niger Delta region"), slide 9 ("1850s British traders arrive", "1901 Nigeria becomes a protectorate"), and slide 5 (Achebe misquote). The "Check before class" list flags four items but changes nothing. *Why it matters:* A teacher reading the vocabulary list aloud teaches a false meaning of the protagonist's name on Day 1. Students also get wrong geography and a misattributed quotation in the lesson whose purpose is correcting false stories about Africa. *Breaks:* R11; Brian's "names and quotations exactly right."
3. **The visuals do not show Igbo society before colonization.** *Where:* slide 11 (present-day family in modern attire, used for "laws, courts, religion, agriculture, trade, and art"); slide 10 (egwugwu, obi with no pictures); slide 8 (Nigeria shaded within Africa, one pin, Igboland not marked, no world → country → place zoom, Umuofia not placed). *Why it matters:* Half the request is "Igbo society before the colonizers," yet students never see a yam barn, an egwugwu mask, a compound, Igbo-Ukwu bronzes or an Mbari house. The deck asserts a complex society without showing one. *Breaks:* R1, R2, R6, R7, R8, R13.
4. **The vocabulary does not fit the reading.** *Where:* slide 10 notes say chi, egwugwu and obi "will come up in Chapter 1"; they do not (chi first appears in Chapters 3–4, obi in Chapter 2, egwugwu in Chapter 10). The words actually in Chapter 1 (Umuofia, harmattan, kola, cowries, ilo, title) and the lesson's own terms (protectorate, colonizer, clan) are never defined. *Why it matters:* Students hit unfamiliar words in the passage with no help, after spending vocabulary time on words they won't see for days. *Breaks:* R5; cognitive load; scaffolding for ELs.
5. **The printed and stacked versions do not match the plan.** *Where:* plan PDF (Goals blank, steps 2–3 truncated); stack (0-minute duplicate "Chapter 1 — Close Reading" row); deck-versus-plan minutes (8 vs 12, 17 vs 15, 4 vs 5); reply text promising a van Gogh painting the deck does not contain. *Why it matters:* A teacher or substitute working from paper loses the four teaching points and all the vocabulary. With no objective, nothing confirms the exit ticket measures what the lesson set out to teach. *Breaks:* Brian's "a deck's minutes must add up"; clear objective and alignment; Yawp!'s "ready to print and use."

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **Achebe quotation (slide 5, step 2):** "Achebe called it 'a thoroughgoing racist book' in his 1977 essay." Achebe's sentence is about Conrad the man, not the book: "Conrad was a thoroughgoing racist." That wording is also from the revised version (*Hopes and Impediments*, 1988). The 1975 lecture and the 1977 *Massachusetts Review* text said "a bloody racist." (High confidence on "book" being wrong; fairly high on the wording history.)
- **"Okonkwo … means 'man of the sky'"** (step 3): false. It is a name tied to Nkwo market day, as the planner's own flag says. (High confidence.)
- **"in the Niger Delta region"** (step 3): Igboland lies mainly east of the Niger, inland and north of the Delta. (High confidence.)
- **"1901 · Nigeria becomes a protectorate"** (slide 9, step 3): the Protectorates of Southern and Northern Nigeria were proclaimed on 1 January 1900, and "Nigeria" as a single colony dates from 1914. (High confidence.)
- **"1850s · British traders arrive"** (slide 9): British traders were on the lower Niger and the coast well before then. The 1850s does mark the Niger expeditions and the 1857 Onitsha mission and trading post, so the date is loose rather than flatly wrong. (Medium confidence.)
- **Timeline framing:** "The novel is set at the end of this timeline", but the last item on the timeline is 1958 (publication), not the 1890s setting. Muddled rather than false.
- **Paste range:** "pages 1–3 … through the description of his compound and his wrestling fame." The wrestling is in Chapter 1's opening; the compound and obi are described in Chapter 2. (About 80% confident.)
- **Obi definition** ("main living quarters at the center of a man's compound"): in the novel, Okonkwo's obi stands "immediately behind the only gate", at the entrance rather than the center. This is minor.
- **Framing (not an error but an omission):** the plan presents the 1977 Conrad essay as "the key to *Things Fall Apart*'s purpose" (1958). Achebe himself more often named Joyce Cary's *Mister Johnson*, read at university, as the provocation, and it is never mentioned.
- **Correct:** the Yeats line ("Things fall apart; the centre cannot hold"), Ogidi as Achebe's hometown, Unoka as Okonkwo's father, the opening sentence of Chapter 1, and 1958.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Correct flagged facts in place; do not just list them.** When the fact-check flags an item, rewrite the plan text and slide (or delete the item) before delivery. Keep the "Check before class" list only for genuinely uncertain items. Verify every attributed quotation, including quotations of critics and authors, against the source. *(R11; Brian: names and quotations exactly right.)*
2. **Image–caption integrity rule.** A portrait may appear only on a slide whose visible text names that person, with the name in readable type. Context days about an author must include that author's portrait. Reject images whose date or era contradicts the slide's claim (a modern photo for "pre-colonial"), and stop the reply from describing images that are not in the deck. *(R4, R6, R11, R13.)*
3. **Context-day image targets from the subject's world.** For "society before X" slides, search for specific artifacts and period photographs: for Igbo, egwugwu masks, a yam barn, an obi and compound, Igbo-Ukwu bronzes, Mbari houses, G. I. Jones photographs. Give every vocabulary term that names a thing a picture of it. *(R1, R6, R7, R13.)*
4. **Map and timeline requirements.** Maps should zoom world → country → region, shade the cultural region (Igboland), not just the modern state, and plot every named place or else drop it. Timelines should include the book's setting as a marked point and its publication as a separate one. *(R2, R3, R8.)*
5. **Tie vocabulary to the assigned passage, and keep exports in sync.** Pull terms from the exact pages students will read, plus the lesson's own concept words (protectorate, colonizer). Check that the deck's section minutes equal the plan's. Never truncate steps or leave Goals empty in the plan PDF; write a one-sentence objective the exit ticket measures. Do not add a 0-minute duplicate stack row for a handout. *(R5; clear objective and alignment; Brian: minutes must add up; ready to print.)*
