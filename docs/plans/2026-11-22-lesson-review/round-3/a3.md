# a3: The Odyssey Book 9, the Cyclops: hero or reckless show-off? (9th grade)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Teach a 50-minute lesson for 9th grade on Book 9 of The Odyssey, the Cyclops: is Odysseus a hero here or a reckless show-off? Include the slide deck."

**What came back:** One turn (132 s). A six-part plan adding to 50 minutes: class starter (5, three options: "Cleverness can save you in ways courage can't", a risk-for-credit memory prompt, "What courage actually is"), context (7: flashback at the Phaeacian court, Odysseus's epithet, Polyphemus son of Poseidon, the word *hubris*), close reading of two passages with a think-aloud and a hands-up poll (15), whole-class discussion anchored by a one-sentence written claim (12), Short Response "Hero or show-off?" with three sentence starters (8), closing with one exit ticket (3). Deck: 14 slides, 51 minutes (the deck card itself warns "51 min for a 50-minute period"); three images (marble head of Odysseus, slide 4; a Greek vase of the blinding of Polyphemus, slides 6 and 10, the same image twice; bust of Homer, slide 7). Handout PDF: 6 pages (Yawp! sheets for starter, Short Response and exit ticket; a close-reading sheet; then a near-blank page 5 with a stray bullet and the sentence starters alone on page 6). Stack after "Add everything": 50 of 50 minutes, "26% of class is writing · 13 min", exit ticket and the handout as 0-minute rows at the end.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** Opens in students' experience ("did something risky just to get the credit"), a short context block, reading, then a claim-first discussion and individual writing; the closing returns to *hubris*. The starter answers are only gestured at ("Hold that thought") and never set beside what Odysseus does.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** Passage 1 (the escape) is a three-sentence summary, not text, with an instruction to read "your text" at line numbers that do not match Fagles. Passage 2 splices two separate moments of Fagles' Book 9 into one block without ellipsis, changes wording, cuts the curse before its key clause, labels Fagles "in the public domain", and leaves out the crew's pleas and the "Nobody" trick, the two pieces of text this question most needs.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 3.** Context is accurate and brisk (flashback, narrator's own version, epithet, son of Poseidon), *hubris* is defined on slide 5, and the "Odysseus is telling this story himself. What might he leave out?" frame (slide 7) is a strong idea. Missing: the "Nobody" ruse, without which the name-shout loses its meaning; no gloss for "vaunted", "Laertes' son", "raider of cities".
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R2-R7, R13): 2.** The images are of the thing itself and well chosen (R6), but the vase is used twice (slides 6, 10), and slide 6's note still says "Yawp! could not find one it could use" under the picture; the two reading slides (8, 9) show neither text nor picture (R7, R9); no map of the voyage or of where the Cyclops episode sits.
- **E. Writing: 3.** The Short Response is precisely aligned to the question and asks for a claim with a specific moment; the anchor sentence before discussion is good practice. But the stack counts only 26% writing, the deck cuts the Short Response to 6 minutes against the plan's 8, the handout has no writing room for the annotation sentence or the Short Response, and there is one exit ticket, not three.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** Hands-up poll after reading, written claim before discussion, teacher moves ("What line made you think that?"), an exit ticket with a clear watch-for (summary without a claim). No check that students understood what happened in the escape before judging it.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** Plan and stack add to 50, but the deck is 51 and its sections differ from the plan (close reading 13 vs 15, discussion 14 vs 12, Short Response 6 vs 8); the handout's verse prints as run-on prose, page 5 is almost blank, the sentence starters fall alone onto page 6, and Passage 1 assumes every student has a Fagles copy at the right lines.
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** Three starters, excellent discussion moves, useful speaker notes, honest framing that both readings are defensible ("Receive every position warmly"). Undercut by contradictory copyright statements (opening: "public domain, so the key passage goes directly on the excerpt slide"; handout: "This translation is in the public domain" over a Fagles credit; closing note: "Fagles ... is still in copyright ... fair use") and by an excerpt slide promised but not built.
- **I. Access: 2.** Sentence starters for the Short Response and discussion stems. No glossary, no chunked or modern-language support for the epic diction, and the one printed passage is crammed into italic run-on lines that struggling readers and ELs will find hardest to annotate.
- **Overall: 2.** The question, discussion design and writing prompt are strong and a teacher would want them, but the text at the centre is not reliable: one passage is a summary, the other is a spliced, mis-credited quotation that omits the evidence the debate turns on.

## 3. Strengths

1. The claim-first discussion is well built: two minutes of silent writing ("Odysseus is / is not a hero in this episode because ___"), then teacher moves that force evidence ("What line in the text made you think that?", "Is there a difference between what he does and why he does it?").
2. The narrator frame on slide 7 ("Odysseus is telling this story himself. What might he leave out?") with the Homer bust is a genuinely sophisticated, age-appropriate lens for 9th grade.
3. Pictures are of the subject's world, not mood art (R6): a classical head of Odysseus and a Greek vase painting of the blinding of Polyphemus.

## 4. Problems

1. **The quoted passage is spliced and mis-credited.** Where: handout p.4 Passage 2. The name-shout ("Cyclops — if any man ... makes his home in Ithaca!") is joined directly to Polyphemus's prayer to Poseidon, which in the poem comes only after Polyphemus recalls the prophecy, begs Odysseus to return, and Odysseus taunts him a second time; there is no ellipsis, "groaned back in answer" becomes "in agony", a bridging clause is supplied, and the curse stops at "never reaches home", cutting "all shipmates lost". It is labelled Fagles and "in the public domain". Why: students annotate words that are not Homer's in that order, and the cut removes the direct cost to the crew that the lesson's question depends on. Principle: R11, R10 (should have been a labelled slot).
2. **The key evidence is absent.** Where: plan step 3, handout, slides 8-9. Neither the "Nobody" trick nor the crew's pleas to stop taunting the Cyclops appear anywhere. Why: "show-off" versus "hero" turns on Odysseus throwing away the anonymity that saved them, against his men's begging; without these, students argue from the summary. Principle: close reading, discussion requires evidence, R9.
3. **Passage 1 is not a reading.** Where: handout p.4 "Passage 1: The Escape Plan" (a summary) and slide 8 ("Follow along on your handout"). Students are told to mark leadership "as you read this section in your text", at "approx. lines 375–414" of Fagles, which is not the escape. Why: there is nothing to follow along with on the handout, and students with other translations (or none) cannot find it. Principle: R9, R10.
4. **Materials not ready to print.** Where: handout p.4 verse collapsed into run-on prose; p.5 near-blank; p.6 sentence starters orphaned; no lines for the annotation sentence or the Short Response ("write on the back"). Why: the one passage students annotate is the hardest thing on the page to read. Principle: Yawp! ready to print, room to write; cognitive load.
5. **Deck and plan do not agree, and writing is thin.** Where: deck 51 minutes with section times different from the plan; Short Response 6 minutes on slide 13 vs 8 in the plan; one exit ticket. Stack shows 26% writing. Why: a teacher pacing from the deck shortchanges the writing that measures the objective. Principle: Brian (deck minutes add up; three exit-ticket options), Yawp! students write a lot.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- Passage 2 (handout p.4): two non-adjacent passages joined without ellipsis; "groaned back in agony" (Fagles: "groaned back in answer"); bridging line "and reached his hands out to the heavens and prayed to lord Poseidon" is not Fagles' wording as I recall it (Fagles has him pray "stretching his hands to the starry skies"); the curse is cut after "never reaches home". High confidence on the splice and the cut; medium confidence on the exact bridging wording.
- "This translation is in the public domain" over a Robert Fagles credit (handout p.4): false; Fagles (Penguin, 1996) is in copyright, as the planner's own closing note says. High confidence.
- Line references "approx. lines 375–414" (escape) and "474–505" (name-shout) for Fagles Book 9: these do not match; in Fagles the name-shout and curse fall around lines 560-590. Medium confidence on exact numbers, high confidence they are wrong.
- Slide 6 note "Yawp! could not find one it could use" while a picture of Polyphemus is on the slide: internal contradiction. High confidence.
- "In Greek epic, [hubris] always costs something" (slide 5): an overgeneralisation, but harmless at this level.
- Context facts (Phaeacian court flashback, son of Poseidon, King of Ithaca, epithet) are correct. The planner's checks list 5 quotations "unverified", 0 verified.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. When a quotation cannot be verified against a source text (here 5 of 5 unverified), do not print it as a continuous quotation; print a labelled slot ("paste lines __ from your edition") or a public-domain translation (Butler, Palmer) clearly credited. Never label a copyrighted translation public domain. (R10, R11)
2. Require an ellipsis or a line-break marker wherever printed passages skip lines, and check that quoted speeches are contiguous in the source. (R11)
3. For "judge the character" lessons, have the planner list the decisive textual moments first (here: "Nobody", the crew's pleas, the boulder, the full curse) and require that each be in the student materials. (R9, discussion requires evidence)
4. Handout renderer: keep verse line breaks inside quoted passages, avoid near-blank pages and orphaned lists, and give every writing task real ruled lines. (Yawp! ready to print)
5. Deck builder: fit slide minutes to the plan's step minutes exactly (the "Fit the deck to 50 minutes" fix should be automatic), never reuse an image on two slides, and always offer three exit-ticket options. (Brian; R6)
