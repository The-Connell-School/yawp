## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** The lesson opens on the theme in students' own lives: "We don't choose what we want — we only choose whether to chase it" (slide 3). Direct teaching is short (slides 4–6). The closing question, "do you think Macbeth already wanted the crown?" (slide 12), comes back to the starter. However, students' starter answers are never explicitly set beside the text's treatment, and the stack's default starter ("captain of my destiny") is a weaker lead-in than the one on the slide.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** The aside is quoted accurately on slide 8 and handout p.4. But the 8-minute read-aloud of 1.3.38–78 is supplied nowhere: slide 7 has no text and no edition or page reference. Lines 79–129 are skipped entirely; these include Ross naming Macbeth Thane of Cawdor and Banquo's "instruments of darkness" warning. The excerpt also stops mid-sentence at "fantastical...", and the second aside ("If chance will have me king...") is missing.
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 2.** The context is brief and defines three terms (slide 6). It has errors and gaps, though. It calls the audience "Elizabethan" when the play is Jacobean (c.1606). It glosses a thane as "roughly a duke". It never mentions James I or Banquo as his ancestor, which is the context this exact scene turns on ("Thou shalt get kings"). The aside's hard words are left unglossed: soliciting, earnest, seated, use of nature, fantastical, imperial theme.
- **D. Visuals and dual coding (R2, R3, R4, R6, R7, R13): 2.** There is one real picture (slide 9, credited) and no map, no timeline, and no faces or names for the characters (R4: Banquo, Ross, Angus and Duncan are never introduced). The empty slots are labeled honestly (R10 done right). Content slides 5 and 6 are words only.
- **E. Writing: 2.** At 36% the amount is good, and the exit tickets are well aligned to the objective. However, the Yawp! assignment attached to the Short Response step is the generic library prompt "Ambition costs something... from the text, from the news, or from your own life" (plan step 6, stack row 6, handout p.2). That is not the objective-aligned prompt on slide 11, so the writing students actually receive does not measure the objective. The starter on slide 3 (FW-012) is also not the one the stack and handout p.1 use (FW-001).
- **F. Checking for understanding: 2.** The pair-share in step 5 and the exit ticket are the only checks. Nobody checks that students understood the three prophecies or what happened between lines 78 and 127 before they analyse the aside.
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The plan and deck both total 50, but the agenda slide renders its first item as "7 minutes", so the agenda shows 45. Three minutes for spoken closing responses plus a written exit ticket is not realistic. Handout p.4 says "answer in full sentences" but leaves no lines to write on. The stack says the handouts are "5 pages" when the PDF has 4. The read-aloud has no text to read from.
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** It offers real options: two read-aloud modes, three starters, homework as a fallback, and three exit tickets. The speaker notes are specific and expert (slide 8: "Give students 90 seconds to annotate silently... Shakespeare never names it"). But the "Check before class" box tells the teacher the correct 38–78 range "must extend", which is wrong, and the stack places the close-reading handout at minute 50 as a 0-min "Read aloud" when it is needed at minute 12.
- **I. Access: 2.** Sentence starters (handout p.4) and three vocabulary terms are the only scaffolds. The passage has no glosses, no line numbers and no modern paraphrase. It is printed in small grey type, and lines 38–78 are not printed at all.
- **Overall: 2.** The core is good: the model sentence, three sharp pair questions, and the aside-vs-soliloquy exit ticket. But a strong teacher could not run it tomorrow. The written assignment students get is off-objective, the reading has no text, the scene's middle is missing, and the context leaves out James I and Banquo.

## 3. Strengths

1. The modeling step is genuine I-do/we-do/you-do. A think-aloud on the aside produces a model claim on the board ("the thought of murder arrives before anyone suggests it"), and students are then told to write "a sentence like this one" backed by a line (step 4, slide 9 notes).
2. The pair questions are sharp and text-dependent, especially "Banquo is standing right there. What does it mean that Macbeth says none of this to him?" The exit ticket's watch-for (aside vs. soliloquy; "the witches made him") shows real subject knowledge.
3. The quotations are accurate and the image gaps are honest. The aside is quoted correctly line for line, and missing pictures get labeled slots rather than quiet substitutes (R10, R11).

## 4. Problems

1. **The writing students actually do does not match the objective** (plan step 6, stack row "What ambition costs", handout p.2). The plan's prompt asks what the aside reveals, citing lines 130–142. The attached Yawp! assignment is the generic "Ambition costs something... from the text, the news, or your own life". Clicking "Add everything" therefore assigns 10 minutes of writing that need never touch Macbeth. In the same way, slide 3's starter differs from the stack and handout starter. *Breaks:* objective alignment; Yawp! values (the starter and short response are the Yawp! assignments); Brian's "the deck must be the lesson".
2. **No reading slide has the reading, and half the scene is missing** (step 3, slide 7, handout p.4). The 8-minute read-aloud of 1.3.38–78 has no text on a slide or handout and no edition or page reference. The lesson then jumps to line 127, skipping Ross's news that Macbeth is Thane of Cawdor. That news is the "truth" the aside reacts to ("Commencing in a truth? I am Thane of Cawdor"). It also skips Banquo's warning about "instruments of darkness" telling "truths" to "betray's", which is the scene's built-in counter-reading. Students cannot understand why Macbeth reacts as he does without these lines. *Breaks:* R9, close reading, R1.
3. **The context is inaccurate and misses the point of the scene** (step 2, slides 5–6, slide 9 "wants"). It says the audience is "Elizabethan" when the play is Jacobean (c.1606, written under James I). It calls a thane "roughly a duke" (closer to a baron; below an earl). It never mentions that King James claimed descent from Banquo, though that is exactly why the witches' "Thou shalt get kings" matters and why Banquo is drawn as the honest foil in this scene. *Breaks:* R1, R11, R8.
4. **No one is introduced and nothing is shown** (slides 4–7). Banquo, Ross, Angus and Duncan appear in the questions without being introduced (R4). There is no map of Scotland, only an empty slot (R2). There is no timeline placing the historical Macbeth, Shakespeare and James I (R3). The only picture is a theatre sketch on a modeling slide (R6, R7, R13). Twelfth graders reading 1.3 need to know who is on stage.
5. **There is no access to the language, and no room to write** (slide 6, handout p.4). Only thane, hereafter and aside are defined. The words that block meaning in the aside (soliciting, earnest of success, seated heart, use of nature, fantastical, the imperial theme) are not glossed. The handout passage has no line numbers, which the questions rely on. The three "answer in full sentences" questions have no writing lines. The excerpt is cut at "fantastical...", which drops the payoff "Shakes so my single state of man...". *Breaks:* R5; scaffolding and access; materials ready to use.

Also worth noting: the model sentence and the exit ticket's watch-for ("the aside is evidence against that reading") settle the debate that slide 4 says "the play refuses to settle". Students will then parrot the model instead of arguing either way, which falls short of Brian's "framing choices".

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **Quotations:** none wrong. "This supernatural soliciting... My thought, whose murder yet is but fantastical", "Two truths are told...", and "shalt be king hereafter" are all accurate. High confidence.
- **Line labels:** the handout's "lines 130–142" actually begins at "Two truths are told" (about 127) and ends at about 139 with an ellipsis mid-sentence. In standard numbering, 130–142 runs to "...nothing is / But what is not". The range is mislabeled and the sentence is cut off. High confidence (numbering varies by edition, but the excerpt does not match its own label in any edition).
- **"Elizabethan audiences"** (step 2) and "Elizabethan theatre" (slide 9): Macbeth is Jacobean, c.1606. High confidence.
- **"Thane: roughly equivalent to a duke or earl"** (slide 6): this overstates the rank. A thane was a lesser royal official or lord, below an earl (mormaer), and closer to a baron. Fairly confident.
- **The planner's own "Check before class"** is wrong where it says the 38–78 read-aloud "must extend to include" the aside. That range correctly covers Macbeth's entrance through the witches vanishing; the aside is a separate step. It is also wrong that the scene runs "through about line 156", since it is longer in most editions. Moderately confident on the scene length; high confidence that the flag misleads.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Bind the Yawp! assignment to the step's own prompt** (E, objective alignment). When a step has a text-specific prompt, generate a new short-response assignment from that prompt rather than attaching the nearest library item. Make the slide's starter the same item the stack adds by default.
2. **Supply or locate every passage a step reads** (R9, R10). A read-aloud step must put the lines on a slide or handout, or give the exact act, scene, lines and edition. Check that the lesson's passages cover the plot links the analysis depends on (here, Ross's news and Banquo's warning), or add a one-slide bridge summary.
3. **Add a "who's on stage" slide and a gloss pass for Shakespeare and verse** (R4, R5, access). Introduce named characters with a face or portrait before they are discussed. Automatically gloss archaic or hard words in any excerpt beside the line, and print excerpts with line numbers. Every question on a handout should get writing lines.
4. **Add a context fact check for era and patron** (R1, R11, R8). For Shakespeare, check Elizabethan vs. Jacobean dating, and surface the context the scene itself depends on (James I/Banquo for Macbeth 1.3). Do not raise self-flags that contradict correct plan content.
5. **Keep the central question open** (Brian's framing choices; discussion with evidence). When a plan names an "unsettled" question, the model sentence and the exit-ticket watch-for should model a claim on one side and acknowledge the other, not mark one reading as wrong.
