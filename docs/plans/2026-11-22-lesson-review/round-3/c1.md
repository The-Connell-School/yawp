## 3. Strengths

1. **The facts are accurate and well chosen.** The 1692 timeline (slide 5) correctly gives Jan/Feb 1692, May (Court of Oyer and Terminer), June–September executions, 19 hanged, Corey pressed, 200+ accused, and the October dissolution. The 1953 timeline (slide 8) correctly gives the Jan 22, 1953 opening and McCarthy's 1954 censure. Notes on Miller's 1956 HUAC appearance are accurate, and the slide notes stress that "Miller was writing into the crisis, not looking back at it." That is a sharp teaching point.
2. **The Short Response prompt is excellent** (step 6, slide 12). "What does one story let him say about the other that a direct attack could not?" pushes students past listing parallels to the purpose of allegory. The circulating prompt in the notes ("Now bring in 1953...") is real coaching.
3. **The structural insight comes back across slides.** Slide 6 introduces "confess or accuse," the big idea on slide 7 restates it, and slide 11 returns to it. One idea, revisited, in good retrieval style.

## 4. Problems (most serious first)

1. **The history is never connected to the play's people on screen (R8, R4, R1).** Betty Parris, Abigail Williams and Tituba appear on the 1692 timeline (slide 5) but are never identified as characters students will meet tomorrow. John Proctor, Reverend Parris, the Putnams, Judge Danforth and Reverend Hale are absent from every slide; Proctor appears only once, in slide 9's speaker notes. The deck ends with "Next time: Act One — the Parris household," and students do not know who Parris is. A context day exists to equip students to read this book, and this one teaches the history in isolation.
2. **There are almost no pictures of the subject (R6, R7, R13, R4).** There is no image from 1692: no trial illustration, no meetinghouse, no period woodcut. Slides 4 and 7 carry only text plus a "Picture to add" note. McCarthy has no face (slide 9). The single Miller photo (a 1966 Anefo picture, used for "Miller in the 1950s") repeats on slides 9 and 11. The picture slots are flagged only in speaker notes, not on the slide (R10).
3. **The deck minutes do not match the lesson (Brian: minutes must add up; feasibility).** The deck is labeled "13 slides · 51 min." Slide 11 alone claims 19 minutes for a 10-minute step, and the agenda slide (slide 2) merges starter and KWL into "Class starter, salem · 8 minutes" and lists "Context" twice. A teacher pacing from the deck will overrun.
4. **Key terms go undefined (R5; cognitive load).** Slide 4 uses "theocracy." Slides 8 and 9 use HUAC, blacklist, Communism, "name names" and contempt of Congress. To an 11th grader in 2026, McCarthyism is as foreign as Salem. Only "allegory" gets a define slide, so students are asked to compare two worlds while understanding the vocabulary of only one.
5. **The starter does not open the day's theme (R12.1), and there is no single exit-ticket choice (Brian).** The three starters are generic library prompts about belief, lies and identity. None puts students inside the experience the play is about: being blamed, accusing to protect yourself, or a group turning on someone. Only one exit-ticket type is offered, against Brian's three.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- **"He called it a 'perfect fit'"** (step 4). I don't know of this as a Miller quotation. In "Why I Wrote The Crucible" (The New Yorker, 1996) and his autobiography *Timebends*, Miller describes the parallel in other words. Treat this as unverified and possibly invented (medium confidence it is not his phrase).
- **"HUAC begins — 1947"** (slide 8). HUAC was created in 1938 and made permanent in 1945. In 1947 it *began its Hollywood hearings*, which the detail line says correctly, but the label is wrong.
- **The garbled check note** ("This is not an error; Corey was pressed with stones"). The underlying fact is correct; the note is a leak of the checker's output and only confuses the teacher.
- **Arthur Miller photo.** The credit reads "Eric Koch for Anefo, Arthur Miller 1966, CC0," so this is Miller in 1966, not the 1950s as the plan wants. That is minor, but it is labeled as a 1950s image in intent.
- Otherwise none found. The Salem dates and counts, Betty Parris (9) and Abigail Williams (11), the January 22, 1953 premiere, the 1950 list of 205, the December 1954 censure, and Miller's 1956 HUAC appearance and contempt citation are all correct (high confidence).

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **On a context day for a literary text, require a "who you will meet" slide.** Each historical figure who is also a character (Parris, Abigail, Tituba, Proctor, Putnam, Danforth, Hale) should be named on screen beside their real-world counterpart, with a face where one exists (R8, R4, R1).
2. **Make the deck's minutes come from the plan's step minutes.** Validate that per-section slide minutes equal step minutes and that the total equals the period length. Build agenda titles from the step titles, not concatenated types (Brian: minutes add up; G).
3. **Run a vocabulary pass on every content slide.** Any term above grade level (theocracy, HUAC, blacklist) gets a define slide or an on-slide gloss at or before first use (R5).
4. **Escalate image sourcing for historical subjects.** Search public-domain archives (Library of Congress, Wikimedia Commons) for period images before giving up. Never reuse the same image on two slides. Put unfilled slots on the slide face as a labeled placeholder, not only in the notes (R6, R10, R13).
5. **Choose the class starter by theme fit, not library tag.** Generate or select a starter that asks about the unit's central human experience (here: blame, fear, accusing to survive). Always offer three exit-ticket types (R12.1; Brian).
