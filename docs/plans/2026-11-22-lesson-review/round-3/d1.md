# d1: Language and Power in 1984 (unit plan, 12th grade)

## 1. The request and what came back

**Request:** "Build a unit plan for 1984 for 12th grade: four weeks, 50-minute periods, with a focus on language and power, ending in a research-backed essay."

**What came back:** A unit map titled "Language and Power in 1984" (English 12, "Four weeks, 50-minute periods") with **24** day cards of 50 minutes each (objective, reading, in-class activity, check, "Build this day" button). Days 1-11 cover context and the novel (Part One in Days 2-6, Part Two in Days 7-8, Part Three in Days 9-10, quiz Day 11); Days 12-24 are essay work (pre-writing, thesis, two research days, body paragraphs, intro, one drafting day, peer review, three revision/grammar days, submission, presentations). Above the map: three "things to check" questions, five class-starter cards (one per thematic cluster), a care note for the Room 101 chapters, and a "Check before class" block with three automated chapter flags. Printable unit plan PDF: 5 pages. No deck or handout (expected for a unit plan). No "Add everything" button, so the stack is empty (0 of 50 minutes, 0% writing); that is consistent with a unit plan, where days are built one at a time. Checks object: factsCorrected 1, factsFlagged 3, no quotations checked.

## 2. Scores

- **A. Lesson arc (R12): 3.** The unit opens on the theme in students' own experience (Day 1 starter "when has a word or a phrase shaped what you were allowed to think or do?"), recurring thematic starters per cluster, and Day 12 sends students back to mine their own starters and Short Responses for the essay, which is exactly the R12 tie-back. Missing: any brief check of what students already know about Orwell/totalitarianism on Day 1.
- **B. The text at the center (R9, R11): 2.** The right passages are named (opening telescreen/posters, doublethink, Syme on Newspeak in Part One ch. 5, Goldstein's book, the O'Brien interrogation, "He loved Big Brother"), but none is quoted or located by page, and the reading schedule is broken: Day 3 assigns Part One ch. 3-7, Day 4 then assigns ch. 4-6 (already read), Day 5 ch. 5 again, and ch. 8 is never assigned. No companion nonfiction (Orwell's "Politics and the English Language" is the obvious one for this focus).
- **C. Direct instruction (R5, R11): 2.** Key terms are named (doublethink, Newspeak, arguable claim, credible source) but nothing in the unit defines them or plans when vocabulary is taught; mini-lessons are pointers to other Yawp! decks with inconsistent slide ranges (Thesis Statements "slides 14-33" on Day 4, "slides 14-43" on Day 13).
- **D. Visuals and dual coding: null (no deck).** Day 1 does call for a timeline of 20th-century totalitarianism, a map of Oceania/Airstrip One, and Orwell's life, which is the right R1-R4 instinct, but nothing is built.
- **E. Writing: 3.** Writing runs through the whole unit: daily starters, two graded Short Responses (Days 6, 11), one-sentence arguments that seed the thesis (Day 10), and a staged essay. But the "research-backed" essay gets one day of finding sources, one of integrating, and a single full drafting day, which is thin for a senior research essay.
- **F. Checking for understanding: 3.** Every day has a concrete check (exit tickets, cold-call share-out, whiteboard claims, two reading quizzes). Several checks are not tied to the day's objective (Day 2 "quick share: one place they marked").
- **G. Feasibility: 2.** The request was four weeks (about 20 periods); the unit is 24 days, nearly five weeks, and the header still says "Four weeks". Day 24 (two-minute speech plus a question for every student) cannot fit 50 minutes for a class of 25-30. Part Two (10 chapters including Goldstein's book) is assigned across two nights.
- **H. Teacher usability: 3.** Offers genuine options (essay scope, "Day 24 ... can easily become a Socratic seminar or a written reflection"), a thoughtful care note for Room 101, and per-day "Build this day" buttons. Undercut by the "Check before class" block, which shows the teacher automated flags, one of them wrong (see section 5), and ends on three questions rather than one closing question.
- **I. Access: 1.** No reading support at all: no chunking, no audio/reading-guide option, no vocabulary plan for Newspeak terms, no sentence frames except the quotation templates on Day 15, no differentiation for English learners, and the reading pace is heavy.
- **Overall: 2.** A strong skeleton a teacher could borrow ideas from, but the wrong length, a broken reading schedule, and a thin research arc mean a teacher must rebuild it before using it.

## 3. Strengths

1. The thematic class starters are excellent and well sequenced to the novel ("If you knew every conversation you had was being recorded and reviewed, what would you change about the way you talk?" before the telescreen chapters; "Two things at once" before doublethink), and Day 12 explicitly turns them into essay material.
2. Day 8 uses Goldstein's book as a source-evaluation bridge ("who wrote this, and how do we know?") into the research days, a smart, unit-specific link between literature and research skill.
3. Day 9/10 build nuance into the essay ("does the Party need Room 101, or is Newspeak enough?"), and the care note for the torture chapters is humane and practical.

## 4. Problems

1. **The unit is 24 days, not four weeks.** Where: unit header ("Four weeks") vs. 24 day cards. Why it matters: a teacher who scheduled four weeks runs out of time during revision; the plan does not even flag the overrun. Breaks: Brian's "minutes must add up"; Feasibility.
2. **The reading schedule is incoherent.** Where: Days 3-6 (ch. 3-7, then 4-6, then 5, then "Part One complete"; ch. 8 never assigned), Day 9 assigns Part Three ch. 1-4 for a "Room 101" lesson although Room 101 is ch. 5. Why: students cannot follow a homework calendar that repeats and skips chapters; close-reading days do not line up with what they have read. Breaks: R11 (accuracy), clear alignment.
3. **The automated "Check before class" flag is itself wrong.** Where: "Part One has only six chapters, so there is no chapter 7." Part One of *Nineteen Eighty-Four* has eight chapters. The third flag ("chapter 5 would already be assigned") is confusing. Why: the teacher is told a correct assignment is an error, and sees internal checker notes as if they were advice. Breaks: R11; Brian's "expert, kind, on the teacher's side".
4. **The research essay is under-built and the language focus lacks nonfiction.** Where: Days 14-15 (two research days), Day 18 (one drafting day); no "Politics and the English Language", no real propaganda or political speech texts, though the prompt invites "real-world language". Why: seniors are asked for a research-backed argument with almost no instruction in research or reading secondary sources. Breaks: alignment of task to objective; scaffolding.
5. **No access plan.** Where: whole unit. Why: a 300-page novel in eleven days with Part Two in two nights and no reading supports will lose struggling readers and English learners; Newspeak vocabulary (thoughtcrime, unperson, ownlife) is never planned for. Breaks: R5; scaffolding and access.

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- "Part One has only six chapters, so there is no chapter 7" (Check before class): **wrong**; Part One has eight chapters (Part Two ten, Part Three six). High confidence.
- Day 9: reading "Part Three, chapters 1-4" for a lesson titled Room 101; Room 101 is Part Three ch. 5 (the planner's own flag notes this but the day was not fixed). High confidence.
- Day 4 vs. Day 3 reading overlap (ch. 4-6 after ch. 3-7) is a scheduling error rather than a fact error.
- Correct: Syme's Newspeak conversation is in Part One ch. 5; "He loved Big Brother" is the final line (Part Three ch. 6); the Appendix is "The Principles of Newspeak"; Goldstein's book is "The Theory and Practice of Oligarchical Collectivism". No quotations beyond the last line were offered. High confidence.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Enforce the requested length**: convert "N weeks" to N x 5 days and refuse to title a unit "Four weeks" when it has more days; if more are needed, say so and offer what to cut. (Feasibility; Brian: minutes add up)
2. **Give the planner a chapter map per book** (parts, chapters, key passages with page/chapter) and validate reading assignments for sequence: no repeats, no gaps, the day's close-reading passage inside what has been read. Feed that same map to the fact checker so it does not invent chapter counts, and show checker output to the teacher only as corrections it is confident of. (R11, R9)
3. **For "research-backed" essays, require a research arc** of at least four to five days (question, finding, reading/annotating a source, integrating, citing) plus one or two paired nonfiction texts tied to the unit focus. (Alignment; scaffolding)
4. **Add a per-unit access layer**: a vocabulary list with first-use days, a reading-pace check (pages per night), and one support per reading day (chunked passage, audio, guiding questions, sentence frames). (R5; access)
5. **Check per-student activities against the clock** (e.g., N students x 2 minutes) and convert impossible days into options. (Feasibility)
