## 3. Strengths

1. The framing of dialect is exactly right for 11th graders: slide 7 ("a different system ... not a mistake") and slide 11 setting a porch line beside its standard-English version, with the note "Standard English flattens it ... the sound of dignity, not deficiency."
2. The guiding question "What does the narrator know that the porch community doesn't?" (slide 9) and the Short Response ("Janie chooses to tell her story to Pheoby - not to the porch ... who Hurston thinks deserves to hear a woman's story?") get straight to the purpose of the frame.
3. Honest handling of copyright: a labelled paste slot with the exact start and end of the passage, rather than a quiet paraphrase (R10).

## 4. Problems

1. **The deck has almost no visuals, and the map shows the wrong place.** Where: slide 6 (Northeast map, Harlem pinned, "Eatonville - not on the map"); slides 4, 5, 7-10 words only; Hurston's face missing (slide 5). Why: students meeting Eatonville and Hurston for the first time see neither; the one map undermines R2's "show where". Breaks: R2, R4, R7, R13.
2. **Invented vocabulary word with a slur in its gloss.** Where: plan step 3 "Words to know": "muttamulatto - not in standard use; Hurston uses mulatto (a period term ... now considered offensive)". Also "sitters-and-talkers - Hurston's coinage". Why: a teacher told to "put these on the board" introduces a nonexistent word and an offensive term that is not in the passage, and the real hard words of the opening are untaught. Breaks: R5, R11; Brian "no rabbit holes".
3. **A fabricated quotation.** Where: plan step 4, Q2: "The porch community says Janie 'must be 'bout forty.'" The porch actually asks "What dat ole forty year ole 'oman doin' wid her hair swingin' down her back lak some young gal?" Why: students are sent to find words that are not there. Breaks: R11; Brian "quotations exactly right".
4. **Writing is squeezed.** Where: stack (30% writing; Short Response 7 min; exit ticket 0 min; Closing 3 min); handout Q3 "Write it here:" with no lines. Why: the graded writing, the main evidence of learning, gets less time than the context talk. Breaks: Yawp! values (students write a lot); feasibility.
5. **Materials not ready to print.** Where: handout p. 4 (passage slot, printed last), title truncated on p. 1, stack/PDF page-count mismatch, deck 51 min. Why: the teacher must source, paste and reorder before the copier. Breaks: R9; materials ready to use; Brian "minutes add up".

## 5. Factual or quotation errors

- "muttamulatto" (plan step 3): **not a word in the novel**; the gloss imports "mulatto", which is not in the opening pages. High confidence.
- "The porch community says Janie 'must be 'bout forty'" (plan step 4 Q2): **not in the text**; the line is "What dat ole forty year ole 'oman doin' wid her hair swingin' down her back lak some young gal?" High confidence. The planner's own flag proposes a replacement ("Pearl Stone ... 'she ain't cover forty'") that I do not recognise from the text either; medium-high confidence that it is also wrong.
- "Richard Wright called it folksy and apolitical" (plan step 2): a paraphrase presented as attribution; Wright's 1937 *New Masses* review said the novel "carries no theme, no message, no thought" and likened it to the minstrel tradition. High confidence.
- "sitters-and-talkers - Hurston's coinage": I do not believe this phrase is in the novel (Hurston writes of "These sitters"). Medium confidence.
- "Hurston transcribed the speech of the community ... not an approximation": overstated; her dialect is crafted literary representation grounded in fieldwork. Interpretive, medium confidence.
- Correct: the opening sentence ("Ships at a distance ... mocked to death by Time."), Hurston 1891-1960, Notasulga, Eatonville as an early incorporated all-Black town, written in about seven weeks in Haiti, Boas, Alice Walker's revival, the dialect line on slide 11 ("What she doin' coming back here in dem overhalls? Can't she find no dress to put on?"). Still in copyright (1937) is correct.

## 6. Fixes to the planner

1. **Map builder must contain every place it names**: zoom to fit all pins (world, then country, then Florida), and never ship a map whose legend says a place is "not on the map". (R2)
2. **Vocabulary must come from the passage**: words-to-know candidates must appear in the assigned text (checked against the source or the book's word list); drop any word the checker flags instead of leaving it in the plan with a note. (R5, R11)
3. **Quotations in plans for copyrighted books must be verified or paraphrased**: if the checker cannot match a source ("work": "none"), present quoted dialogue as paraphrase or remove the quotation marks; never show the teacher a "correction" the checker cannot verify. (R11)
4. **Always fetch a real author portrait** from public-domain sources (Library of Congress, Wikimedia) before falling back to a slot; for setting-driven lessons, search for period photographs of the place. (R4, R6, R7, R13)
5. **Protect writing time and print order**: graded Short Response at least 10-12 minutes, exit ticket given real minutes, writing lines for every handout question, the reading passage placed first in the packet, titles never truncated on print. (Yawp! values; R9; ready to use)
