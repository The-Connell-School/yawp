# The Yawp! lesson planner: every rule, in one place

*Nov 24. Every rule for the lesson planner that Kevin and Brian have set, or that came out of the reviews, gathered from where each was first written. Each rule says where it came from and how it is upheld:*

- ***instructions*** *means the planner is told it;*
- ***code*** *means Yawp! makes it true or checks it after the planner writes;*
- ***UI*** *means the page shows it.*

*A rule held only by the instructions is followed most of the time, not every time; the tests say which.*

## 1. The shape of a lesson

**R12 The arc of a lesson.** *(instructions)*

1. Open on a thematic topic, as a question about the students' own experience and views. Always do this on day 1 of a unit, and mostly in other lessons too. Mid-unit, a lesson may open by recalling earlier work.
2. At the start of a unit, briefly find out what students already know.
3. Teach directly what can be known: short, punchy pieces, with the key terms.
4. Bring in the text against what the students said, through questions. Give time to think and write alone, to discuss with peers, and for the teacher to teach the words the reading needs.

**R29 Tell what can be known; ask what can be interpreted.** Direct instruction is for vocabulary, terms, history and who is who, never for what the work means. *(instructions)*

**S3 Teach what was asked.** Every part of the request has a step that teaches it. A request to plan is never answered with only a question: when a detail is missing, build the lesson with a labeled slot and say what was assumed. *(instructions)*

**S4 Model first, then let them try.** Before students close-read or write, the teacher shows how on two lines or one sentence, thinking aloud: I do, we do, you do. *(instructions)*

**S6 Check understanding during the lesson.** After direct teaching and after the first read, one question that everyone answers at once, plus what to do if most of the class misses it. *(instructions)*

**S9 Minutes that agree.** The plan's steps add up to the period. The deck's agenda and slide minutes match the plan's steps. *(instructions + code: the deck's minutes are set from the plan)*

**B1 Offer options; never prescribe.** "If you like…"; framing choices for a step. *(instructions)*

**B2 No rabbit holes.** Every step serves the objective. *(instructions)*

## 2. Theme and thematic topic (literature)

**R26 Topic is not theme.** A thematic topic is a word or two with no claim (love, ambition, fate). A theme is what the work says, or appears to say, about it: an arguable sentence. One topic can carry opposite themes. *(instructions)*

**R27 Open on the topic, as a question.** "When does wanting more become dangerous?", never "Power corrupts. Agree or disagree?" *(instructions)*

**R28 Never tell the theme.** No slide, title, subtitle, handout, starter, prompt or exit ticket says what the work means. Themes live in the teacher's notes, as what to listen for. *(instructions)*

**R30 From their view to the text's.** Questions move students from what they think to what the text seems to say, in tentative words ("so far", "seems to"), always asking for the line that shows it. *(instructions)*

**R31 Students make the claim.** A writing task asks what the text seems to say about a topic. It never asks students to illustrate a claim they were handed. *(instructions)*

**R32 Found over time.** The opening topic question returns. Across a unit, students track each topic, then draft and revise a theme statement. Nonfiction gets the same treatment for the author's argument. *(instructions)*

## 3. The words of the text

**R9 No reading without the reading.** A passage students annotate is on the slide and printed on the handout, or the slide says exactly where to find it. *(instructions + code: excerpt slides print the source's lines)*

**S1 The text in students' hands.** Every close-reading step puts the words in front of students. A lesson that annotates includes the handout. *(instructions)*

**R10 Labeled slots, never quiet substitutes.** When something can't be supplied (a copyrighted passage, a film still), a labeled slot says so. *(instructions + code: picture slots)*

**R11 / S2 / B6 Exactly right.** Facts, dates, names and quotations must be exactly right, with act and scene, chapter or stanza named. Every picture is credited. *(instructions + code: quotations are checked against the source text, facts by a second model, and errors the lesson relies on go to a repair pass)*

**R24 Quote only a real text.** A work in copyright, including a modern translation of an old work, is never quoted from memory. Yawp! prints a public-domain translation and says which, or the teacher's own pages, or leaves a labeled slot and describes the moment with its page. *(instructions + code: the quotation check; the teacher's pages become the source)*

**R25 Tell the teacher what was checked.** Every lesson that quotes says in one line, at the end, what its quotations were checked against, or that they weren't and how to fix it. *(code)*

**Teacher's pages.** A PDF, a photo of the pages or pasted text becomes the lesson's text: quotations are checked against it and passages printed from it. *(code)*

## 4. What the slides show

**R1 Think like an English teacher.** A context day equips students to read this particular book: where, when, who, which words, and what it looked like. *(instructions)*

**R2 Where, on a map,** starting from what students know: the world, then the country, then the place. *(instructions + code: places found from Yawp!'s own list)*

**R20 The right places.** Ancient and literary places (Thebes, Troy, Elsinore), never an American town of the same name. Nothing the lesson names as its setting is left off the map. *(code)*

**R3 When, on a timeline,** never as a list of dates. *(instructions)*

**R21 Timelines with pictures.** Events carry pictures, visible on the whole line, and famous events get pictures of the event itself. *(code: the picture editor names each event; the line draws them all)*

**R4 Who.** Real people get their faces, and the author is on the title slide. Main characters are introduced by name before students meet them. *(instructions + code: author picture)*

**R5 Words.** A word a student of this grade may not know is defined on screen, at or before its first use. *(instructions)*

**R6 Pictures from the subject's world.** Not decorative metaphors, and no picture used twice. *(instructions + code: the picture checker; one picture per subject)*

**R7 / R13 Dual coding.** Every slide that teaches content pairs its words with something to look at: a picture of the thing itself, a map, a timeline, a diagram or a marked passage. Routine slides (write, talk, exit) may be words only. *(instructions + code: the picture editor and lookup)*

**R22 Show the world.** When a text's world is far from students, one slide shows it in two to four pictures side by side: the ruins, what it looked like, the people, an object. *(instructions + the "world" slide)*

**R23 Memorable, not only clear.** Most content slides carry a picture, a diagram of how the text is built, or the passage with its key phrases marked. Decoration doesn't count. *(instructions)*

**R8 Connect the context to the book.** Name the book's characters and places beside the history. *(instructions)*

## 5. Writing

**Y1 Students write a lot.** The stack shows the share of the class spent writing. *(UI)*

**Y2 The class starter and exit ticket are Yawp! writing assignments.** *(instructions + UI)*

**B3 Three exit-ticket options:** check understanding, explain a term, ask a question. *(instructions)*

**R31** (above) **Students make the claim.**

**S5 (part) Sentence starters.** Every writing task gets two or three. *(instructions)*

**Room to write.** One line of room for a sentence, five or six for a paragraph, never more than eight. *(instructions + code: runaway blanks are cut)*

## 6. Access and care

**S5 Words and access.** Hard words are glossed where students meet them. Verse and dialect get a line on how to read them. A struggling reader gets a way in (a shorter chunk, the audio, a partner read). *(instructions)*

**S7 Care with hard content.** When a lesson touches suicide or self-harm, sexual violence, slurs, genocide or abuse, the teacher gets a care note with how to frame it, a way to opt out and when to involve a counselor. *(instructions)*

**Pictures fit for school.** No graphic violence. Pictures of atrocity are always the teacher's choice. *(code: the picture checker)*

## 7. Units

**S8 A unit is built backward.** It starts from the final task and a short rubric. Every skill is taught before it's assessed. The reading pace is one a class can keep. Day 1 introduces the text. *(instructions)*

**R18 A unit at a glance.** Each day shows its title, minutes, activity icons and its reading on one line; the full text is one click away. *(UI)*

**R32** (above) **The topic tracker and theme statement across the unit.**

## 8. How the reply reads

**R14 Lesson first, caveats last.** The reply opens on the lesson or the unit map. Notes for the teacher come at the end, under "Check before class". *(code)*

**R15 No narration.** The planner never narrates its own process: what it has, what it searched, what it will write itself, or what it's about to do. *(code: those sentences are removed)*

**R16 No wall of text.** A unit reply has at most two sentences before the map, then the map, then the rest. *(code + UI)*

**R17 Notes that are real.** Nothing under "Check before class" says it isn't an error. *(code)*

**B7 Expert, kind, on the teacher's side.** *(instructions)*

**B8 One closing question.** A plan ends with one question to the teacher. *(instructions)*

**Y3 Materials ready to print and use.** Student-facing language and room to write. *(instructions + code: the PDFs)*

## 9. Practical

**R19 Not slow.** A lesson should arrive in under about 2½ minutes and a unit map in under about 2. *(measured)* About 80% of the wait is the planner writing. Streaming the lesson as it's written is the open option.

**No lesson lost.** If a deck was asked for and the reply lost it, the deck is written from the plan. The planner answers the moment a request arrives, and the browser retries once. *(code)*

**B4 A deck's minutes add up to the lesson.** See S9.
