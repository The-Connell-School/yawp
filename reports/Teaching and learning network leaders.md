# YAWP! outreach: teaching-and-learning network leaders

Research summary for Brian and Kevin · November 2026

## The short version

- These are the people school leaders actually listen to. Their audiences are small (hundreds to low thousands), but the readers are directors of teaching and learning, deans, department chairs and principals, the people who decide what tools teachers get.
- Start with the three anchors. Justin Cerenzia is the warm door through Kevin. The CTTL (Ian Kelleher and Glenn Whitman) is the toughest test of whether YAWP! keeps students thinking. Kim Marshall reaches more principals than anyone else here.
- Justin already knows the category. Episcopal uses Flint for AI feedback on student writing, so his honest read on what works is valuable before any introductions.
- One pattern kept coming up: these networks want examples led by practitioners. A teacher who uses YAWP! and will co-present or co-write opens the Marshall Memo, NCTE's leadership conference, SAIS and the regional cohorts.
- The independent-school associations are the cheapest rooms to be in. SAIS takes proposals from non-members, NCAIS says its monthly Zoom cohorts always have room for a co-facilitator, and the Association for Academic Leaders is the closest national match to Justin's group.
- This audience leans "pedagogy over platforms" (an actual NAIS 2026 session title). Lead with the prewriting builders. The Grading Assistant needs care.

> Muse can do the legwork. Several people here changed jobs in 2026, so titles and contact routes should be confirmed before outreach. Muse can do that, keep the list current, track who Brian has talked to, and draft first notes for Brian to edit and send.

I'm building YAWP!, a writing tool for middle and high school ELA teachers. Our prewriting builders are meant to make students do the planning themselves (the claim, the evidence, the reasoning), and I'm not sure we've gotten it right everywhere. Would you look at it against your framework and tell me where it falls short? I can set you up with an account or walk you through it in 20 minutes.

**Kim Marshall** (feedback page on marshallmemo.com)

> Mr. Marshall, your Fordham piece on AI in teacher evaluation stuck with me, especially readers asking for human judgment and to be told when AI is involved. I'm building YAWP!, a writing tool for middle and high school ELA teachers, and we're working through the same questions with AI feedback on student essays. How much should the teacher review, and what should students be told? If you have a view on which guardrails matter most, I'd be glad to hear it, and happy to share what our teachers are seeing.
