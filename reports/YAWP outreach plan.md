# YAWP! outreach plan: who to contact and how

Research summary for Brian and Kevin · November 2026

## The short version

- **The strategy is to work the network.** Brian talks to the voices in this space, shows up where they are, and collects opinions, advice and connections. He isn't pitching. Every relationship is something to build on, and the goal is appearances we don't have to pay for.
- **There are two kinds of network.** Part 1 is deep: center directors, newsletter writers and convenors whose readers are the deans, T&L directors and principals who choose tools. Part 2 is wide: ELA teachers, creators and podcasts that reach classroom teachers.
- **Start with the three anchors.** Justin Cerenzia (warm intro through Kevin), Ian Kelleher and Glenn Whitman at St. Andrew's CTTL, and Kim Marshall.
- **On the teacher side, start with writing specialists.** Their content lines up with YAWP!'s builders, and none showed ties to Brisk or MagicSchool. Big TikTok accounts are mostly comedy. Several big edtech trainers already work with competitors.
- **Talk to the skeptics.** Ask for feedback, not promotion. If YAWP! holds up with them, that counts for a lot.
- **A teacher partner opens doors.** These networks want examples led by practitioners. A teacher who uses YAWP! and will co-present or co-write unlocks the Marshall Memo, NCTE's leadership conference, SAIS and the regional cohorts.
- **Going viral is too big a mountain right now.** Get our own presence solid first.

> **Let Muse do the legwork.** Most audience numbers come from search results and people's own claims, and several people changed jobs in 2026. Muse can confirm titles, handles and contact routes, keep the list current, find new names, track who Brian has talked to, and draft first notes for Brian to edit and send in his own voice. That keeps his hours for the conversations themselves.

## The idea: work the network

The people in this report are the network we want to be part of. The point isn't to sell to them. Brian gets to know them, shows up where they are, and learns from them. Sign-ups and sales come later, from the trust and the connections.

Every relationship is something to build on. One conversation leads to an introduction, and an introduction leads to an invitation. The aim is guest spots, mentions and appearances that come through people we know, not ones we pay for.

**What Brian is doing**

- **Talking to voices in this space.** Short calls, thoughtful comments on their posts, personal emails.
- **Making appearances when he can.** Podcast guest spots, guest posts, free PD sessions, conference sessions, live demos for groups that want one.
- **Getting opinions.** What do they think of YAWP!? What's missing? What would make them nervous?
- **Getting advice.** How would they reach ELA teachers? What do teachers actually need help with right now?
- **Getting connections.** Who else should he be talking to?
- **Getting impressions.** How do teachers react the first time they see the product?

**His angle**

Brian is a builder who wants to get it right, not a vendor with a pitch. He's curious, he's open about how YAWP! uses AI, and he wants honest reactions, including criticism. Something like:

> "I'm building YAWP!, a writing tool for middle and high school ELA teachers. I've been following your work on [specific post or episode]. I'd love 20 minutes of your honest take on what we're building, especially [the part that relates to their work]. No pitch. I'm trying to get this right."

**What a good outcome looks like**

- A 20-minute call
- Honest feedback, even if it stings
- An intro to someone else on the list
- An invitation onto a show, a blog or a PD session
- A teacher who tries it with their class and tells us what happened

**How it fits together**

1. **Presence first** (below), so people who look us up find something real.
2. **Conversations** with the anchors and network leaders (Part 1), the writing teachers (Part 2, Group A) and the skeptics.
3. **Appearances** wherever we're invited: podcasts, guest posts, PD sessions, conferences.
4. **A teacher council** made up of the people who stick around.
5. **Paid spots** only as a last resort, if a door won't open any other way.

## First: firm up our own presence

Going viral is too big a mountain to climb right now. When a teacher or principal hears about YAWP! from someone on this list, the first thing they'll do is look us up. We need something solid for them to find.

- **LinkedIn.** Put the YAWP! logo on Brian's LinkedIn so people connect him with the product. Brian posts **twice a week**.
- **Facebook.** Set up a YAWP! Facebook page. Teachers spend time there, and we're planning to show up in Facebook teacher groups, so they need a page to land on.
- **Website.** Review the website, and update it if needed, so we feel good about it as the place people land. Check that it's clear in a few seconds what YAWP! is and who it's for, that signing up for the free tier is easy, and that it matches what we say in posts and outreach.
- **Do this before outreach starts**, so partners and their audiences find an active, real presence.

## How Brian gets in touch

**The basics**

- **Warm before cold.** Before reaching out, check who already knows them. Look at mutual LinkedIn connections, ask Kevin, ask the teachers already using YAWP!, and ask people like Justin. An intro beats a cold note every time.
- **Engage before asking.** Follow them for a week or two. Listen to an episode, read a few posts, leave a real comment or two. Then reach out, mentioning something specific.
- **Use the door they've opened.** Contact forms, "be a guest" pages, workshop pages, Substack replies, LinkedIn messages. Don't dig for personal emails.
- **Read before writing.** Subscribe to their newsletter or listen to their show, and mention a specific piece.
- **Bring a teacher.** Co-presenting with a teacher who uses YAWP! opens more doors than Brian alone.
- **Participant first.** At institutes and conferences, attend and listen before asking to present.
- **Keep it short.** One reason you're writing, and one small ask: a 20-minute call or "would you try it and tell me what you think?"
- **Follow up once.** If there's no answer after a week or two, send one friendly nudge, then let it go.
- **Always ask who else.** End every conversation with "Who else should I be talking to?" That's how the network grows.
- **Muse helps, Brian sends.** Muse finds each person's public contact route and drafts a first note. Brian makes it his own before sending.

**By type**

| Who | Best way in | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Center directors and leader newsletters | Warm intro from someone they know, or a reply to a specific post. Then LinkedIn | Ask for their read and advice. Later, ask about a slot in their group or event |
| Teacher-creators | Contact or workshop page on their website, Instagram DM, comments on their posts | They're teaching full time. Keep the ask small, and ask about district rules before offering anything |
| Podcast hosts | "Be a guest" page or contact form, a reply about a specific episode, or an intro from a past guest | Pitch an episode topic that's useful to their listeners, not the product |
| Substack writers and skeptics | A thoughtful comment on a post, or a reply to their newsletter email | Engage with their argument first. Ask for criticism, not a review |
| LinkedIn voices (admin audiences) | Comment on their posts for a couple of weeks, then send a connection request with a short note | LinkedIn is also where Brian's twice-a-week posts do the warming up |
| Organizations and school associations | Partnership or PD inbox, state NCTE affiliate offices, or an intro from a member | Offer a free PD session, not a demo |
| Facebook groups and Reddit | Join as a person and help with answers | Ask the admins before posting anything about YAWP! |

# Rules, plan and open questions

## Rules to know

- **Anything we give a creator must be disclosed**, free Premium included, even if we didn't ask them to post. Disclosure goes in the post or video itself, not just a platform label. We have to tell them how to disclose and check that they do.
- **No fake or AI-written reviews.** No reviews from staff, investors, friends or family unless they disclose. Fines run up to ~$51K per violation (2024 figure).
- **Teachers have district rules.** Many districts limit gifts from vendors, and some ban teachers from making money on social media. Keep perks small for current teachers, and ask about their district before offering cash.
- **No real student work on camera.** Build a demo class with made-up sample essays that creators can use instead.
- **"AP" is a College Board trademark.** Check before using it in materials.
- **Facebook groups and Reddit are for helping, not ads.**
