## Q2. CTTL at St. Andrew's Episcopal School (Potomac, MD): who runs it, what it runs, and its reach

### Takeaway
Yes, the user's "guy at St. Andrew's" is the CTTL. Glenn Whitman (Director, sometimes listed as Executive Director or Dreyfuss/Dreyfus Family Director; also a history teacher) and Dr. Ian Kelleher (Dreyfuss Family Head of Research; science teacher) lead it, and both were still active in 2026. ## Q3. What have they said about AI, writing, feedback and grading? Edtech ties?

### Takeaway
Cerenzia is a "(mostly) pragmatically optimistic" adopter. EA works directly with edtech startups (Flint for AI feedback on student writing, and Viva for AI oral assessment of student writing), and he appeared in a Flint webinar and gave Flint a testimonial. That makes him a natural fit for a startup pilot conversation. The CTTL is more cautious: Kelleher's August 2026 position paper "The Thinking Problem" argues the danger of AI is "not that students will cheat, it's that they won't have to think," and that schools should judge any AI or edtech tool by whether it supports or replaces student thinking.

Integrity, equity and ethics depend on an earlier question about how learning happens. AI substitution is "nearly undetectable inside a success narrative of strong grades and polished work." ### Inferences
- **For YAWP!:** EA already uses Flint for AI writing feedback, including in middle school English, and Viva for oral checks on student writing. YAWP! will be compared directly with Flint. The pitch should stress what is different: ELA-specific structured prewriting (thesis, CER, five-paragraph scaffolds), teacher-controlled grading assistance, and keeping students doing the thinking. It should not be framed as "AI feedback."
- **For the CTTL:** YAWP!'s scaffolded prewriting and exit tickets need to be framed in "Thinking Problem" terms: tools that require students to produce the thinking (retrieval, elaboration, planning a claim and evidence) rather than replacing it. A Grading Assistant pitch should stress teacher judgment and speed of feedback, not automated grading.
