# Networks, consortia, cohorts and convenings for K-12 academic leaders, and who runs them

Research date: 2026-11-27. Method note: the session could not open ncte.org, ncais.org or digitalpromise.org directly (those hosts are not on the session's network allowlist), so every finding below comes from web-search result snippets and summaries of the cited pages, not from full page reads. Names and titles should be checked on each organization's staff page before outreach. Individual staff email addresses that turned up in search results are deliberately left out. Only generic or role inboxes and official web forms are listed as contact routes.

## Independent schools: who runs the academic-leader and teaching-and-learning (T&L) groups at regional and national associations?

### Takeaway
The best way in to independent-school academic leaders is through a handful of named professional-learning staff at the regional associations: Ann Marsh Rutledge (SAIS), Dr. Darcie TeVault (VAIS), Becky Biggs (AISNE, title confirmed for 2025), and the NYSAIS associate directors. Several of these groups take outside proposals from non-members: SAIS accepts them openly, and NAIS Thrive does not require membership. These are the cheapest openings that need no sponsorship. Many associations (FCIS, PAIS, AIMS, AISGW, CSEE, CAIS-California, One Schoolhouse) have no indexed academic-leader program or organizer, so those need a direct phone call or a check of their staff pages.

### Inferences
- SAIS is the clearest no-cost speaking route: non-members can propose, it keeps proposals on file, and its T&L conference audience (academic deans and department chairs) matches YAWP!'s buyers. The February 2027 window has closed. The next one probably opens around May 2027, by analogy with the 2026 timing (an inference).
- Ann Marsh Rutledge (SAIS) and Darcie TeVault (VAIS) are the most clearly named T&L convenors. Both run design- and innovation-oriented programs where an AI writing case study would fit. A good first approach is an advice-seeking note through the association's contact form, offering a free 20-minute "what ELA teachers are actually doing with AI feedback" segment for a cohort session, not a pitch.
- The NCAIS volunteer page openly says "there is always room for a co-facilitator." Co-facilitating would need a school-based partner, so Brian would get furthest by co-presenting with an NCAIS-member teacher who uses YAWP!.
- NWAIS's "Leading in an AI World" series and NAIS's AI webinar series show AI is a live leadership theme. Neither was found to take vendor presenters.
- With ISEEN's executive director role changing hands in 2026, a new leader may be open to fresh conversations, but ISEEN's focus is experiential education, not writing, so it is a low priority.

## Public schools and districts: which networks gather chief academic officers, curriculum directors and ELA supervisors, who runs them, and do they take outside presenters?

### Takeaway
The district-side groups split two ways. Membership networks (Council of the Great City Schools, the Digital Promise League, CRPE's Early Adopters) are run by named staff but are closed to vendors except through sponsorship. Big conferences (AASA, CoSN, Learning Forward) have open calls, but CoSN bars vendor submissions, and most 2027 calls have already closed. The best open doors for an ELA tool are NCTE's Conference on English Leadership (CEL) and state or regional ELA leader networks (Oregon, Dallas-Fort Worth MetroCREST, Kansas).
