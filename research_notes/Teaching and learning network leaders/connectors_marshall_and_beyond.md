## Q1. Kim Marshall and the Marshall Memo: reach, readers, price, AI and writing coverage, article selection, other work, and a realistic path for YAWP!

### Takeaway
The Marshall Memo is one of the most trusted "deep, not viral" channels to principals, coaches and superintendents. Kim Marshall, a former Boston teacher and principal, has written it every week since 2003. A year costs $50, and 1,954 readers answered his June 2026 survey (the total subscriber count is not published). He does not feature products. He summarizes 5-10 articles a week from a fixed list of publications that includes Educational Leadership, English Journal, Edutopia and Independent School. AI in teaching, writing and supervision comes up often. The realistic path is for Brian or a YAWP! teacher to publish a practitioner article in one of those publications, and separately to engage Marshall on AI-assisted feedback, a topic he is actively surveying and writing about.

### Cited Findings
**Who he is and the product**
- Kim Marshall is a former Boston teacher and principal. He launched the weekly Marshall Memo in 2003 to summarize research and ideas for front-line educators. It is based in Boston and has readers across the US. — [Marshall Memo homepage](https://marshallmemo.com/); [Kim Marshall bio](https://marshallmemo.com/about.php)
- It is published 50 times a year and is "designed to keep principals, teachers, instructional coaches, superintendents, and other PreK-12 educators well-informed on current research and effective practices." — [Fordham Institute, Marshall commentary bio, 26 Mar 2026](https://fordhaminstitute.org/national/commentary/should-ai-be-used-teacher-evaluation)
- Schedule: year-round, with a one-week break at Christmas and two weeks in early August. Kim writes the summaries on Monday and sends the issue Tuesday morning. An older page says Monday evening, which conflicts. — [Subscribe page](https://marshallmemo.com/subscribe.php); [Marshall Memo issues via search](https://www.marshallmemo.com/issues/735ec2ee08fae56d8bf1abb28c0b4c44/MarshMemo1094.pdf)
- **Price:** $50 for a one-year individual subscription, with lower per-reader rates for multiple readers at the same school or agency. Payment is by card, check or purchase order. — [Subscribe page](https://marshallmemo.com/subscribe.php)

In a FAQ ranking by number of summaries through December 2020, Educational Leadership is #2 and Edutopia is in the top dozen. — [Memo 1053](https://www.marshallmemo.com/issues/e8b6406a84f4f268529a049b2fc29fe1/MarshMemo1053.pdf); [Memo 946](https://marshallmemo.com/issues/ea72eb43c702d400f99d9713cf39ec69/MarshMemo946.pdf); [Best of Marshall Memo FAQ](https://www.bestofmarshallmemo.org/faq/)

**His own views on AI**
- He used Claude to help analyze the 522 open-ended survey comments. — [Fordham, 26 Mar 2026](https://fordhaminstitute.org/national/commentary/should-ai-be-used-teacher-evaluation)
- He found that many supervisors use AI to draft or tidy classroom-visit notes, and a few feed rubrics such as Danielson's into AI to produce ratings. He concluded that AI use in evaluation is largely unregulated, with few guardrails and minimal training. Respondents wanted human judgment and transparency, for example teachers being told when AI is involved. — [Fordham, 26 Mar 2026](https://fordhaminstitute.org/national/commentary/should-ai-be-used-teacher-evaluation)
- In a 2025 interview he called a constrained, transparent post-observation AI summary tool a useful application, provided teachers know it is in use. — [Eduleadership interview, "Kim Marshall on Teacher Evaluation, Feedback Conversations, and Artificial Intelligence"](https://www.eduleadership.org/p/kim-marshall-on-teacher-evaluation)
- Fordham also ran a Gadfly issue on "The human stakes of AI grading". I did not see whether Marshall took part. — [Education Gadfly Weekly](https://fordhaminstitute.org/national/gadfly/education-gadfly-weekly-human-stakes-ai-grading)

**Public contact route:** the marshallmemo.com site (subscribe and feedback pages). — [Subscribe](https://marshallmemo.com/subscribe.php); [Feedback from the field](https://www.marshallmemo.com/feedback-from-the-field.php)

### Inferences
- **The path into the Memo is indirect but real.** Marshall picks articles from his fixed publication list and writes every summary himself. A pitch for YAWP! to be "featured" would almost certainly not work, and I saw no sign that he covers products. A practitioner article would work: Brian or, better, a YAWP! teacher could write in English Journal, Edutopia, Educational Leadership, Independent School or Kappan about, for example, "what happened when 8th graders used a structured thesis/CER builder and the teacher used AI-assisted feedback with human sign-off." Such a piece could be picked up. Kappan has an open submission route (see Q6).
- **The most natural direct conversation is AI-assisted feedback and evaluation.** He has surveyed readers on exactly this and wants guardrails, transparency and human judgment. YAWP!'s Grading Assistant is a close analogue for student writing. A short, non-salesy note could offer data or teacher experience on human-in-the-loop AI feedback, or answer a question he raised in the Fordham piece.
- **Suggested first approach:** subscribe ($50) and read for a month. Then send a brief note through the site responding to a specific Memo item on AI and writing. Offer to share what YAWP! teachers are seeing on human-reviewed AI feedback, and mention any article placed in a publication he reads. Do not ask for a feature.
- A Marshall interview with Justin Baeder (Eduleadership) and his co-author relationship with Jenn David-Lang suggest those two are warm adjacent routes (Q2).

### Gaps
- Total subscriber count is not published; only the 2026 survey response counts are known. The full June 2026 survey results sit behind linked files I could not open.
- I could not confirm whether he ever accepts article submissions, guest pieces or tool mentions. No source says either way.
- The full current publication list and its count (39 vs. 60 conflict) could not be verified because marshallmemo.com was unreachable by fetch.
- The co-author of the 3rd edition (if any) and its publisher were not confirmed.
