Research date: 2026-11-26. Method note: in this session WebFetch could not reach podcasts.apple.com or podcast.feedspot.com (both hosts blocked by the session's network allowlist), so I could not read Apple ratings counts, chart ranks, or Feedspot rankings directly. Every audience number below comes from a search-result snippet of a show's own site, a directory (Castbox, Podchaser, Libsyn), or a third-party estimator, with the source named. Where nothing was found the entry says "unverified". Apple ratings and recent-episode dates should be re-checked by hand before outreach. No personal emails are listed. Contact routes are only public forms or pages.

Each show profile covers: host, links, audience indicators, focus and audience, fit for YAWP!, AI stance, guests and founders, sponsorship and contact, 2026 activity, and a suggested first touch. The first-touch suggestions are my inferences and are labeled that way.

## Q1. Which ELA-specific podcasts are active, and what does each look like?

### Takeaway
Five ELA-specific or writing-heavy shows are clearly active in 2026: Brave New Teaching, The Spark Creativity Teacher Podcast, Teaching Middle School ELA, Cult of Pedagogy (general pedagogy, ELA-adjacent), and Catlin Tucker's The Balance (blended learning, with a strong writing and AI-feedback thread). Truth for Teachers is active but has broadened beyond the classroom. Talks with Teachers (Brian Sztabnik) shows no 2026 activity. "Your Ed Pod" could not be found under that name, so it should be treated as unverified.

### Inferences
- Best ELA guest targets for Brian, in order: (1) The Balance, which has already hosted two writing-feedback founders, so a founder interview is a proven format; (2) Brave New Teaching, whose arc from "threat" to "AI for good" suits a "why we lean in, and where we draw lines" conversation; (3) Spark Creativity, which is optimistic and creative-ELA, so pitch exit tickets and prewriting as creativity scaffolds rather than grading.
- Suggested first touch, The Balance: comment on or share the Sept 2026 Adam Sparks episode. Then pitch an episode on "what good AI feedback on student writing should and shouldn't do," positioned as complementary to the Class Companion conversation, not a repeat.
- Suggested first touch, Brave New Teaching and Spark Creativity: offer free-tier classroom access and invite the hosts to try the CER or thesis builder before any ask. Then pitch a practical episode built on teacher artifacts.
- Suggested first touch, Teaching Middle School ELA: the ChatGPT series shows the audience wants concrete AI workflows, so a "rubric plus feedback" walkthrough episode fits. It is unclear whether the host takes guests, so confirm first.
- Cult of Pedagogy is the largest teacher-audience show found, at about 200k unique downloads a month (self-reported). The realistic route is a paid sponsorship (inquiry form, fit-screened) rather than a founder guest spot. A tool-roundup mention (like Ep 266) is an earned-media possibility once the product has traction.
- Truth for Teachers: Watson's "stay human" positioning makes a sponsor read a poor fit right now. A thoughtful, respectful exchange (e.g., replying to her Substack) could still be valuable as a credibility test with skeptics.

## Q2. Which AI-in-education and edtech podcasts have real audiences?

### Takeaway
The biggest AI and edtech teacher-facing shows with verifiable signals are the 10 Minute Teacher (Vicki Davis: about 8M downloads and a media kit), the Easy EdTech Podcast (Monica Burns: an active sponsor program), My EdTech Life (Fonz Mendoza: frequent AI-critical guests), and EdSurge. Edtech Insiders is the most founder-centric show but reaches industry and investors, not teachers. Several suggested names are non-US (AI in Education Podcast, Australia; Shukes and Giff, which looks Canadian based on host handles; The Edtech Podcast, UK).
