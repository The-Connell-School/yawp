Research date: 2026-11-26. Method note: all findings come from web search results (titles/snippets and search-engine summaries). Direct page fetches failed for several primary sites in this session (fordhaminstitute.org, shakeuplearning.com, curmudgucation.substack.com, blog.heinemann.com returned DNS/network-blocked errors), so "work with me"/sponsor pages could not be read in full. Audience numbers are reported exactly as the source states them, with the source; where no number was found it is marked **unverified**. No personal emails are listed. Handles are only listed where a source surfaced them; otherwise marked "not verified here."

Legend for "Stance": Enthusiastic / Nuanced / Skeptical / Refusal.

## Key Question 1: Who are the major edtech tool reviewers/trainers for teachers (verify the named examples, find others)?

### Takeaway
Most of the named "edtech trainer" class is still active and has gone heavily into AI. Matt Miller (Ditch That Textbook + AI for Admins), Eric Curts, Kasey Bell, Monica Burns, Jake Miller, Catlin Tucker, Rachelle Dené Poth, and Holly Clark all publish AI-tool content for K-12 teachers. Several take sponsors (Miller, Bell, Burns, and the Cult of Pedagogy podcast). Brisk is the most visible AI-writing/feedback sponsor in this group. Tony Vincent now works as a district AI Coordinator, and Richard Byrne is still active, but I found no 2026 detail on him. I could not verify any AI-grading content from Alice Keeler.

### Inferences
- The trainer tier covers both teachers and admins. Matt Miller is the standout bridge to admins (AI for Admins) and is already commercially tied to Brisk, a direct YAWP! competitor in feedback. He is a high-reach but possibly conflicted target. A sponsorship slot (Ditch Summit / newsletter) is the realistic route, not an unpaid review.
- Monica Burns and Kasey Bell visibly run sponsored posts, podcast bonus series, and paid memberships. Both are plausible paid-placement partners, and both have K-12 ELA-adjacent audiences.
- Eric Curts publishes a public AI-tool evaluation rubric. A good first touch is to run YAWP! through his rubric yourself and share the result openly, inviting critique. That is credible and low-pressure.
- Tony Vincent's move to a district AI Coordinator role makes him a "practitioner-buyer" voice. Pitch him as a pilot-feedback contact rather than an influencer.
- Suggested first touches:
  - Matt Miller: sponsor inquiry via the DTT sponsorship contact, framed around writing-process (not end-product) tools, to match his stated view.
  - Burns/Bell: their sponsorship/work-with-us forms, offering a free-tier walkthrough for a "time-saving ELA tools" episode.
  - Catlin Tucker: a guest-post or podcast pitch (she accepts guest posts) on UDL-aligned prewriting scaffolds such as CER and thesis builders.
  - Jake Miller, Poth, Clark: a light first touch via LinkedIn/podcast guest suggestion.

## Key Question 2: Who are the influential voices specifically on AI and student writing, including skeptics? Is their audience K-12 or higher ed?

### Takeaway
The most K-12-relevant writing-specific voices are:
- **Practitioner/nuanced**: Nick Potkalitsky (7–12 ELA teacher), Kelly Gallagher, and Leon Furze.
- **Skeptical**: Peter Greene (HS English, anti-AI-grading), Tom Mullaney (K-12 critical), and Benjamin Riley (cognitive science).
- **Higher-ed but widely read by K-12 writing teachers**: John Warner and Marc Watkins.

Watkins and Greene have written directly against AI grading, which matters most for YAWP!'s Grading Assistant. Anna Mills and Ethan Mollick are mainly higher-ed. Dan Meyer is math-focused. Audrey Watters is an "AI refusal" voice and unlikely to engage with a vendor.
